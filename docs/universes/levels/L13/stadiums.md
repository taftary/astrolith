## How it looks

A stadium is a continuous tiered bowl around a rectangular pitch: one ring of seats, no columns
breaking the view, a roof over every head. From orbit the great roofs read as signatures — a
brilliant white ring between city wings, an arch drawn across a capital, a dome and its
rectangular neighbor parked side by side in asphalt. Modern roofs are lightweight membranes on
steel: Wembley's sliding section opens to feed the grass sun while the fans stay covered, and
its 133-m arch carries the whole north roof on forestay cables. Around the bowl runs the
apron — parking lots, vacant grass, transit stops — and on the Bay shore a stadium can sit
between the water and the freeway with the whole metro behind it.

## How it changes over time

Bowls are rebuilt more often than retired. Houston's Astrodome opened in 1965 as the world's
first domed, air-conditioned stadium — the Eighth Wonder, 70,000 seats with a chapel and a
bowling alley inside — and after forty years of sports, conventions, and concerts it hosts no
more majors while proposals for its second life pile up. Brasilia's 1974 bowl was demolished
and rebuilt from 2010 to 2013 for the World Cup, rising to 72,788 seats under a double-layer
suspended membrane roof at some R$1.4 bn. Rio's Maracana opened in 1950 for 200,000 standing
and shrank through the 2000, 2006, and 2013 rebuilds to 73,139 all-seater. London's old Empire
Stadium stood 1923–2000, came down in 2002–03, and the arched successor opened in 2007 and is
still being refitted — new pitch, new floodlights, new sound.

Sources: NASA Reliant and Brasilia pages; Britannica Maracana and Astrodome pages; Foster Wembley pages.

## How it is created

The sequence runs demolition and piling, concrete bowl, steel roof lift, fit-out and turf.
Wembley stands on some 4,000 piles down to 35 m; its 1,650-ton arch was lifted in weekend
stages over temporary steelwork to carry a 7,000-ton roof, breaking ground in 2002 and
opening in 2007 at £798M. Domes were the earlier moonshot: the Astrodome's lamella steel
truss spanned a record 642 ft under 2,150 tons of roof steel, built 1963–65 with its cooling
plant moving two million cubic feet of air a minute. Maracana went up fastest — cornerstone
January 1948, open June 1950, still unfinished with the canopy on scaffolding and no press
box. Roofs keep score: Wembley's 315-m arch is the longest single-span roof support standing,
Brasilia's membrane covers 55,155 m2.

Sources: Multiplex Wembley pages; Library of Congress Astrodome survey; sbp Brasilia pages.

## How it interacts with the others

A stadium is a transport machine one day a week: Wembley counts itself a public-transport
destination with three stations in walking range, coaches from fifty towns, a fan walk down
Olympic Way, road closures, gates two hours before kickoff, and three-quarters of the crowd
arriving without cars. World Cup hosts rebuild nearly every mode of transport, airports
first — the Brasilia portrait reads stadium-to-airport across the lake. Houston shows the
unplanned version: stadium, dome, and surface parking set down zoning-free among houses,
vacant lots, and forest. Maracana rides the train and the metro to its own station. When the
crowd leaves, the bowl goes quiet and the parking apron empties until next week.

Sources: Wembley visitor pages; NASA Brasilia pages; NASA Reliant caption pages.

## Typical size and mass

The pitch measures 105 by 68 m by FIFA guidance. Wembley seats 90,000 — largest in Britain,
second in Europe — under a 315-m arch 133 m high, enclosing four million cubic meters with
2,000 toilets inside. Maracana now seats 73,139, down from 200,000 standing, its paid-final
record 173,850. Michigan's Big House seats 107,601, the largest in the hemisphere, on
foundations poured in 1927 for a future 150,000. The Astrodome spans 710 ft across and 642 ft
clear, 62 m tall on 9 acres; Brasilia's rebuild cost roughly R$1.4 bn, second only to Wembley
in its day.

Sources: FIFA pitch pages; Wembley official pages; Britannica Maracana pages; Library of Congress Astrodome survey.

## Example objects

- Maracana, Rio de Janeiro — opened 1950 for 200,000, now 73,139, home of the 1950 and 2014 finals and the 2016 ceremonies, still Brazil's largest.
- Wembley, London — opened 2007 for 90,000 under the 315-m arch, England's home, cup finals, NFL London games, concerts.
- Michigan Stadium, Ann Arbor — opened 1927 for 107,601, the Big House, record crowd 115,109, largest stadium in the Americas.

Sources: Britannica Maracana pages; Wembley official pages; Michigan tour pages.
