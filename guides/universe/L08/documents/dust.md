# Dust — the glow between planets

This page is the L08 part doc for interplanetary dust: the
zodiacal cloud, dust rings, meteoroid streams, and the
Gegenschein at system scale. Mentions live in `sun.md:141-
142` (dust rings), L09 `companions.md:22` (zodiacal light),
L07 `comets.md:68` (showers), and L06 `ism.md:85-101`
(grains, Stardust). Epsilon Eridani's dust sits in L06
`systems.md:146-148`.

Look west after sunset in spring (east before dawn in
autumn) from dark skies: a faint pyramid of light leaning
along the ecliptic — sunlight scattered by dust orbiting
between the planets, each grain a mote spiraling sunward.
Opposite the Sun, a faint brightening marks the
Gegenschein — backscattered light from the same cloud.
The inner system glitters with ground rock; the sky's
darkest places are dusty, not empty.

## The zodiacal cloud

A lens-shaped haze from near the Sun past the belt,
thickest at the center plane: comet crumbs and asteroid
collisions ground to microns, spiraling inward under
radiation drag over thousands of years, vaporized or blown
out at the end. It outshines the Milky Way's integrated
starlight at the ecliptic — the dominant sky brightness
interplanetary probes must subtract. Warps and bands in it
map recent collisions; Earth's resonant ring herds dust
along our own orbit.

## Dust rings and the Gegenschein

Dust herds along planetary orbits — rings circling the Sun
in step with Venus and Earth, found by spacecraft
photometry (see `sun.md:141-142`). The Gegenschein glows
faintly anti-sunward where backscatter peaks; asteroidal
dust bands (Karin, Veritas families) stripe the cloud
from known breakups millions of years ago. Every ring is
a fossil collision still spiraling in.

## Meteoroid streams and showers

Comets shed; streams persist: Halley feeds the Eta
Aquariids and Orionids (see L07 `comets.md:68`), Swift-
Tuttle the Perseids, Tempel-Tuttle the Leonids — Earth
crossing each ribbon yearly on schedule. Most grains are
sand; the rare boulder survives as fireball and meteorite
(see `neos.md`). Parents identified, returns predicted,
storms when we cross fresh trails (Leonids 1833, 1966).
Daytime streams (Arietids) hide in the Sun's glare,
mapped by radar alone.

## Interstellar dust through the system

The system moves through the local cloud, and interstellar
grains stream through on hyperbolic paths — Stardust
caught a few and brought them home (see L06 `ism.md`).
Big ones, and recently interstellar visitors 1I/2I/3I
(see L07 `comets.md`), cross unbound; the heliosphere's
magnetic walls filter the smallest (see `heliosphere.md`).
Dust is the system's weather vane for the galactic wind.

## Epsilon Eridani's dust — the neighbor's cloud

Our neighbor K star wears the phenomenon large: inner
belts plus an outer Kuiper-like ring, and inner dust that
is rock, not ice (`systems.md:146-148`) — a warmer
zodiacal cloud around a younger Sun, with a Jupiter-like
planet (~7.3 AU) shepherding. What ALMA and JWST map there
informs what the zodiacal glow implies here.

## How it interacts with the others

The Sun lights it (`sun.md`): all glow is borrowed
sunlight; sungrazers feed dust at the end (L09
`companions.md`). Belts supply it (`belts.md`): collisions
and Yarkovsky grind the replenishment. Comets shed it (L07
`comets.md`): streams as orbital ribbons. The bubble
filters it (`heliosphere.md`): interstellar grains sorted
at the wall. The threat page counts its boulders
(`neos.md`); the disk page shows its youth (`disks.md`);
the lifetimes date its parents (`lifetime.md`).

Target visual: a dedicated dust frame (zodiacal pyramid
over an observatory, public-domain) is proposed; no new
raster file is added here.

## Key numbers

- Zodiacal pyramid: spring west, autumn east; brightest
extended feature at the ecliptic.
- Grains spiral sunward over kyr; bands map known
breakups (Myr old).
- Showers yearly on schedule; storms on fresh trails;
fireballs nightly somewhere.
- Interstellar grains hyperbolic; Stardust sampled.
- Epsilon Eridani: belts plus outer ring; inner dust rock.
- L08 frame: 10^12–10^13 m — one system with its glow
per view.

## Example objects

The zodiacal pyramid is the glow standard: borrowed
sunlight on ground rock — dark skies' false dawn.

The Gegenschein is the faint standard: anti-sunward
brightening — backscatter peak.

The Perseids are the calendar standard: Swift-Tuttle's
ribbon, yearly — comets as meteor showers.

Epsilon Eridani is the neighbor standard: belts and ring
imaged — our cloud writ large.
