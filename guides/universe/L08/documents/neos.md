# Near-Earth objects — the neighboring threat

This page is the L08 part doc for near-Earth asteroids and
comets: orbital classes, sources, impact record, and
planetary defense at system scale. Fragments appear in
`belts.md:60-67,98-101` (Bennu drift, Tunguska,
Chelyabinsk, Chicxulub); DART/Hera in `lifetime.md:117-
135`; meteor showers in `dust.md` and L07 `comets.md:68`;
the bombardment record in L10 (`impacts.md`).

Near-Earth space is a shooting gallery with doorbells:
telescopes find new house-sized rocks nightly, radar maps
the close ones, and two missions proved we can push back.
Most burn as fireballs; the rare big ones write history —
Tunguska flattened forest, Chelyabinsk broke glass across
a city, Chicxulub ended an era. The threat is also the
opportunity: the same rocks hold samples (Bennu's grains
now in labs) and the next spacecraft targets.

## The classes

Orbits relative to Earth's divide the population: Atiras
inside Earth's orbit entirely; Atens crossing with periods
under a year; Apollos crossing with periods over a year
(the majority, Apophis among them); Amors approaching but
not crossing. Sizes run meters to kilometers; survey
completeness runs opposite — kilometer-plus bodies mostly
found, 140-m city-killers about half-mapped, house-sized
arriving unannounced. Potentially Hazardous Asteroids:
~2,000 known, defined by size (140 m+) and closeness
(within 20 lunar distances).

Sources: NASA CNEOS pages; NASA Apophis pages.

## Sources and delivery

NEOs leak from the belt through Kirkwood gaps and
Yarkovsky drift (see `belts.md`), plus burned-out Jupiter-
family comets darkened past recognition. Resonances pump
eccentricities until Earth encounters finish the job —
objects live millions of years here before hitting sun,
planet, or ejection. Fresh craters on the Moon and Mars
(L10 `impacts.md`) are the same rain, counted.

## The record on the ground

Tunguska 1908: stony airburst leveling 2,000 square km of
Siberian forest, no crater. Chelyabinsk 2013: ~20 m rock,
400-kiloton burst, 1,500 injured mostly by glass. Meteor
Crater 50,000 years ago: 30–50 m iron, 1.2-km bowl (see
L12 `craters.md`). Chicxulub 66 Ma: 10 km, 180-km ring,
three quarters of species (see L10 `impacts.md`). Energy
scales with the cube of size and square of speed — the
city-killer line sits near 140 m, the civilization line
near a kilometer, and completeness follows the same order.

## Apophis 2029 and the defense ladder

April 13, 2029: the 340-m Apophis passes ~31,000 km above
the surface — inside geostationary orbit, naked-eye over
Europe, Africa, and Asia — no risk for at least a century,
but tides may shake avalanches on its surface. OSIRIS-APEX
arrives after; ESA's RAMSES will ride along. The defense
ladder demonstrated so far: find (surveys nightly), track
(radar to meter detail), deflect — DART moved Dimorphos in
2022, confirmed kinetic impact works; Hera now surveys the
result. Next rungs: faster response, bigger targets,
gravity tractors and ion shepherds on paper.

Sources: NASA Apophis pages (2026); NASA JPL 2029
planning; NASA DART pages.

## Key numbers

- Classes: Atira/Aten/Apollo/Amor by orbit; PHAs ~2,000
known (140 m+, within 20 lunar distances).
- Apophis: 340 m; April 13, 2029; ~31,000 km; naked-eye;
no risk 100+ years; OSIRIS-APEX + RAMSES.
- DART 2022: Dimorphos moved; Hera surveying.
- Record: Tunguska 1908 (2,000 km2 flat); Chelyabinsk 2013
(~20 m, 400 kt); Meteor Crater (1.2 km); Chicxulub
(180 km, 66 Ma).
- L08 frame: 10^12–10^13 m — one system with its
crossers per view.

Sources: NASA pages named above; in-repo `belts.md`.

## Example objects

Apophis is the visitor standard: 340 m at 31,000 km —
science and defense rehearsal in one pass.

Didymos/Dimorphos is the pushed standard: DART moved it —
deflection demonstrated.

Bennu is the sampled standard: drift measured, grains
home — Yarkovsky caught in the act.

Chelyabinsk is the warning standard: 20 m unannounced —
glass across a city.

Sources: NASA pages named above.
