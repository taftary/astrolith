# Disks — the young form of planetary systems

This page is the L08 part doc for planet-forming disks:
protoplanetary, transitional, and debris disks at 10–1,000
AU — the scale where the young system fills the view with
rings, gaps, and dust. Mentions live in L06
(`systems.md:28-51,69-71`, `lifetime.md:49-53`), L05
(`open.md:175`, `clouds.md:122-124,249-253`), and L07
(`lifetime.md:11`); the Sun's own formation in `sun.md`
and `lifetime.md`; Epsilon Eridani's belts in L06
`systems.md:146-148`.

A planetary system spends its first millions of years as a
disk: gas and dust orbiting a young star, grains sticking
to pebbles to boulders to worlds, gaps opening where
planets sweep clean. ALMA photographs this directly now —
rings and gaps in systems younger than the time their
planets supposedly needed. The disk is the system before
planets; debris disks are the system after, ground-down
leftovers glowing in infrared.

## Protoplanetary disks — planets under construction

HL Tauri, 450 light-years out and barely a million years
old, wears concentric bright rings split by dark gaps —
planets the likely sweepers, impossibly early by old
clocks. VLA saw deeper: a 3–8 Earth-mass dust clump in the
inner disk, grains grown to centimeters where Earth-like
worlds would form — the earliest protoplanet stage ever
seen. TW Hydrae, nearest at ~200 light-years, gaps at
Earth's own orbital radius; PDS 70 shows a circumplanetary
disk — a moon system forming inside a planet gap. DSHARP
found rings, gaps, and spirals in most disks surveyed:
substructure is the rule, and grain growth, planet
carving, and gas physics share the credit.

Gas clears in 3–10 Myr, accretion ends ~2.3 Myr (see L06
`systems.md`): the window for gas giants is short, for
rocky worlds longer. What remains becomes debris — or us.

Sources: NASA HL Tau APOD (2014); NRAO HL Tau VLA (2017);
ALMA planet-formation pages.

## Transitional and debris disks — the leftovers

Transitional disks wear cleared inner holes — planets
already grown, photoevaporation finishing the gas.
Debris disks are second-generation dust: asteroids and
comets colliding and grinding down, lit by starlight.
Epsilon Eridani keeps inner belts and an outer Kuiper-like
ring around its K star (see L06 `systems.md`) — rock, not
ice, in the inner dust (`systems.md:146-148`). Vega,
Fomalhaut, Beta Pictoris (with imaged planets in its
warp): each a planetary system's rubble signature, the
Kuiper Belt writ large. Our zodiacal cloud is the same
phenomenon at home scale (see `dust.md`).

## Proplyds — disks under fire

In Orion, massive stars photoevaporate neighbor disks into
cometary proplyds — d203-506 and hundreds more, disks with
bright rims facing the Trapezium, evaporating faster than
planets can grow. Environment decides: clustered birth
shortens the planet window; isolated birth (like the Sun's
likely nursery edge) keeps it open. L05 `clouds.md` holds
the nurseries; this page holds the disks.

## How it interacts with the others

The Sun remembers (`sun.md`, `lifetime.md`): solar nebula
collapse, CAIs and chondrules as first solids (L06/L07
`lifetime.md`), migration sculpting orbits. Terrestrials
condensed inside the snow line, giants outside
(`terrestrials.md`, `giants.md`). Belts are failed or
leftover disks (`belts.md`): Kirkwood gaps as fossil
resonances, Yarkovsky drift still delivering. The
heliosphere's edge tastes interstellar dust, not disk dust
(`heliosphere.md`). Comets fall from the Oort's deep
freeze (L07 `comets.md`, `oort.md`); near-Earth fragments
threaten (`neos.md`). Exoplanet systems show every outcome
(L06 `systems.md`).

## Key numbers

- HL Tau: 450 ly, ~1 Myr, gaps already; inner clump 3–8
Earth masses, cm grains.
- TW Hya: ~200 ly, gap at Earth's radius; PDS 70:
circumplanetary disk imaged.
- Gas gone 3–10 Myr; accretion ends ~2.3 Myr.
- L08 frame: 10–1,000 AU — one disk with rings per view.

Sources: NASA, NRAO, and ALMA pages named above; L06
`systems.md`.

## Example objects

HL Tau is the construction standard: rings at 1 Myr —
planets impossibly early.

TW Hya is the near standard: 200 ly, Earth-radius gap —
the closest site.

PDS 70 is the moon standard: disk inside a gap — satellites
forming.

Epsilon Eridani is the debris standard: belts plus outer
ring — rubble signature.

Orion proplyds are the endangered standard: disks
evaporating — environment deciding.

Sources: pages named above.
