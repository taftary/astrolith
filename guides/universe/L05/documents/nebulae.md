# Nebulae — the diffuse kinds

This page is the L05 taxonomy doc for diffuse nebulae at
10–100 light-years: emission, reflection, and dark kinds —
what each looks like, its lifetime, and how it relates to
the clouds. Star-forming cloud detail lives in
`clouds.md` (canonical for H II glow); Pleiades dust in
`open.md:40-44` and `previous-l4.md:60-63`; supernova
remnants in `supernovae.md`; planetary nebulae in L06
(`evolved.md`); the Crab image in `lifetime.md:142-148`.

From 30 light-years out, nebulae sort by light source, not
by stuff — the same dust glows, mirrors, or silhouettes
depending on who lights it. Learn the three readings and
every glow in the galaxy parses: lit from inside, lit from
outside, or dark against light behind.

## Emission nebulae — lit from inside

Hot young stars ionize the hydrogen around them; the gas
glows red-green in recombination light. The Orion Nebula's
core, Carina's walls, the Eagle's pillars with their EGGs
— all canonical in `clouds.md:24-39`, which holds the
physics, Larson rules, and PHANGS/JWST results. Lifetimes:
millions of years, until winds and radiation disperse the
cloud or the stars die. At L05 an emission nebula is one
glowing complex among spiral-arm ridges — the work in
progress (see L06 `previous-l5.md:93`).

## Reflection nebulae — lit from outside

Dust that borrows light: blue veils around hot stars too
cool to ionize, scattering starlight like daytime sky.
The Pleiades' Maia veils are the type (`open.md:40-44`) —
dust the cluster is passing through, not its birth cloud.
Lifetimes: as long as the encounter, thousands to millions
of years. Color rule: blue by scattering, never red by
glow — the diagnostic that separates them from emission in
one glance.

## Dark nebulae — light blocked

Dust silhouetted against glow behind: Bok globules (single
collapsing knots in `clouds.md:48-50`), the Horsehead and
Keyhole named in `clouds.md:168,180`, the great rift
clouds splitting the Milky Way's band. Dark means dense —
these are future stars mid-assembly, infrared cocoons
where the next emission nebulae are being built. Lifetimes:
hundreds of thousands of years to collapse or dispersal.

## How they relate

One cloud lives all three lives: dark knot to collapsing
core to lit H II region to dispersed veil to reflecting
passerby — the sequence in `lifetime.md:39-53` and L06
`systems.md`. Mass decides: massive stars light emission,
passing stars light reflection, quiet dust stays dark.
Supernovae interrupt (see `supernovae.md`): blast waves
compress dark clouds into collapse and shred veils into
streamers. Planetary nebulae replay emission at small
scale (see L06 `evolved.md`).

Target visual: a dedicated trio frame (Orion/Pleiades/
Horsehead, NASA/ESA — link, not copy) is proposed; the
page shares the Eagle Pillars anchor in `clouds.md`. No
new raster file is added here.

## Key numbers

- Emission: Myr lifetimes; pillars, globules, EGGs inside.
- Reflection: blue by scattering; Pleiades veils the type.
- Dark: Bok globules to rift clouds; collapse in 100s kyr.
- One cloud, three lives in sequence.
- L05 frame: 10^17–10^19 m — one glowing complex per view.
