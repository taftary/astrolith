# Entry — diving from L4 into Galactic structures

This page walks the L4-to-L5 dive in plain steps: the approach, the
effects and visuals on entry, and how long it takes. The bridge from L4
lives in [previous-l4.md](./previous-l4.md); the part docs from the
loop's first pass — [arms.md](./arms.md), [clouds.md](./clouds.md),
[open.md](./open.md), [globulars.md](./globulars.md),
[disk.md](./disk.md) — hold the detail, and the dated timeline runs
through [lifetime.md](./lifetime.md).

## The approach, in plain steps

1. **Pick the home portal.** Start in the L4 cell holding the Milky Way.
   Thirty-two markers share a poor-group cell like ours (up to 2,000 in a
   rich one like Virgo); only portals open, populations stay points
   (ADR 0010). Target the Orion Spur portal — ours: the Sun's lane some
   ~26,000 light-years (~8 kpc) from the galactic center, on the spur's
   inner edge between Sagittarius and Perseus.
2. **Watch the preview.** Above 0.02 rad the marker draws its interior
   inside itself at the exact positions the open cell will show, at true
   size (`marker + child x ratio`, ladder R7): first a brighter patch of
   the disk, then dust lanes and glowing knots resolving inside it —
   arms, clouds, clusters — growing as the dot fades from full
   brightness toward a faint floor (0.15) at the open angle while its
   children brighten from 0 to 1, both curves continuous, so the moment
   of opening is a pure origin shift with no pop. At most 6 markers
   (largest on screen) preview at once. Only portal markers preview:
   field populations are displayed and counted but never open and never
   preview.
3. **Fall through the milestones.** The L4–L5 span is crossed through two
   invisible magnification milestones, because its 2.49-decade span
   (ladder anchors e 20.98 down to e 18.49) exceeds the 1.5-decade
   single-crossing limit. Each is a silent exact no-op on the path (zero
   position, unit ratio): generation, snapshots, labels and previews never
   see them. The dive pushes them by proximity to the targeted portal and
   unwinds them the same way. You notice them only as continued smooth
   motion across two and a half decades of scale (ladder gap note).
4. **Open at 0.14 rad.** Past the open angle (about a third of the view)
   the marker's dot eases to zero as the camera passes inside; the open
   cell closes back into its marker below 0.10 rad, re-targeted. You are
   now in L5, inside the Orion Spur cell. The parent's siblings hang
   1/ratio cells away (about 309 L5-cell widths for the 3.24e-3 ratio);
   nothing shallower is drawn.

## Effects and visuals on entry

**What appears.** The coin becomes countryside: the smooth tilted disk
resolves into winding arms edged with dark dust, star-forming clouds
glowing pink and gold inside them, and clusters scattered through — the
Eagle Nebula's pillars ~7,000 light-years off in Serpens Cauda in the
Sagittarius Arm with young NGC 6611 (1–2 million years old, ~8,100 stars)
burning above them, the Pleiades' blue-white grouping ~444 light-years off
in Taurus wrapped in dust-blue haze (a passing interstellar wisp the
cluster is moving through, not leftover birth material), the Orion cloud
complex closer still at 1,000–1,400 light-years with stellar ages up to
12 million years (see [clouds.md](./clouds.md),
[open.md](./open.md)). What L4 showed as "spiral structure" is now terrain
you can point at.

**What fades.** The galaxy context falls away: Andromeda's tilted disk 2.5
million light-years off, Triangulum, the Magellanic Clouds, the swarm of
fellow galaxies — all shrink back into the L4 marker you came through. The
whole-galaxy story (mergers, types, nuclei) stops being weather and becomes
somebody else's story; here the story is the building site itself.

**What the markers do.** The L4 marker you entered closes behind you into an
ordinary portal, re-targeted, sitting 1/ratio cells away among its siblings.
Ahead, the L5 cell offers its own markers: 32 cloud populations plus
largest-cloud portals (ladder R6) — the next dive down to the stellar
neighborhood. What waits inside is now written up part by part: the arms
you fall through ([arms.md](./arms.md)), the clouds lighting up below
([clouds.md](./clouds.md)), the young clusters scattered around
([open.md](./open.md)), the ancient ones overhead
([globulars.md](./globulars.md)), all set in the disk frame
([disk.md](./disk.md)).

## Survey context

The countryside you dive into is mapped, not decoration. Gaia's
billion-star velocity maps (DR3) trace the disk's rotation directly —
young blue B-type stars drawing a crisp wavy run of proper motion with
longitude while old G-type stars smear it — and Gaia open clusters
still outline the arms, favoring long-lived structure. Beyond the Milky
Way, ALMA's PHANGS survey maps cold carbon-monoxide gas at ~100-parsec
resolution in ~90 nearby star-forming galaxies (~100,000 giant clouds),
showing the same cloud-arm patterns run everywhere. None of this changes
the entry mechanics above; it is why the preview's arm-and-cloud
geography can be trusted as real structure. Sources: ESA Gaia DR3
stellar-motions story
(`https://www.cosmos.esa.int/web/gaia/dr3-where-do-the-stars-go-or-come-from`);
ALMA PHANGS census; L05 [arms.md](./arms.md) (Gaia arm fits) and
[clouds.md](./clouds.md) (PHANGS census).

## How long it takes

The seeded autopilot runs L1 to L10 in about 40 seconds, through the
Local Group with Virgo as the rich sibling (ladder R8); travel time per
rung scales with its decade span (`Δe · ln 10 / k`), so the L4–L5 leg
(2.49 decades, galaxy scale at e 20.98 down to cloud scale at e 18.49)
is one of the shorter crossings — the longest is the four-decade
heliopause-to-Sun leg. That compression is honest: rung gaps on screen
stay proportional to the real decade gaps. Sources: ladder R6
(navigation, travel time); R5 (anchors).

## What entry proves

Entry is complete when the coin is gone, the arms wind past the view
edges, and the next markers are clouds, not galaxies: you count
star-forming complexes, not island universes. If you can name the spur
(Orion, ours), the far lantern (Eagle, ~7,000 light-years out in the
Sagittarius Arm), the near blue grouping (Pleiades, ~444 light-years in
Taurus), and the closest nursery (Orion, 1,000–1,400 light-years out) —
you are in L5. The detailed looks, lifetimes and interactions of each
kind live in the part docs: [arms.md](./arms.md) for the lanes,
[clouds.md](./clouds.md) for the nurseries, [open.md](./open.md) for the
young families, [globulars.md](./globulars.md) for the ancient swarms,
[disk.md](./disk.md) for the frame that holds them — with the dated story
in [lifetime.md](./lifetime.md).

## Key numbers

- Preview starts: marker angular radius above 0.02 rad (at most 6 markers,
  largest on screen); dot fades full to 0.15 floor, children 0 to 1; past
  open angle dot eases to zero.
- Open: 0.14 rad; close back below 0.10 rad.
- L4 to L5 ratio: 3.24e-3; 32 markers per L4 cell (2,000 rich); siblings
  ~309 L5-cell widths away.
- Span crossed: 2.49 decades (e 20.98 to e 18.49); L5 span 10^17–10^19 m.
- Invisible milestones L4–L5: two, silent exact no-ops.
- Autopilot L1 to L10: about 40 s total, through the Local Group with Virgo
  as the rich sibling.
- Eagle Nebula: ~7,000 ly away (ESO; SIMBAD-based estimates run ~5,700 ly),
  pillars lit by NGC 6611; Pleiades: ~444 ly, 1,000+ stars, ~100–125 Myr.
