# Supernovae — the blasts and their remains

This page is the L05 part doc for supernova remnants at
10–100 light-years: the Crab, Cassiopeia A, Vela, and the
Cygnus Loop — the scale where one remnant fills the view
with filaments and shock. The Crab paragraph in
`lifetime.md:142-148` moves here; triggering mentions in
`clouds.md:153-155`; Geminga in L06 (`ism.md:153`); pulsar
detail in L06 (`remnants.md`); elements forged in the
[elements catalog](../../catalogs/documents/elements.md).

A supernova outshines its galaxy for weeks; its remnant
glows for a hundred thousand years. From 30 light-years
out, remnants read as lace: filament shells in optical
red, hot interiors in X-ray blue, a pulsar wind nebula
glaring at the center of the young ones. Each is a forge
and a trigger — making heavy elements and compressing the
next clouds into stars. Elements forged live in the
[elements catalog](../../catalogs/documents/elements.md).

## The gallery

**Crab (1054)** — the witnessed one: Chinese court
astronomers' guest star, now ~11 light-years of filaments
6,500 light-years out, with a 33-millisecond pulsar
spinning 30 times a second at its heart, equator at
millions of miles per hour. Still argued over — filaments
too fast, mass too low for a standard blast; 2025 models
favor a pulsar-driven explosion. The L05 anchor remnant.

**Cassiopeia A** — the young one: a few centuries old,
unseen at birth behind dust, now the brightest radio
source past the Sun — silicon, sulfur, and iron knots
from the star's own layers, mapped element by element.

**Vela** — the near old one: ~11,000 years, ~800–900
light-years, degrees across the southern sky, its pulsar
glitching as its crust cracks — middle age on display.

**Cygnus Loop (Veil)** — the delicate one: tens of
thousands of years, shock fronts resolved into lace,
oxygen and sulfur filaments where the blast meets a
cavity wall — the radiative phase made beautiful.

Sources: NASA Crab pages (2023–2025); NASA SVS Crab
multi-wavelength; Chandra Crab handout; MNRAS SN 1054
pulsar-driven model (2025).

## The three phases

Free expansion (centuries): ejecta undecelerated, Cas A
now. Sedov-Taylor (millennia): swept mass dominates,
adiabatic glow, Vela now. Radiative (tens of kyr):
cooling filaments, momentum conserved, Cygnus now. Then
merger into superbubbles and the ISM (see `disk.md:76-80,
138-153`) — remnants fade into the fountains and corona,
their elements (oxygen to uranium) becoming clouds,
planets, people (see catalog `elements.md`).

## Triggers and terminations

Blast waves compress dark clouds into collapse — star
formation triggered at bubble rims (see `nebulae.md`,
`clouds.md`) — and shred reflection veils, ending quiet
births nearby. Iron-60 in Earth's ocean crusts (see L06
`ism.md`) is a nearby supernova's receipt from a few
million years ago. Geminga's quiet pulsar next door (L06)
is an old remnant's heartbeat.

## Key numbers

- Crab: 1054 AD, ~11 ly, 6,500 ly out, 33-ms pulsar.
- Cas A: centuries old, brightest radio past the Sun.
- Vela: ~11 kyr, ~800 ly, degrees wide.
- Cygnus Loop: tens of kyr, radiative lace.
- Phases: free (centuries), Sedov (millennia), radiative
(tens of kyr), then superbubbles.
- L05 frame: 10^17–10^19 m — one remnant per view.

Sources: NASA pages named above.
