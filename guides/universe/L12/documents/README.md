# L12 — Cities and landscapes

L12 = L12 (ladder row L12, Cities and landscapes, 10^3–10^5 m). This
folder is the L12 visual and lifetime reference (Issue #229): what
its parts look like, how they were created, how they changed through
time, and how they interact — each page with target-visual images
and named sources. The bridge from the previous level and the entry
transition come first; the dated timeline runs through everything.
L12 continues the beyond-MVP tail: many cities per region, then buildings below.
Start at the bridge, dive through entry, pick any part, then confirm
dates in the lifetime — and continue down to
[L13](../../L13/documents/README.md) for buildings per city.

## Scope and scale

L12 covers cities and landscapes of one planet: 1–100 km patches in the
10^3–10^5 m range around whole metros, lake and valley widths, farm
section grids, and street blocks — about 0.008 of the 12,756 km planet
width at 100 km, about 0.00008 at 1 km. It sits between the
[L11 — Regions of a planet](../../L11/documents/README.md) above and the
[L13 — Buildings](../../L13/documents/README.md) below,
under the [Universe](../../documents/README.md#levels) ladder.
Frozen scale rules live in `docs/universes/ladder.md`; this folder
holds the descriptive reference only. The worked example is Earth
cities and countryside; other worlds appear only as comparative notes
in part docs.

Read order: bridge ([previous-l11.md](./previous-l11.md)) first, then
the entry transition ([entry.md](./entry.md)), then any part below,
then the dated spine ([lifetime.md](./lifetime.md)) for dates behind
each claim.

sets the spread, lines set the links, patchwork sets the managed

## Figures at a glance

Canonical detail lives in the linked pages; this index carries only
the navigation-level numbers: L12 span 10^3–10^5 m (cities 1–100 km
across); metros 20–100 km; street blocks 0.1–1 km; farm sections
1.6 km with quarter cuts at 800 m and pivot circles about 400 m
radius; New York urban area 8,413 km2 at about 100 km across
(19,426,449 people) and Los Angeles 4,239 km2 at about 73 km across
(12,237,376 people, densest large US area); watch at Sentinel-2 10 m
(290 km swath, 5-day revisit), Landsat 30 m under the 16-class
legend, and VIIRS Black Marble night maps to 500 m (2014–2022 global
radiance up 34 percent with bidirectional change); urban share about
6 in 10 by 2030 toward 70 percent by 2050 with over 12,000 cities of
50,000-plus and 33 megacities in 2025; L11-to-L12 leg is short (one
to two decades, quiet magnification; open 0.14 rad, close 0.10 rad,
preview from 0.02 rad) —
see the [L11 bridge](./previous-l11.md#key-numbers), the [entry
numbers](./entry.md#key-numbers), and the dated
[timeline](./lifetime.md#cross-check-of-aligned-timescales).

## How this level differs from L11

[L11](../../L11/documents/README.md) frames one region — a 100 to
1,000 km patch with its base, lift, flat, blue, and flow, where a
city is still a dot and the farm section is far below the zoom. Here
one patch of that region fills the view: the counted regional order
falls back into surroundings (ranges as valley walls and green
ridges, waters as shorelines and channels, plains as section grids)
while cities, towns, networks, farmland, city-scale landscapes,
volcanoes, craters, glaciers, coasts, forests, deserts, and skies
take center as the mass, the spread, the links, the patchwork, the
anchors, the cones, the rings, the ice, the shore, the green, the
tan, and the weather — so new city, suburb, road, field, lake, cone,
crater, glacier, shore, wood, dune, and storm results
enter through the part docs, not this index.
