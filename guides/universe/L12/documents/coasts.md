# Coasts — where land meets sea at walking scale

This page is the L12 part doc for coasts at 1–100 km:
beaches, cliffs, barrier islands, tidal flats, deltas, and
wetlands — the scale where one shoreline with its towns
fills the view. The regional seas live in L11 (`oceans.md`);
rivers and their mouths in L11 (`rivers.md`); tsunami run-up
in L11 (`earthquakes.md`); ports and seawalls in
`ports-airports.md`; the dated sea-level swings in
`lifetime.md:151-154`.

From 10 km up, coasts are the planet's handwriting: white
surf lines on sand, green marsh mazes, brown sediment
plumes fanning from river mouths, barrier islands strung
like beads, cliff shadows at sunset. No two kilometers
alike — and none permanent. Every coast in this page is
moving, and the towns on it are negotiating.

## Beaches and cliffs

Sand is rock in transit: rivers deliver, waves sort, wind
dunes, storms steal. A beach is a river of sand flowing
alongshore — groin it in one town and the next town's beach
starves. Dunes back the beach where wind wins, anchored by
grass, breached by blowouts in storms. Where rock resists,
cliffs stand: chalk, basalt, granite walls undercut at the
base into caves, arches, and stacks — each stack a former
headland, each stump a former stack, collapse in sequence.
Cliff-top towns buy views with retreat measured in
centimeters a year and meters a storm.

## Barrier islands and tidal flats

Barriers are beaches unmoored: long thin islands paralleling
the coast — Outer Banks, Frisian Islands — migrating
landward as seas rise, breached by inlets in storms,
rebuilt by longshore drift after. Towns on barriers live on
borrowed sand, renourished by dredge at million-dollar
intervals. Behind them lie the nurseries: tidal flats and
salt marshes flooded twice daily, cordgrass trapping mud,
oyster reefs breaking waves — the Wadden Sea the largest
unbroken system, shorebirds refueling by the millions. Marsh
drowns or migrates with sea level; seawall it and it
drowns instead — the coastal squeeze.

## Deltas — rivers building land

Where rivers meet the standing sea they drop their load:
bird-foot deltas fingering into gulfs (Mississippi),
fan deltas spreading wide (Nile), braided plains behind
levees (Ganges-Brahmaputra). Deltas grow lobe by lobe then
abandon them — the bird foot is one lobe among dead sisters
(see L11 `rivers.md`). Dams starve them of mud while seas
rise: the Mississippi loses a football field an hour in bad
years; the Colorado Delta — 80% of its wetlands gone
(`landscapes.md:115-118`) — is a delta kept alive by
negotiated pulse floods. Delta cities (New Orleans,
Dhaka, Alexandria) sink as they pump groundwater and the
sea climbs — subsidence plus rise, the double drowning.

## Wetlands — the drowned margin

Marshes, mangroves, and swamps: a quarter of marine fish
nurseries, storm surge sponges, carbon vaults out of all
proportion to area. Mangroves walk into the sea on stilt
roots where frost never comes; reed marshes filter
farmland runoff; peat bogs keep millennia. Four-fifths of
some systems are already gone to dikes, ponds, and ports —
and restoration now replumbs deltas on purpose, cutting
levee notches to rebuild marsh grain by grain.

## Retreat — the moving line

Seas rise with melt (see L11 `ice.md`) and shores answer:
beaches narrow, cliffs quicken, barriers roll over
themselves landward, marshes climb where they can. Towns
choose armoring, nourishment, elevation, or retreat —
seawalls for harbors, sand for resorts, stilts for villages,
buyouts for the doomed. The far-future fate in
`lifetime.md:151-154` is this negotiation extended: higher
seas, warmer storms, coasts redrawn within the mortgage
span, not the geologic one.

## How it interacts with the others

Cities crowd the edge (`cities.md`, `towns.md`): ports,
resorts, and fishing towns with one eye on the tide gauge.
Roads thread the shore (`networks.md`): coast highways,
causeways, bridges hopping barriers — and the first
infrastructure abandoned to the sea. Farms drain the margin
(`farmland.md`): polders, dikes, drained marsh becoming
fields below sea level. Forests fringe (`forests-deserts.md`):
mangroves, dune woods, maritime scrub. Lakes echo
(`landscapes.md`): bays and spits in miniature. Rivers feed
(`landscapes.md`, L11 `rivers.md`): mud, floods, and the
delta lobes. Glaciers carved the fjord coasts (`glaciers.md`);
volcanoes build black-sand ones (`volcanoes.md`); quakes
drop or lift whole shores overnight (L11 `earthquakes.md`:
1964 Alaska). Storms rewrite nightly (`sky.md`, L11
`weather.md`): surge, overwash, breached inlets. Ports
harden the working shore (`ports-airports.md`).

## Key numbers

- Beaches: sand in transit; groins starve downdrift;
dunes anchored by grass.
- Barriers migrate landward with rise; nourishment by the
dredge-load, inlets by the storm.
- Marshes: nurseries, sponges, vaults; squeeze between
rise and wall.
- Deltas grow lobe by lobe; dams starve, seas rise;
Colorado Delta −80% wetlands; double drowning in sinking
cities.
- Retreat menu: armor, nourish, elevate, retreat — chosen
per kilometer.

## Example objects

The Outer Banks are the barrier standard: beads migrating,
inlets breaching, sand by the dredge.

The Mississippi is the delta standard: bird foot alive,
sisters dead, mud starved by dams.

The Wadden Sea is the flat standard: tides twice daily,
birds by the millions, marsh climbing.

The Colorado Delta is the negotiated standard: −80% gone,
pulse floods buying back marsh.

A chalk cliff is the retreating standard: caves to arches
to stacks — collapse in sequence.
