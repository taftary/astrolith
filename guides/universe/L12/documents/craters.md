# Craters — the rings that fell from the sky

This page is the L12 part doc for impact craters at 1–100
km — the scale where one ring fills the view with rim,
floor, and ejecta readable. The great basins live in L10
(`impacts.md`); the 10–300 km structures will live in the
planned L11 craters page; ~100 m pits belong at L13.

From 10 km up, an impact ring is geometry no volcano
makes: a raised rim without a cone, an ejecta blanket
thinning outward in rays, a floor flatter than the land
around it — and often a town inside, farming the fractured
rock. Volcanoes build up; impacts dig down and throw out.
Learn the difference here and every circle on every world
reads correctly.

## Meteor Crater — the textbook bowl

Between Flagstaff and Winslow on the Colorado Plateau sits
the freshest simple crater on Earth: 1.2 km across, 180 m
deep, rim 45 m above the plain, dug 50,000 years ago by a
30–50 m iron-nickel rock at 12–20 km/s. Arid air preserved
it so well that Shoemaker trained Apollo astronauts on its
rim in the 1960s and proved impacts there first — the
diagnostic tools (shocked quartz, overturned flap, fallback
breccia) were learned here and applied to the Moon. A
similar strike today could destroy a city the size of
Kansas City. Tourists walk the rim; the desert keeps the
rays.

Sources: NASA Earth Observatory Meteor Crater (2025); USGS
Meteor Crater pages; NASA asteroid-day pages.

## Ries — the farmed ring

Southern Germany hides a 24–26 km complex crater under
farms and a medieval town: 14.8 million years old, dug by a
kilometer-plus rock at ~70,000 km/h with 1.8 million
Hiroshima-bombs of energy. Nördlingen sits inside the
depression, its church built of suevite — impact-melted
rock — and its stones studded with 70,000 tonnes of
microscopic diamonds from the shock. Crater lakes filled
the bowl for two million years, then silt won; loess
covered the rest. Forests trace the buried rim in dark
green from orbit; fields mark the floor. For centuries
everyone called it volcanic — until Shoemaker and Chao
proved the sky fell in 1960.

Sources: ESA Ries crater (2019); NASA Earth Observatory
Ries (2026); RiesKraterMuseum research.

## Pingualuit — the clear eye

Northern Quebec holds a perfect 3.4-km circle, 1.4 million
years old, filled with a 246-m-deep lake of the purest
water measured — no tributaries, only rain and snow, last
to freeze and last to thaw, open just weeks each August and
September. Its sediments keep an unbroken climate diary of
the ice ages. Beside it, the 8-km Couture ring holds its
own lesser lake. Two bullseyes in tundra, readable from
space like punctuation.

Sources: NASA Earth Observatory crater lakes (2012);
Sépaq Pingualuit pages.

## Small rings — Kaali and its kin

Estonia's Kaali field: nine small pits in farmland, the
largest about a football field across, dug within human
memory a few thousand years ago — iron age witnesses,
mythology keeping the receipt. Saudi Arabia's Wabar sands
hide similar fresh glass. At this size every crater is a
local event: a farm, a forest stand, a suburb's bad day —
and the L13 portrait. Simple bowls all: no central peaks,
no rings of rings, just rim, breccia lens, and ejecta.

## Anatomy at close range

Simple craters (Meteor, Pingualuit, Kaali): bowl, raised
rim, overturned rock flap, breccia floor, rays fading in
thousands of years. Complex craters (Ries): terraced walls
slumped inward, central uplift, impact melt sheet
(suevite), fractured basement leaking groundwater into
springs — Nördlingen drinks impact plumbing. Multi-ring
belongs at L10/L11; at L12 the lesson is rim-versus-cone:
craters have rims without mountains, ejecta without flows,
and no vents, dikes, or ash — the negative of `volcanoes.md`.

## Erasure and neighbors

Earth destroys small craters fastest: weather, rivers, ice,
and burial — Meteor survives on desert, Pingualuit on
shield rock, Ries as farmland over fill. The Moon keeps all
(see L10 `moons.md`): Tycho's rays cross half the near
side; Mars keeps Ries-scale rings for billions of years
(see L10 `impacts.md`). Counting small craters dates young
lava seas; their absence dates Earth's restless surface.

## Key numbers

- Meteor Crater: 1.2 km across, 180 m deep, rim +45 m;
50,000 years; 30–50 m iron rock at 12–20 km/s; Apollo
training ground.
- Ries: 24–26 km; 14.8 Ma; ~70,000 km/h; 70,000 tonnes of
micro-diamonds; town inside; church of suevite.
- Pingualuit: 3.4 km; 1.4 Ma; lake 246 m deep; open weeks
a year; Couture 8 km beside it.
- Kaali: nine pits, largest ~100 m, a few thousand years —
human memory.
- Rule: rims without mountains, ejecta without flows.
- L12 frame: 1–100 km — one ring with rim, floor, and
blanket per view.

Sources: NASA, USGS, ESA, and Sépaq pages named above.

## Example objects

Meteor Crater is the textbook standard: fresh bowl,
trained astronauts, city-killer math.

Ries is the inhabited standard: town, church, diamonds,
farms — 15 million years domesticated.

Pingualuit is the pure standard: perfect circle, purest
water, climate diary in mud.

Kaali is the human standard: witnessed pits in farmland —
myth keeping the receipt.

Tycho (Moon) is the preserved standard: rays halfway
across the near side — what Earth erases.

Sources: pages named above; L10 `impacts.md`.
