# Volcanoes — single mountains of fire

This page is the L12 part doc for volcanic edifices at
1–100 km: cones, calderas, maars, and lava fields one
frame at a time — the scale where a single mountain fills
the view. The volcanic regions live in L11
(`volcanoes.md`); the global physics in L10
(`volcanism.md`); towns built on volcanoes sit in
`towns.md`; vents and craters under 100 m belong at L13.

From 10 km up, a volcano is architecture. A perfect cone
with a bowl crater; a ragged crater holding a lava lake;
a blue lake in a ring wall 10 km across; a black tongue
of lava crossing green forest; a town grid stopped at the
edge of a flow. Each edifice is one eruption history made
visible — and some are still being written.

## The cone family at close range

**Cinder cones** are the small sharp ones: under ~300 m,
bowl craters, often born in a single decade. Paricutin
grew on a Mexican farm from 1943 to 1952 and stopped;
Sunset Crater outside Flagstaff keeps its red oxidized
top; hundreds dot the San Francisco field and Hawaii's
flanks. Climb one and the crater marks the vent exactly.

**Stratocones** are the grand ones: Fuji-symmetry built of
layered lava and ash. At L12 a stratocone fills the frame
entirely — base 10–20 km, summit in the clouds — with
radial erosion gullies (barrancas) scoring the flanks and
parasitic cones on the skirts. St. Helens is the dissected
example: on May 18, 1980 its north flank collapsed and the
lateral blast leveled 600 square km of forest in minutes,
then a dome began rebuilding in the crater — destruction
and construction in one frame, still steaming.

**Shields at this zoom** lose their mountain look:
Hawaiian slopes average single degrees, so a 10-km frame
shows crack systems, pit craters, and lava tubes rather
than a peak — the mountain is the island (see L11
`volcanoes.md`).

**Domes** plug the vent: St. Helens' gray lobe, Soufriere
Hills' growing tooth — quiet piles that fail loudly into
ash clouds racing downhill.

**Maars** are water explosions frozen as circles: wide low
craters, often holding lakes, from magma meeting
groundwater. No mountain at all — just a ring and a mirror.

**Calderas** dwarf everything: Crater Lake's 10-km ring
holds impossibly blue water around Wizard Island's cinder
cone — a cone inside a caldera inside a mountain. From
orbit the ring wall, not the peak, is the signature.

Sources: USGS volcano-type pages; NPS volcano pages; L10
`volcanism.md`.

## Lava fields — fire as ground

A'a and pahoehoe: the same basalt as broken clinker or
ropy skin, mapped by color and texture across flows that
crossed farmland in days. Lava tubes thread underneath —
caves for a future L13 page — skylighting where roofs fell.
Eruption columns from orbit: ash drifting downwind,
sulfur staining snow, new black deltas where flows meet
the sea (Hawaii's coast grows in parcels). Towns read the
flows as property lines: Hilo's streets stop at the 19th-
century flow edge; Catania paves over Etna's; Naples
spreads under Vesuvius with evacuation routes drawn in the
city plan (`towns.md`, `networks.md`).

## How one mountain lives and dies

Paricutin: 1943 cinders, then lava, quiet by 1952 — a full
life in a decade. St. Helens: 1980 blast, dome growth
through the 2000s, glaciers returning to the crater. Fuji:
last 1707, forests reclaiming flanks, monitored for the
months of warning volcanoes usually give. Etna: near-
continuous Strombolian breath with flank eruptions every
few years. Vesuvius: 1944 last eruption, three million
neighbors — the most watched mountain on Earth. Lifetimes
span days to millennia; the region outlives every cone
(see L11 `volcanoes.md`).

## How it interacts with the others

Cities skirt and watch (`cities.md`, `towns.md`): Naples,
Catania, Hilo, Goma — observatories, sirens, and land-use
lines drawn on flows. Roads thread between (`networks.md`):
Hawaii's Chain of Craters Road buried and rebuilt,
Iceland's ring road washed by jokulhlaups. Farms take the
ash soils (`farmland.md`): among the richest ground on
Earth, terraced to the crater rims on Java. Forests reclaim
(`forests-deserts.md`): blast zone to wildflowers to fir in
decades. Lakes pool in the bowls (`landscapes.md`,
`lakes.md` at L11): crater mirrors, caldera blues. The sky
carries the plume (`sky.md`): 2010 Eyjafjallajokull ash
closing a continent's airspace from one small mountain.

Fuji cone, public-domain USGS/NASA) is proposed for a
future pass. No new raster file is added here.

## Key numbers

- Cinder cones: under ~300 m, often one eruption;
Paricutin 1943–52.
- St. Helens 1980: blast leveled ~600 km2; dome rebuilding
since.
- Crater Lake caldera: ~10 km across; Wizard Island cone
inside.
- Fuji base 10–20 km; Etna near-continuous; Vesuvius 1944
with millions nearby.
- Eyjafjallajokull 2010: one small mountain, a week of
closed European airspace.
- L12 frame: 1–100 km — one edifice with its flows per view.

Sources: USGS, NPS, and Smithsonian GVP pages; L10
`volcanism.md`; L11 `volcanoes.md`.

## Example objects

St. Helens is the dissected standard: blast, dome,
returning glacier — a life cycle in one frame.

Fuji is the perfect standard: symmetry, gullies, last
1707 — the icon at rest.

Paricutin is the brief standard: farm to cone to quiet in
a decade — birth watched whole.

Crater Lake is the drowned standard: 10-km ring, blue
water, cone inside — collapse holding sky.

Vesuvius is the watched standard: 1944 quiet, millions
near — risk drawn in streets.

Sources: USGS, NPS, and Smithsonian GVP pages.
