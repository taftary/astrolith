# L11 behind us — Regions of a planet as previous level

This page is the bridge
from L11 (Regions of a planet) into L12 (Cities and landscapes): what
the previous level looks like, what carries over when you zoom in,
and what changes at L12 scale. The part docs from the loop's first
pass now exist — follow the cross-links below.
Entry itself — the visual transition and its effects — lives in
[the L11-to-L12 entry](./entry.md). The dated timeline runs through

## What L11 is and how it looks

L11 is the region scale at the R11 anchor (500 km range or basin, 5.01e5 m, e 5.70).
large mountain ranges, big basins, long coastlines, whole countries. Its content per the ladder
is continents, countries, mountain ranges, with the part docs covering continents and landmasses,
mountain ranges and highlands, plains and basins, oceans and coasts, rivers and valleys.
Our example region is any Earth region: green-brown land and blue seas, white ice on high peaks,
with rivers threading the valleys and towns along them as points.

Inside the view are the parts. The base is the landmasses: continents, cratons, shields, growth
and breakup, the bimodal Earth. The lift is the ranges: mountain belts, highlands, orogeny,
wearing to hills — the Andes run about 8,900 km long and 200–700 km wide, the longest
continental range (Britannica, about 5,500 miles). The flat is the plains: basins, lowlands, deserts, fill and drought.
The blue is the waters at regional scale: oceans, seas, coasts, islands — oceans cover about
71 percent of the surface and hold about 97 percent of Earth's water (NASA Earth facts). The flow is the rivers:
the Nile runs about 6,650 km and the Amazon about 6,400 km on the conventional ranking
(Britannica; the order is debated by source and mouth) — both cross many L11 cells.
The farmland grid is not yet visible; the US survey section of 1 sq mi (2.6 km2, 640 acres)
is far below this zoom.

## What carries over into L12

Everything L11 shows at region scale is still there, only closer.
The region L11 frames as one patch becomes the ground: the mountain axis, the coastline,
the major river path, the climate and vegetation tint — still around us. The face L11 watches
as land and water becomes the terrain under our feet — cities you can drive across, road networks
you can trace, farmland patchwork you can drive across in an hour. The waters L11 counts as blue
shapes become the lakes with shorelines, the rivers with channels, the valleys with walls.
The longest Interstate, I-90 Seattle–Boston at 3,020.44 mi (FHWA Route Log, longest US Interstate), runs whole across L11; a single L12
cell holds only a 10–100 km segment with its street grid. Sources: ladder
L11 and L12 rows; NASA Earth Observatory pages; US Census urban-area pages.

## What changes at this zoom

**From regions to cities and landscapes.** At L11 the view holds
regions at the R11 anchor (500 km, 5.01e5 m, e 5.70). At L12 each L11 cell opens
into cities and landscapes at the R11 anchor (10 km, 1.0e4 m, e 4.00) through two
milestones: areas about 10 km across — the size of
large metros, lakes, valleys, farmland blocks — the cities, towns, networks, farmland,
and lakes and valleys (see [cities](./cities.md),
[towns](./towns.md), [networks](./networks.md), [farmland](./farmland.md),
[landscapes](./landscapes.md).

**From a patch to a built ground.** The L11 patch — land brown-green, water blue, relief
as shadows — fills past the edges and becomes streets and fields: the true curvature
(about 450 cell units of radius at L12, nearly flat) carries the ground to a horizon ring
at the true distance, the day-night line becomes local light, the sky arc stands without
haze, and the region outline and ground stay faintly around the city until the next open.
City portals sit at river and coast vertices. A 10 km city spans roughly one-fiftieth of an L11 region. The largest US urban area, New York–Jersey City–Newark, covers
3,248 sq mi (8,413 km2, about 100 km across, 19.4 million people in 2020); Los Angeles–Long Beach–Anaheim
covers 1,637 sq mi (4,239 km2, about 65–75 km across, 12.2 million people, densest large US area).
Lake Superior runs 563 km long and 258 km wide at its greatest width (Britannica) — its length is L11-scale, its bays are L12.
The Grand Canyon runs 446 km long (277 miles, NPS) but averages 16 km rim-to-rim (10 miles, up to 29 km) — length L11, width L12.
Populations (shown points) never open; only portals do. Sources: ladder L11 and L12 rows; ADR 0010 (portal vs
population).

**From reading a region to standing over a city.** L11 shows a region — base, lift, flat,
blue, and flow. L12 shows what one patch of that region holds up close: the grey of the cities,
the feather of the suburbs, the lines of the roads, the rectangles of the fields, the dark of
the lakes and the cut of the valleys. The region is still there, 100 to 1,000 km around — but here the story is the city, not the region. Recent night-light
change is bidirectional rather than one-way brightening: a 2014–2022 Black Marble analysis of VIIRS data found global
radiance up 34 percent with neighboring brightening and dimming side by side, while Annual NLCD (Collection 1, 1985–2023,
Landsat 30 m, 16 classes) maps the matching land-cover change year by year. Sources: NASA Earth
facts page; USGS science pages; NASA Black Marble 2014–2022 analysis; USGS Annual NLCD Collection 1.

## How the parts fit together

One city runs through the part docs. The cores
([cities](./cities.md)) set the built mass — cities, downtowns, districts; the feather
([towns](./towns.md)) sets the spread — towns, suburbs, villages, sprawl; the lines
([networks](./networks.md)) set the links — roads, rails, infrastructure corridors; the patchwork
([farmland](./farmland.md)) sets the managed land — farmland, fields, countryside; and the anchors
([landscapes](./landscapes.md)) set the green and blue — lakes, rivers at city scale, valleys, hills, forests and parks.
The dated version of this story runs through [the lifetime](./lifetime.md), and the dive
that brings you here is in [the entry](./entry.md). Sources: L11 part docs
as the pattern; NASA Earth and USGS pages.

## Key numbers

- L11 anchor: 500 km range or basin 5.01 × 10^5 m (e 5.70); regions, continents, countries, mountain ranges per ladder row.
- L12 anchor: city 10 km, 1.0e4 m (e 4.00); cities and landscapes, lakes, valleys per ladder row.
- Earth: equatorial diameter 12,756 km (7,926 miles, NASA); oceans cover about 71 percent; largest urban areas about 70–100 km across.
- Region to city step: a 100 km city is about 0.1 of a 1,000 km region; a 10 km town about 0.01; lake and valley widths in the same range.
- Portals: many region portals per L11 cell lead to many city portals per L12 cell (counts fixed in the loop); populations are shown points that never open (ADR 0010).
- Land survey: US section 1 sq mi (2.6 km2); quarter-section 800 m; quarter-quarter 400 m — visible only at L12.
- Road context: I-90 3,020.44 mi whole (FHWA Route Log, longest US Interstate); single L12 cell sees 10–100 km plus 0.1–1 km blocks.
