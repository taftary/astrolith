# Sky — the weather at city scale

This page is the L12 part doc for the sky at 1–100 km:
cloud fields, fog, lake bands, storm cells, and city-made
weather — the scale where one storm system with the towns
under it fills the view. The global winds live in L10
(`clouds-weather.md`); regional storms in L11
(`weather.md`); lake snows in L11 (`lakes.md`,
`weather.md`); night lights in `cities.md`.

From 10 km up — or from a satellite passing over a metro —
the sky reads as texture over the grid: popcorn cumulus
over heated suburbs, a stratus lid graying the whole
valley, fog filling river bends at dawn, a thunderhead
anvil throwing shade across three towns, snow bands
streaming off the lake in parallel white streets. At this
zoom weather has addresses, and cities write back.

## Cloud fields — popcorn and lids

Fair-weather cumulus dots the afternoon where the ground
heats unevenly: first over dark plowed fields and asphalt,
later drifting downwind in streets aligned with the wind.
Stratus lids the whole frame on inversion mornings — the
marine layer over coastal metros burning off by noon,
valley gray lasting weeks in winter. Anvils mark the day's
towers: cumulonimbus punching to the tropopause ceiling
(see L10 `clouds-weather.md`), hail and downbursts under
the core, lightning mapped strike by strike. Von Karman
vortex streets trail downwind of islands and peaks —
cloud calligraphy spelling the wind.

## Fog — the cloud on the ground

Radiation fog pools in valleys and river bends on clear
calm nights, gone by mid-morning; advection fog rides warm
air over cold water — San Francisco's summer blanket,
Grand Banks' grey weeks; tule fog seals California's
Central Valley for days, shutting highways; ice fog
crystallizes the air itself below −30°C in Fairbanks and
Yakutsk. Fog closes airports, piles cars, and waters
redwoods drop by drop — the redwood fog belt is a forest
made by stratus (`forests-deserts.md`).

## Lake bands — snow streets

Cold air over warm open water builds cloud streets that
dump snow on lee shores in bands narrow enough to bury one
town and spare the next (see L11 `weather.md` for the
mechanism: ~13°C gap, fetch, axis-aligned single bands at
3+ inches per hour). Buffalo, Erie, South Bend, Sapporo's
sea-effect twin off the Sea of Japan — winter economies of
plows, roofs, and school closures. The season ends when
ice caps the lake; warming shortens it from both ends.

## Storm cells — one thunderhead per town

A supercell at this zoom shows hooks, inflow tails, and
debris balls on radar, hail swaths and tornado tracks on
the ground in `farmland.md` fields. Flash floods fill
washes and underpasses in minutes — the desert city's
killer, not the tornado's. Hurricanes at this zoom are
rainbands and surge, not spirals (see L11 `weather.md`);
their tornadoes spin out of the bands over suburbs.
Lightning starts the fires of `forests-deserts.md`; hail
strips the crops of `farmland.md`.

## City-made weather

Cities heat their own sky: the heat island runs degrees
warmer than surrounding fields (`cities.md`), lifting
popcorn over downtown first, bursting thirstier storms
downwind — Atlanta's afternoon dome, Houston's bay-breeze
collisions. Roughness slows surface winds and piles
pollution into brown domes; downwind suburbs harvest the
rain the city seeded. Night glows orange under the lid —
the Black Marble's bright blobs (`cities.md`) are heat and
light leaking to space together. Green roofs, white roofs,
and park cool-islands are the adaptation drawn in the
L13 green page.

## How it interacts with the others

Cities bake (`cities.md`, `towns.md`): islands of heat,
domes of haze, plows for the bands. Roads ice first
(`networks.md`): bridges freezing before pavement, fog
closing the passes. Farms watch (`farmland.md`): hail,
frost, and the rain the city seeded downwind. Forests burn
from its lightning (`forests-deserts.md`): smoke columns
joining the anvils. Coasts steer (`coasts.md`): sea
breezes, marine layers, surge under the bands. Lakes fuel
(`landscapes.md`, L11 `lakes.md`): streets of snow while
ice lasts. Valleys trap (`landscapes.md`): inversions,
tule, smog weeks. Volcanoes close the sky (`volcanoes.md`):
ash grounding fleets from one small mountain. The global
jets and tilt run it all from L10/L11.

## Key numbers

- Cumulus over heat first (asphalt, plowed dark); stratus
lids valleys for weeks.
- Lake bands: ~13°C gap, 3+/hr single bands, sun a county
away; season ends with ice.
- Supercells: hooks and tracks in fields; flash floods the
desert killer.
- Heat islands degrees warmer; storms thirstier downwind;
glow leaking to space.
- Fog: radiation nightly, advection seasonal, tule by the
week, ice fog below −30°C.
- L12 frame: 1–100 km — one storm system with its towns
per view.

## Example objects

Buffalo bands are the lake standard: buried town, spared
county — snow with addresses.

A supercell is the violent standard: hook on radar, track
in fields — minutes per town.

Tule fog is the still standard: valley sealed for days —
highways closed, orchards dripping.

The heat dome is the urban standard: popcorn first over
downtown — the city raining on its suburbs.

An ash plume is the closed standard: one mountain grounding
a continent — 2010's week without wings.
