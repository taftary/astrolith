# Glaciers — the slow rivers of ice

This page is the L12 part doc for glaciers at 1–100 km:
valley tongues, moraines, kettles, and outwash — the scale
where one ice system fills the view from snowfield to
snout. The ice sheets and shelves live in L11 (`ice.md`);
the global caps in L10 (`surface.md`, `hydrosphere.md`);
moraines as farmland in `farmland.md`; the dated ice ages
in `lifetime.md`.

From 10 km up, a valley glacier is a white tongue with
dirty edges: crevassed falls where it steepens, a grey
stripe of medial moraine where two tongues joined, a
crescent terminal moraine bulldozed at its farthest
advance, and a milky river leaving the snout. Behind the
ice, the valley is a U — the V of rivers ground square —
with hanging side valleys, polished walls, and lakes in
the overdeepened bowls. Ice writes in straight lines;
water writes in curves.

## Anatomy of one glacier

Snow compacts to firn to ice in the accumulation zone
above the year's snowline; below, the ablation zone melts
faster than ice arrives. The equilibrium line between them
rises with warming — the glacier's health drawn as an
altitude. Ice flows centimeters to meters a day, cracking
transverse where it stretches over falls, marginal where
it drags the walls, calving where it meets water. Debris
rides the edges as lateral moraines, joins as medial
stripes, and dumps as terminal crescents marking each
standstill — moraines are the glacier's diary, readable
centuries later in forest lines.

Meltwater does the finishing: subglacial streams carve
tunnels, eskers ridge where tunnels filled with gravel,
kettles pit where buried ice blocks melted (the Pleistocene
moraines and kettle lakes of `lifetime.md:26-29`), and
outwash plains spread braided gravel to the sea. Silt
ground to flour turns rivers and lakes milky turquoise —
the color of active grinding.

Sources: NSIDC glacier pages; NPS glacier pages.

## Retreat — the measured goodbye

Alpine glaciers worldwide have retreated dramatically since
1850, and the numbers are gravestones: Glacier National
Park held ~150 glaciers then and 26 named ones today; the
Muir Glacier in Alaska retreated visible miles in a
lifetime of photographs; the Alps' tongues stair-step moraines
decade by decade. Each1890s moraine now stands forested
far above the ice; each trimline on valley walls marks
where ice stood within living memory. Melt feeds the rivers
of `landscapes.md` in summer and the seas of L11 `ice.md`
forever — mountain ice is the first reservoir to empty.

Glacier hazards belong to this scale too: ice-dammed lakes
bursting into outburst floods, debuttressing walls
collapsing as ice leaves, thinning tongues detaching into
dead ice that pits into kettle fields. The ice-to-lake
succession — tongue, lake, meadow — runs wherever glaciers
die.

## How it interacts with the others

Valleys frame the view (`landscapes.md`): U-troughs,
hanging falls, fjord fingers where tongues met the sea.
Towns drink and fear (`towns.md`, `cities.md`): summer
meltwater, winter flood storage, outburst paths zoned or
ignored. Roads climb the troughs (`networks.md`): rail and
highway grades following the U, tunnels under the cols.
Farms take the outwash (`farmland.md`): gravel plains,
kettle lakes, loess from ground rock. Forests march behind
retreat (`forests-deserts.md`): lichen to shrubs to fir on
moraines in sequence. Lakes are the children (`landscapes.md`,
L11 `lakes.md`): tarn, finger lake, kettle pond. Volcanoes
under ice flood catastrophically (`volcanoes.md`):
jokulhlaups from subglacial eruptions. The sky feeds the
snow (`sky.md`): winter storms building, summer heat
starving. The sheets and shelves set the sea level from L11
(`ice.md`).

## Key numbers

- Flow: cm to m per day; crevasses at falls and margins;
moraines diary every standstill.
- Glacier NP: ~150 glaciers (1850) to 26 named today.
- Trimlines and moraines date retreat within living memory.
- Succession: tongue to lake to meadow where ice dies.
- Melt feeds summer rivers and all seas, grain by grain.
- L12 frame: 1–100 km — one glacier with moraines and
outwash per view.

Sources: NPS and NSIDC glacier pages; L11 `ice.md`.

## Example objects

Muir is the retreating standard: miles in a lifetime of
photographs — the measured goodbye.

Alpine tongues are the diary standard: stair-step moraines
decade by decade — retreat you can walk.

Kettle fields are the after standard: pits where buried
ice died — Pleistocene receipts under farms.

Jokulhlaup plains are the violent standard: subglacial
eruption to flood in hours — fire under ice.

Hanging valleys are the sculpted standard: U-troughs,
falls, fjords — water's V ground square.

Sources: NPS and NSIDC pages; L11 `ice.md`.
