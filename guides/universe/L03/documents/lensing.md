# Lensing — the universe's telescopes

This page is the L03 part doc for gravitational lensing:
arcs, rings, multiple images, and mass maps — the scale
where whole clusters bend whole backgrounds. Paragraphs
live in `entry.md:31-36,76`, `clusters.md:83,136-146,151-
153`, and L01 (`nodes.md:29-32`); CMB lensing stays in L01
(`background.md`); galaxy-scale rings link from L04; jets
in L04 (`jets.md`).

Mass bends light: a cluster's gravity — dark matter most
of all — warps space into a lens that brightens, stretches,
and multiplies the galaxies behind it. Strong lensing draws
arcs; perfect alignment draws rings; weak lensing shears
thousands of faint shapes into mass maps. Every arc is a
weighing and a telescope at once: the lens's mass from the
distortion, the background magnified hundreds of times for
free.

## Strong lensing — arcs and images

**Abell 1689** — the arc factory: one of the most massive
clusters known, its yellow-white members 2.2 billion
light-years out ringed by blue arcs — background galaxies
billions of light-years farther, smeared, mirrored on
either side, their star clusters and dust lanes magnified
into resolvability. In perfect alignment the arcs close
into Einstein rings; here, ring sections per galaxy.

**SMACS 0723** — Webb's first deep field (July 11, 2022):
12.5 hours reaching past Hubble's weeks-deep fields, the
cluster 4.6 billion years back lensing thousands behind —
arcs crossing critical curves, knots magnified hundreds of
times with a microlensed transient, an Einstein cross
candidate only JWST resolves. Mass ~8×10^14 Suns; 19 image
families constraining two dark-matter halos.

**El Gordo, MACS J0416, Abell 370** — mergers as lenses:
the most massive high-redshift merger doubling as
magnifier; Frontier Fields clusters mapped to find the
first galaxies (see `clusters.md`, `mergers.md`).

## Weak lensing and mass maps

Beyond arcs, every background shape shears slightly —
averaging thousands yields the mass map, dark matter
included, no light required. Bullet's offset (see
`mergers.md`): pink gas left behind, blue lensing mass
with the galaxies — dark matter proved by separation.
Cosmic shear across surveys weighs the universe itself
(see L01 `cosmic-web.md`); CMB lensing stays at L01.

## Galaxy-scale rings — the small lenses

Single ellipticals lens quasars and galaxies into rings
and crosses arcseconds wide — galaxy masses weighed one
by one, linked from L04 (`ellipticals.md`); time delays
between images measure the Hubble constant independently.
The EHT "rings" in L04 `nuclei.md` are black-hole shadows,
not lenses — different rings, noted to avoid confusion.

## Key numbers

- Abell 1689: members 2.2 Gly; arcs billions beyond;
mirrored, magnified.
- SMACS 0723: z=0.39 (4.6 Gly back); 12.5 hrs past
Hubble weeks; mass ~8×10^14 Suns; knots ×100s.
- Bullet: gas-versus-mass offset — dark matter separated.
- Rings: perfect alignment; delays measuring expansion.
- L03 frame: ~10^23 m — one lensing cluster per view.

## Example objects

Abell 1689 is the arc standard: blue rings sections —
weighing by distortion.

SMACS 0723 is the deep standard: 12.5 hours, thousands
behind — first deep field.

The Bullet is the proof standard: offset gas and mass —
dark matter separated.

An Einstein ring is the alignment standard: perfect
circle — geometry closed.
