# Groups — clusters in miniature

Poor groups are L3's quiet
majority: a few dozen galaxies at most, spiral-rich, with little or no
hot gas — and the building blocks rich clusters are still assembling
from. Most galaxies in the nearby universe live in one. Sibling docs:
`clusters.md`, `icm.md`, `members.md`; bridge in
`previous-l2.md`, entry in `entry.md`, timeline in `lifetime.md`.

## What they are and how they look

A poor group reads as a loose gathering: no more than about 50 bound
members across 1–2 Mpc (3–7 million light-years), total mass near
10^13 solar masses, galaxy velocity spreads near 150 km/s — against
hundreds to thousands of members, 1–5 Mpc, 10^14–10^15 solar masses,
and ~800–1,000 km/s for a rich cluster. Groups are the smallest
aggregates and the most common galaxy structure in the nearby universe:
at least half of local galaxies belong to one. Spirals dominate where
clusters would show ellipticals, and only about half of nearby groups
show any diffuse X-ray glow at all (confined to the inner 10–50% of the
virial radius, typically 50–500 kpc) — the deep hot atmosphere is a
rich-cluster privilege. X-ray-bright groups tend to host early-type
members; the rest are effectively X-ray-dark. Sources: Sparke &
Gallagher (Galaxies in the Universe); Mulchaey 2000 (ARA&A 38:289,
X-ray properties of groups); Wikipedia "Galaxy group".

## Example objects

- Local Group: home — 134+ known members within 1 Mpc of the barycenter
  (barycenter ~447 kpc away in Andromeda's direction), binding mass
  (2.47 +/- 0.15) x 10^12 solar masses, ~16.7 million light-years tip to
  tip (5.11 Mpc major axis) in a dumbbell: a Milky
  Way lobe and an Andromeda lobe ~800 kpc apart, approaching at ~110–123
  km/s with only ~49 km/s group velocity dispersion. Two ~10^12-solar-mass spirals dominate (Milky Way
  (1.54 +/- 0.1) x 10^12, Andromeda (1.5 +/- 0.5) x 10^12),
  Triangulum (M33, ~5 x 10^10) comes third, and each giant drags a dwarf-satellite
  system (Magellanic Clouds, Sagittarius, Fornax, Sculptor, Draco, Ursa
  Minor, Leo I/II plus dozens of ultra-faints for
  us; M32, M110, NGC 147/185, And I–XXII plus ultra-faints for them).
  The classic Milky-Way–Andromeda merger in ~4.5 Gyr ("Milkomeda", likely
  a giant elliptical) now carries an update: 2025 Gaia+HST Monte Carlo
  work (Sawala et al., Nature Astronomy 9:1206, 100,000 runs over 22
  variables) with LMC/M33 gravity included gives only ~50% within
  10 Gyr and ~2% head-on in 4–5 Gyr — half the runs pass at ~500,000
  light-years and decay slowly by dynamical friction, half never come
  close. Sources: Wikipedia "Local Group" (Makarov infobox values);
  Wikipedia "Andromeda–Milky Way collision"; Sohn et al. 2012 (ApJ
  753:7); van der Marel et al. 2012 (ApJ 753:8); Sawala et al. 2025;
  NASA "Apocalypse When?" 2025-06-02.
- Stephan's Quintet (HCG 92): the compact-group archetype, known since
  1877 — four physically associated galaxies plus one foreground
  interloper (NGC 7320 at ~39 Mly vs 210–340 Mly for the rest; a sixth
  neighbour, NGC 7320C at the group redshift with a tidal tail to
  NGC 7319, restores the "quintet" physically). The intruder NGC 7318B
  plows into pre-existing tidal debris at several million km/h, driving
  a Milky-Way-sized shock that heats gas to ~6 million K (Chandra
  X-rays, log L_X ~42.16 erg/s, ~0.75 keV intragroup gas), churns
  molecular hydrogen (Spitzer's strongest turbulent H2 signal, surviving
  in a cloudy multi-shock layer), and lights an intragroup starburst
  (SQ-A, ~1.45 solar masses/yr) with tidal dwarfs. FAST finds a 0.6-Mpc
  HI structure with starless clouds plus inner/outer tails; the VLA
  finds HI outside the member galaxies, not inside them. Compact groups
  are not stable over a Hubble time: visible mass falls far short of
  binding mass (HCG 92 M/L ~44), so they merge. Sources: Wikipedia
  "Stephan's Quintet"; Moles et al. 1998 (A&A 334:473); Hickson 1982
  (ApJ 255:382), 1997 (ARA&A 35:357); Ponman et al. 1996 (MNRAS
  283:690); Xu et al. 2022 (Nature 610:461, FAST HI); Emonts et al. 2025
  (ApJ 978:111, ALMA/CARMA CO); Chandra Stephan's Quintet album;
  JWST MIRI Stephan's Quintet release 2022-07-12.
- Compact groups as a class: Paul Hickson's 1982 catalogue lists 100
  systems (HCG 1–100) of typically ~4–5 galaxies in close, relatively
  isolated configurations — most show morphological or kinematic
  peculiarities, nuclear radio/infrared emission, and starburst or AGN
  activity, hold large quantities of diffuse gas, and are dynamically
  dominated by dark matter. They most likely form as subsystems inside
  looser associations and evolve by gravity toward merging and demise;
  they are surprisingly numerous and may play a significant role in
  galaxy evolution. Nearby showpieces beyond HCG 92 include HCG 16 (four
  of seven members in the Hubble quartet view), HCG 31 (interacting
  starburst chain), HCG 44 (Leo quartet NGC 3185/3187/3190/3193), HCG 57
  (Copeland Septet, eight members), and Seyfert's Sextet (HCG 79).
  Sources: Wikipedia "Hickson Compact Group"; Hickson 1997 (ARA&A
  35:357).
- Fossil groups: the end-state — one luminous giant elliptical plus an
  X-ray halo, the intermediate (L*) galaxies long since cannibalized through
  dynamical friction, dwarfs remaining. Timescales for dynamical friction
  on L* galaxies imply fossils are old, undisturbed systems with little
  L* infall since initial collapse — isolated laboratories
  for galaxy plus intragroup-medium evolution, a prediction borne out by
  cosmological N-body plus Magneticum hydrodynamic volumes (halo
  concentration tracks "fossilness"). The nearest is NGC 6482, an
  elliptical ~180 million light-years out in Hercules (Chandra: old,
  relaxed X-ray halo). The formal fossil definition (Jones et al.)
  requires a ≥2-magnitude R-band gap between first and second-ranked
  members inside half the virial radius plus extended X-ray emission —
  this document uses the shorthand above. Source: Wikipedia
  "Galaxy group" (Mulchaey 2000; Hickson 1997); Khosroshahi et al. 2004
  (MNRAS 349:1240, NGC 6482); Ragagnin et al. 2019 (MNRAS 486:4001).
- Proto-groups: groups caught forming — galaxies and protogalaxies in
  separate dark halos fusing into one group halo, the small-scale
  analogue of protoclusters. zCOSMOS-deep finds them at 1.8 < z < 3
  (Diener et al. 2013, ApJ 765:109). They belong on the timeline in
  `lifetime.md`, not in the nearby census. Source: Wikipedia
  "Galaxy group" (proto-groups).
- Typical size: groups ≤~50 members, 1–2 Mpc across, ~10^13 solar
  masses; clusters hundreds–thousands, 1–5 Mpc, 10^14–10^15 solar
  masses. Source: Wikipedia "Galaxy group".
- Neighbourhood census: the closest groups beyond home are the IC 342 /
  Maffei group (LGG 104, nearest neighbour, hidden behind the Milky Way
  plane), the M81 group at 3.5 Mpc, the Centaurus A / M83 group at
  3.66 Mpc, the Sculptor group at 3.9 Mpc, and the Canes Venatici,
  M101, M51, Leo Triplet, and Leo I groups out to ~12 Mpc — the
  LGG (Lyons Groups of Galaxies) numbering runs through this whole
  nearby set. NGC 7331 (Deer Lick, LGG 459) sits half a degree from
  Stephan's Quintet and hosts foreground interloper NGC 7320. Source:
  Wikipedia "List of galaxy groups and clusters".

## How they are cataloged (the group-catalog era)

Group science runs on redshift-friends catalogs, not single objects —
the counterpart to `clusters.md`'s SZ/X-ray survey era. Hickson's 100
compact groups (1982, ApJ 255:382; Atlas 1993) select by compactness
plus isolation on the sky; Garcia (1993, A&AS 100:47) lists 485 nearby
loose groups (LGG-style); Geller–Huchra (1983, ApJS 52:61) and later
2MASS high-/low-density-contrast catalogs (Crook et al. 2007, ApJ
655:790: ~300 HDC groups plus ~1,200 LDC systems) go all-sky in the
near-infrared. From SDSS, the Yang et al. halo-based group finder
(2007, ApJ 671:153) yields ~300,000 groups, and the GAMA group catalog
(Robotham et al. 2011, MNRAS 416:2640) gives ~14,000 groups with
dynamical masses — the workhorse samples for pre-processing and
quenching studies. From X-rays, eROSITA's first all-sky survey (eRASS1,
DR1 January 2024) yields 12,247 optically confirmed groups and clusters
(z=0.003–1.32) with a 5,259-system cosmology subsample, cross-matched
by ACT DR6 SZ flags — the faint-group end that ROSAT could barely see.
Sources: Hickson 1982; Garcia 1993; Crook et al. 2007; Yang et al.
2007; Robotham et al. 2011 (GAMA); Merloni et al. 2024 + Bulbul et al.
2024 (eROSITA DR1/eRASS1).

## How they are weighed

Three scales weigh a group, and the lightest ones are the hardest. Member
velocity dispersions give virial masses (~150 km/s typical; Local Group
~49 km/s; HCG 92 ~337 km/s) — cheap but noisy for N ≤ 5 systems where
interlopers and projection dominate. X-ray temperature/density profiles
give hydrostatic masses where gas exists (HCG 62's ~1 keV halo; NGC 6482's
relaxed fossil halo; Stephan's ~0.75 keV shock gas) — clean but only for
the X-ray-bright half. Weak lensing plus stacked kinematics calibrate the
rest (GAMA dynamical masses; eRASS1 weak-lensing-calibrated scaling
relations), anchoring the ~10^13-solar-mass group scale between giant
ellipticals and 10^14–10^15 clusters. The Bullet Group (SL2S
J08544-0121) is the low-mass analogue of the Bullet Cluster: the
lowest-mass system showing dark-matter–baryon separation. Sources:
Hickson et al. 1992 (ApJ 399:353, dynamics); Ponman et al. 1996 (ROSAT
HCG survey); Mulchaey 2000; Robotham et al. 2011 (GAMA); Bulbul et al.
2024 (eRASS1); Wikipedia "List of galaxy groups and clusters" (Bullet
Group).

## How they were created

Groups collapse first and smallest in the hierarchy — the bottom-up
rung that cold-dark-matter models predict: smallest halos collapse
first, then build clusters over the last ~10 Gyr. A slightly overdense patch pulls itself together, its galaxies
settling into a shared halo while the bigger filaments around it keep
feeding latecomers. Compact groups are chance closeness made brief:
HCG 92's median galaxy separation is only 28.2 kpc with crossing time
~0.0054 Hubble times — five galaxies in a tight knot cannot hold that configuration for a
Hubble time, so what we see is a merger in progress — the shock, the
starburst, the 0.6-Mpc HI tails are the creation event still running.
Fossil groups are the same process finished; proto-groups
(1.8 < z < 3) are it just starting. Sources: Kravtsov & Borgani 2012
(ARA&A 50:353, cluster formation); Hickson 1993 (Atlas of Compact
Groups); Diener et al. 2013.

## How they change over time

Left alone, a group eats itself into a fossil: dynamical friction drags
the middleweights into the center until one giant elliptical owns the
halo — the ≥2-magnitude gap plus X-ray halo that defines the class.
Left near a cluster, a group gets eaten instead — Virgo is doing
both right now, a dynamically young cluster still forming from Virgo A
(M87, ~10^14 solar masses, ten times the other subclumps) plus the M86 subclump plus Virgo B (M49) merging today, with the
N/S clouds, Virgo E and Coma I groups infalling and the W-group on
first infall being
tidally stripped in the outskirts (A&A 2026, aa59334-26). Ram-pressure
stripping is already at work on Virgo spirals (HI deficiency known
since Chamaraux et al. 1980; VERTICO CO depletion now), and the same
weather runs hotter in compact groups: NGC 7318B/7319 have lost
56–70% of their dust to the intragroup debris field. And at home, the Local Group's two
lobes keep falling toward each other for a merger billions of years out
— timing now uncertain (~50% in 10 Gyr), outcome (one giant elliptical,
"Milkomeda", gathering the remaining Local Group over ~150 Gyr) not.
JWST adds the latest frame: MIRI (July 2022 first images) resolves
dust-hidden shocks, tidal tails, and star formation across four
Quintet members, plus PAH bands confirming live intragroup birth. Sources:
Wikipedia "Virgo Cluster" (Gavazzi et al. 1999 3D structure; Boselli et
al. 2014 GUViCS); Wikipedia "Galaxy group"; Chamaraux et al. 1980;
Brown et al. 2021 (VERTICO); A&A 2026 (aa59334-26, W-group);
JWST MIRI Stephan's Quintet 2022-034.

## How it interacts with the others

Groups are the parcels clusters are built from: rich clusters grow by
accreting exactly these systems along the filaments (MACS J0717's
13-Mly filament feeding a four-cluster pile-up is the caught-live
analogue; simulations: up to ~45% of cluster galaxies arrive via
filaments), and Virgo's ongoing
assembly is the process caught live — N/S clouds, Virgo E, Coma I
infalling, W-group stripping, M87/M86/M49 subclumps merging. Inside the group, closeness is the
weather — tidal encounters strip dwarfs, feed central giants, salt the
intragroup medium early (bulk metals in by z~2, cf. `icm.md`), and in
compact groups drive shocks and starbursts that pre-process galaxies
before any cluster ever sees them. The Local Group shows the flip side:
too far from Virgo (~54 Mly/16.5 Mpc) to fall in — slowed ~10% by
Virgocentric flow but not bound — it evolves on its own track
while riding the same Local Sheet / Local Volume / Virgo Supercluster /
Laniakea flow toward Norma and Shapley. Sources:
Wikipedia "Virgo Cluster"; `clusters.md` (node accretion);
`icm.md` (early enrichment); `members.md` (cannibalism tracers).

## Pre-processing: transformed before arrival

Clusters get second-hand galaxies. Inside a group, slow tidal
encounters strip dwarfs, harass disks, and cut off fresh gas
(strangulation/starvation) long before any ICM wind blows — so many cluster
spirals arrive pre-quenched, their star formation already fading. GAMA
groups show the quenching excess directly; VERTICO (VLA CO in Virgo,
Brown et al. 2021, ApJS 257:21) shows molecular gas already depleted on
first infall; simulations count up to ~45% of cluster members arriving
via filaments and groups rather than pristine infall. The
backsplash population proves the commute: galaxies that plunged through
a cluster, survived, and now orbit outside it (out to ~2 virial radii),
still carrying the
stripping scars. Compact groups are the extreme kitchen: Stephan's
shock-heated intragroup gas (0.75 keV), stripped dust (NGC 7318B/7319
dust deficits 70%/56%, 98% of 7318B's IR beyond its stellar disk),
HI-free members with gas-only tidal debris, and intragroup starbursts
(SQ-A/SQ-B) show transformation running
at full blast in a system that will never reach clusterhood itself.
Paul et al. 2017 (MNRAS 471:2) formalize the boundary: aggregations
below ~8 x 10^13 solar masses behave as groups. Sources: Wikipedia
"Galaxy group" (pre-processing);
`members.md` (quenching timescales); Brown et al. 2021 (VERTICO);
Paul et al. 2017; Bitsakis et al. 2014 (A&A 565:A25, HCG dust deficits);
Jones et al. 2023 (A&A 670:A21, HI in HCGs).

## Key numbers

- Local Group: 134+ members within 1 Mpc; (2.47 +/- 0.15) x 10^12 solar
  masses; 5.11 Mpc (16.7 Mly) major axis; lobes ~800 kpc apart.
- Group vs cluster: ≤50 vs hundreds–thousands of members; 1–2 vs 1–5
  Mpc across; ~10^13 vs 10^14–10^15 solar masses; ~150 (Local Group ~49)
  vs ~800–1,000 km/s spreads. Group/cluster boundary ~8 x 10^13 solar
  masses (Paul et al. 2017).
- X-ray groups: ~half of nearby groups glow, inner 10–50% virial radius,
  50–500 kpc; Stephan's intragroup gas ~0.75 keV, log L_X ~42.16.
- Stephan's Quintet (HCG 92): 4 bound + 1 foreground (+NGC 7320C at group
  redshift); median separation 28.2 kpc; M/L ~44; shock ~6 million K at
  several million km/h; SQ-A ~1.45 solar masses/yr; 0.6-Mpc HI structure.
- Hickson catalog: 100 compact groups (HCG 1–100), typically ~4–5 members.
- Group catalogs: Garcia 485; 2MASS ~300 HDC + ~1,200 LDC; Yang SDSS
  ~300,000; GAMA ~14,000; eRASS1 12,247 groups/clusters (z=0.003–1.32).
- Fossil example NGC 6482: ~180 Mly away; fossil gap ≥2 mag + X-ray halo.
- Andromeda approach: ~110–123 km/s; merger odds now ~50% within
  10 Gyr, ~2% head-on in 4–5 Gyr.
