# Lifetime — from seeds to island universes

The dated spine of L3: how galaxy
clusters and groups grew from quantum ripples into the largest bound
objects in the universe — and what the accelerating expansion leaves
them. Consistent with the part docs (`clusters.md`, `groups.md`,
`icm.md`, `members.md`); every date carries a source. For the
universe-wide spine this hangs off, see the L02 companion
[dated stages of the L2 web](../../L02/documents/lifetime.md) and the
L01 background; for per-part stories see
[clusters](./clusters.md), [groups](./groups.md),
[ICM](./icm.md) and [members](./members.md).

## Baseline (Planck 2018 flat Lambda-CDM, used throughout)

- Age: 13.787 +/- 0.020 Gyr; Hubble constant H0 = 67.4 +/- 0.5 km/s/Mpc.
- Budget: ~4.9% ordinary matter / ~26.5% cold dark matter / ~68.5% dark energy.
- Seed spectrum tilt n_s = 0.965 +/- 0.004; reionization optical depth tau = 0.054 +/- 0.007.
- Baseline assumes constant Lambda (w = -1); evolving-dark-energy hints (DESI DR2, below 5-sigma) are noted under Fate, not adopted.

Sources: Planck Collaboration 2018 (VI) [S010]; ESA Planck CMB page [S001].

## The stages

**Seeds — the first 400,000 years.** Inflation stretches quantum flickers
into over/under-density ripples at one part in 100,000 (~30 µK on the
2.73 K microwave background, resolved by Planck to a few µK) — the seeds
of everything L3 holds. The ripple spectrum tilt (n_s = 0.965) and
amplitude measured in the microwave background are the initial
conditions for every later L3 halo. Matter–radiation equality at ~47,000 years frees
small-scale growth from radiation washout; recombination at ~380,000
years (~3000 K) makes the universe transparent and releases the earliest light we
see — the microwave background at 2.726 K today, ~400 photons per cm^3, with the
frozen sound-wave ruler (~150 Mpc / ~500 Mly preferred galaxy separation) still
visible in galaxy clustering. No L3 structures exist yet, only the seed
field whose overdense patches will become protoclusters. Sources: ESA Planck CMB page [S001]; Planck 2018 (VI) [S010]; NASA Webb early-universe page [S003].

**First fires — 100 Myr to 1 Gyr.** The first star-forming protogalaxies
(10^5–10^6 solar masses, tens of light-years) ignite 100–250 Myr after
the Bang (Cosmic Dawn ~50 Myr–1 Gyr); dark-matter filaments collapse
first, hydrogen clouds follow into stars. Theory expects the first
massive stars at 30–300 solar masses with few-Myr lives; their
ultraviolet starts ionizing bubbles that percolate through
reionization (~200 Myr–1.1 Gyr, midpoint z_re = 7.68, complete by
z = 5.3). Webb pushes the observed frontier to JADES-GS-z14-0 at
z = 14.32 and MoM-z14 at z = 14.44 (~280 Myr), with star clusters seen
at 460–600 Myr. The first groups assemble
inside the first billion years: small-scale analogues of protoclusters
whose member galaxies still sit in separate dark halos fusing into one
group halo (the Diener et al. 2013 definition — members are centrals
now, satellites later). zCOSMOS-deep finds 42 candidate proto-groups at
1.8 < z < 3; JWST now finds compact proto-groups to z ~ 5
(SCGG-z5 at z = 4.97, six H-alpha members in ~16 kpc). Sources: NASA Webb early universe [S003]; Diener et al. 2013 (ApJ 765:109) [S011]; SAPPHIRES SCGG-z5 2026 [S012]; Max Planck MPIA end of cosmic dawn.

**Protoclusters — redshifts 2–4 (universe 1.5–3 Gyr old).** Overdense
patches light up as protoclusters: the Spiderweb (MRC 1138-262) at
z≈2.2, light traveled 10+ Gyr, universe only ~3 Gyr old, already shows a
hot ICM detected by its Sunyaev-Zel'dovich shadow — ALMA shows it is in
the process of becoming a massive gravity-bound cluster — plus
supermassive-black-hole quenching at work (JWST massive galaxies).
Protocluster members are still mostly centrals in separate halos that
will merge by z = 0 (Diener definition); Hyperion at z = 2.45 shows a
supercluster mid-build at under 20% of the present age. The ICM gets its metals now —
peak star formation near z~2 salts the gas (supernova ejecta held
bound by the deep well), mixed by z~2–3, unchanged
since z~1 at ~1/3 solar (core-collapse plus 12–37% Type Ia; outskirts
uniform to 1.3 virial radii, enriched to ~0.1 solar already at z ~ 2;
IllustrisTNG: outskirts invariant for 10+ Gyr). Oldest surviving cluster ellipticals date their stars here
(done mostly by z~1–2; oldest at z ~ 1.3 already 3.5–4 Gyr old). Star-formation
and black-hole feedback run at maximum (cosmic noon, ~10x present rate).
Sources: ALMA Spiderweb SZ release [S004]; ESA Cosmic eras; McDonald et al. (ApJ 826:124, SPT-SZ metallicity) [S013]; Mantz et al. 2017 [S014]; A&A 2017 (aa28866-16).

**Assembly — redshift 1 to 0.5 (6–8 Gyr ago).** Groups merge into
clusters along the filaments; hierarchical assembly runs bottom-up with
the smallest halos collapsing first and building clusters over the last
~10 Gyr. BCG bulk mass is in place by z~1.5–2,
later growth by minor mergers and dynamical friction dragging satellites
in (rapid mergers during collapse giving way to minor mergers and gas
accretion; fossil groups are the same process finished in isolation).
Intracluster light builds over ~10 Gyr of mergers and harassment as the
receipt drawer. El Gordo at z=0.87 — universe half its
present age (6.2 Gyr old), light traveled 7 Gyr — is the most massive
cluster known at that epoch: ~2.1 x 10^15 solar masses in the 2021
wide-field HST weak-lensing re-analysis (Kim et al. 2021, ApJ 923:101;
early estimates ran to ~3 x 10^15), two subclusters
colliding at several million km/h, with 89 member redshifts (z = 0.870,
dispersion 1,321 +/- 106 km/s), integrated gas temperature
14.5 +/- 1.0 keV (6.6 keV cool core to 22 keV shocked gas), and a double
radio relic — a high-redshift Bullet analogue massive enough that some
analyses argue it strains vanilla structure-formation expectations (an
open cosmological question, not a settled verdict). Sources: NASA Hubble El Gordo asset [S005];
ESO eso1203 [S006]; Kim et al. 2021 [S015]; Menanteau et al. 2012 (ApJ 748:7) [S016]; MNRAS 491:3751 (BCG+ICL over 10 Gyr).

**Today — 13.787 ± 0.020 Gyr (Planck 2018).** Virgo still assembling
(M87 + M86 + M49 merging, groups infalling — N/S clouds, Virgo E, Coma I,
W-group on first infall being tidally stripped); Coma relaxed with its dual
BCG core (NGC 4874 + NGC 4889, 22,000+ globulars bridging the pair);
the Bullet (1E 0657-558 at z = 0.296, 3.7 Gly, subcluster exiting at
~4,500–4,700 km/s with Mach ~3 bow shock, 0.72 Mpc galaxy separation,
8-sigma lensing-mass vs gas offset — the direct dark-matter proof)
frozen mid-merger; SZ surveys cataloging ~10,000 clusters (ACT DR6 SZ
cluster catalogs, ACT-DES-HSC Collaboration 2026, maps 2008–2022 over
~19,000 sq deg, optically confirmed with redshifts plus legacy cosmology
and multiple-system subsamples on LAMBDA) with 1,000+
beyond redshift 1 — the largest SZ catalog to date, cross-matched to
eRASS1 X-ray (12,247 optically confirmed groups/clusters, z = 0.003–1.32).
Planck PSZ2 remains the deepest all-sky SZ list (1,653 detections, 1,203
confirmed; 1,334 validated in the 2024 UPCluster-SZ refresh). The Local Group falls toward Virgo's neighborhood
flow but stays its own group (slowed ~10% by Virgocentric flow, not
bound; 134+ members, 5.11 Mpc / 16.7 Mly tip to tip). Sources: Planck 2018 (A&A, arXiv
1807.06209) [S010]; Clowe et al. 2006 (ApJ) [S017]; Markevitch et al. (bow shock velocity) [S018];
ACT DR6 SZ (LAMBDA) [S019]; UPCluster-SZ 2024 [S020]; eROSITA DR1 [S021].

**Far future — freeze-out.** The ΛCDM expectation: dark energy
increasingly dominates (acceleration began ~9 Gyr in, ~5 Gyr ago;
matter–dark-energy equality near z ~ 0.3–0.4, scale factor a ~ 0.6),
expansion accelerates, and bound clusters finish
accreting — the last hierarchical growth freezes as unbound surroundings
recede. Linear growth freezes and no new large bound structures form
afterwards; bound clusters keep growing significantly to the present day
(roughly doubling before freeze-out) then evolve as island universes.
Clusters remain the largest bound objects while the space
between them grows: island universes, the Big Bang's evidence redshifting
out of their observers' reach (exponential approach to de Sitter; the
CMB fades and is eventually screened; horizon cutoff ~150 Gyr; external
galaxies unobservable ~2 Tyr even in gamma rays; star formation ends
10^12–10^14 yr). Laniakea — a flow basin, not a bound object —
disperses; Virgo grows unreachable; the Local Group merges (Milkomeda in
4–8 Gyr, now revised to only ~50% in 10 Gyr [S007]). Baseline assumes constant
Lambda (w = -1); DESI DR1–DR2 (2024–2025) evolving-dark-energy hints
(2.8–4.2 sigma with supernova combos, below 5-sigma discovery, driven by
low-z tracers) keep alternative fates open, not adopted. Sources: NASA dark-energy page [S008]; NASA universe-evolution graphic (lambda.gsfc) [S009]; Hoffman et al. 2007; DESI DR2 results guide (March 2025) [S022].

## How clusters assemble (the hierarchy in one place)

Clusters are the youngest, still-forming level of the cosmic web: small
groups form first, then merge and collapse into larger virialized systems
along the filaments, dark matter leading and baryons falling into its
wells. Matter flows voids to sheets to filaments to the dense clumps at
filament intersections. Simulations count up to ~45% of cluster galaxies
arriving via filaments (the rest as group members, backsplash, or
filament-processed; Kuchner et al. 2022, ThreeHundred). A caught-live
example: a 13-million-light-year filament streaming into MACS J0717 fed
a four-cluster pile-up (z = 0.54, core ~2 x 10^15 solar masses plus seven
substructures at 1.6–4.9 Mpc), mapped by Hubble in 2-D plus redshift
distances — and a 60-Mly dark-matter filament behind it mapped in 3-D by
lensing. Inside groups, pre-processing runs first: slow tidal encounters
strip dwarfs, harass disks, and cut off fresh gas long before any ICM
wind blows, so many cluster spirals arrive pre-quenched; infalling groups
already show a reduced star-forming fraction out to 5 x R200, even for
pairs and triplets (MNRAS 527:L19). Virgo is the process caught live
today. Detail lives in [clusters](./clusters.md#how-they-were-created),
[groups](./groups.md#pre-processing-transformed-before-arrival) and
[ICM](./icm.md); this timeline carries only the dates.

Sources: Kuchner et al. 2022 (arXiv 2111.11467) [S023]; NASA Hubble
cosmic-web mapping [S024]; ESA/Hubble heic1215 (MACS J0717 filament) [S025].

## What the gas and the galaxies were doing at each date

The ICM and the members run on the same clock as the stages above; this
section only aligns them, with detail in [ICM](./icm.md) and
[members](./members.md):

- Protocluster dates (z ~ 2–3): supernovae salt the ICM to ~1/3 solar
  (outskirts uniform, ~0.1 solar already at z ~ 2); oldest cluster
  ellipticals form their stars (done mostly by z ~ 1–2).
- Assembly dates (z ~ 1–0.5): BCG bulk mass in place by z ~ 1.5–2, then
  minor mergers; intracluster light builds over ~10 Gyr; relaxed clusters
  grow cool cores with a central iron peak inside 0.2 R500 while mergers
  homogenize the metals (Coma vs Perseus contrast).
- Today: two heaters share the work — central-black-hole jets plus
  merger sloshing. XRISM Resolve (~30x CCD energy resolution) made the
  motions measurable: Perseus velocity dispersion traces a V-shape
  (~200 km/s center, ~80 km/s dip, ~200 km/s outskirts to ~800,000 ly,
  black-hole stirring inside vs accretion flows outside); Centaurus core
  streams at 130–310 km/s in an oscillation pattern — first direct
  velocity evidence of sloshing holding off runaway cooling. Sloshing is
  now a measured motion, not a hypothesis; the two drivers dominate at
  different radii. The ICM is magnetized (few µG, ~10 µG in places) and
  merger-stirred systems light up Mpc radio halos/relics.
- Members throughout: infall quenches in 100 Myr (rapid) to a few Gyr;
  jellyfish tails mark ram-pressure stripping (ESO 137-001 at 1,900 km/s);
  intracluster light (up to ~10% of Virgo's stars) traces dark matter for JWST.

Sources: [ICM](./icm.md) (XRISM Collaboration 2025, 2026; Govoni 2015;
Rohr et al. 2024); [members](./members.md) (A&A 2017 aa28866-16; MNRAS
491:3751); JAXA XRISM Centaurus press 2025-02-13 [S026].

## L3 numbers at every stage

- Seeds: 1 in 100,000 ripples (n_s = 0.965); equality ~47,000 yr; transparency ~380,000 yr (~3000 K); BAO ruler ~150 Mpc frozen in.
- First stars: 100–250 Myr (30–300 solar-mass theory); Cosmic Dawn 50 Myr–1 Gyr; JADES-GS-z14-0 z = 14.32, MoM-z14 z = 14.44 (~280 Myr); reionization 200 Myr–1.1 Gyr (midpoint z_re = 7.68, complete z = 5.3); first groups within ~1 Gyr; 42 zCOSMOS proto-groups at 1.8 < z < 3; SCGG-z5 at z = 4.97.
- Spiderweb: z≈2.2, universe ~3 Gyr old; metals mixed by z~2–3 at ~1/3 solar (outskirts ~0.1 solar at z ~ 2); cosmic noon ~10x present SFR.
- El Gordo: z=0.87, 7 Gly light-travel, ~2.1 x 10^15 solar masses (early ~3 x 10^15); dispersion 1,321 km/s; gas 14.5 keV (6.6–22 keV); BCG bulk mass by z~1.5–2, ICL over ~10 Gyr.
- Bullet: 1E 0657-558 at z = 0.296, ~4,500–4,700 km/s, Mach ~3, 0.72 Mpc separation, 8-sigma offset, 3.7 Gly away.
- Today: 13.787 ± 0.020 Gyr, H0 = 67.4 +/- 0.5; ACT DR6 ~10,000 clusters (1,000+ at z > 1); PSZ2 1,653/1,203 (UPCluster-SZ 1,334); eRASS1 12,247 systems (z = 0.003–1.32); XRISM Perseus V-shape 200 → 80 → 200 km/s, Centaurus slosh 130–310 km/s.
- Fate: acceleration since ~9 Gyr in (z ~ 0.3–0.4); accretion freeze-out, island universes, CMB faded/screened; horizon cutoff ~150 Gyr; star formation ends 10^12–10^14 yr; Milkomeda now ~50% in 10 Gyr (~2% head-on in 4–5 Gyr), not certain.

Sources: pages named above; Planck 2018 (VI) for the baseline.

## Caveats

- Age 13.787 (Planck 2018) vs 13.772 (WMAP9) — dataset combos; local H0 ~70–73 tension shifts age priors by ~1% (4–5 sigma tension, unresolved).
- Recombination 370–380 kyr — start vs peak vs complete definitions; z 1089–1100 standard.
- El Gordo mass 2.1 vs ~3 x 10^15 — 2021 wide-field HST lensing revision vs early X-ray/dynamical/SZ scalings; merger-state systematics remain.
- El Gordo vs Lambda-CDM rarity — some analyses claim tension (open question, not a settled verdict).
- Far-future dates are order-of-magnitude (Adams-Laughlin), assume constant Lambda and often proton decay; DESI DR2 evolving-dark-energy hints (2.8–4.2 sigma, < 5 sigma) could revise them.
- Milkomeda merger now ~50% in 10 Gyr (Sawala et al. 2025 [S007], Gaia+HST, 100,000 Monte Carlo over 22 variables, LMC/M33 included), not the certain ~4.5 Gyr of 2012.
- Web/finder systematics propagate here: cluster counts depend on SZ vs X-ray selection (pressure vs density-squared); always cite catalog (PSZ2 vs ACT DR6 vs eRASS1).
