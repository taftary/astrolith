# Members — the galaxies that live here

Loop doc for Issue #220 (iteration 4, deepened iteration 11). Member galaxies are L3's visible
citizens: spirals falling in, ellipticals ruling the cores, one giant at
the very center growing by eating its neighbors — and the stripped
wreckage they leave behind. Sibling docs: `clusters.md`, `groups.md`,
`icm.md`; bridge in `previous-l2.md`, entry in `entry.md`, timeline in
`lifetime.md`.

## What they are and how they look

A cluster's population is sorted by address. Dense cores are ruled by
yellow ellipticals and S0s; the outskirts and the field run blue spirals
— the morphology-density relation, with Virgo's spirals strung along an
elongated prolate filament while its ellipticals huddle central, and
Coma's center overwhelmingly elliptical/S0 with spirals younger and
nearer the edges. At the geometric and kinematic bottom of the well,
coincident with the X-ray peak, squats the brightest cluster galaxy: a
giant elliptical (gE/D/cD with a diffuse envelope), the most massive
galaxy class in the universe. Between the members drifts the
intracluster light — up to ~10% of Virgo's stars as intergalactic red
giants, planetary nebulae, isolated star-forming patches, and stripped
globulars, orphan light JWST now reads as a luminous tracer of dark
matter.

## Example objects

- M87 (Virgo A): home giant — supergiant elliptical 53.5 million
  light-years out, 132,000-light-year isophotal diameter with an
  envelope reaching ~490,000 light-years, (2.4±0.6) x 10^12 solar masses
  inside 32 kpc, 50+ satellites, and a recently (last Gyr) absorbed
  medium spiral written in its chevron halo. It hosts ≥1,500 parsecs
  (4,900 light-years) of blue relativistic plasma jet plus the
  6.5-billion-solar-mass M87* — shadow imaged April 2017, published
  10 April 2019 (named Pōwehi), polarized ring 2021 showing the magnetic
  fields that power the jet. Sources: Wikipedia "Messier 87"; NASA M87*
  history page.
- NGC 4874 + NGC 4889 (Coma): the dual core — two supergiant
  ellipticals co-ruling Coma's center with 22,000+ globular clusters
  (mostly intracluster) bridging the pair, mapped as dark-matter
  tracers. Source: ESA/Hubble potw1849a.
- NGC 1275 / Perseus A: the fed giant — cD galaxy at the Perseus core
  (~240 Mly, ~295,000 light-years) with an 800-million-solar-mass
  center, Seyfert activity, and a filament network fed by the cooling
  gas its own black hole keeps reheated. Source: Wikipedia "NGC 1275".
- ESO 146-IG 005 (Abell 3827): the cannibal extreme — ~27±4 trillion
  solar masses by lensing of background galaxies at 2.7 and 5.1 Gly,
  merging nuclei in a core 1.44 Gly away. Source: Wikipedia "ESO 146-5";
  Gemini press release 2010.
- ESO 137-001 (Norma/Abell 3627): the jellyfish archetype — a barred
  spiral plunging at 1,900 km/s (~6.8M km/h) through 100-million-degree
  gas, trailing a 260,000-light-year X-ray and star-forming tail; Virgo
  shows the same in NGC 4402 (dust trailing, dust-free leading edge) and
  NGC 4522, Coma in the D100 tail. Sources: Wikipedia "ESO 137-001";
  Wikipedia "Jellyfish galaxy"; ESA/Hubble potw1904a; Chandra ESO 137
  album.

## How they were created

Members are immigrants with seniority rights. The oldest cluster
ellipticals formed their stars early (mostly done by redshift 1–2; the
oldest at redshift ~1.3 are already 3.5–4 Gyr old) inside the small
halos that merged into the cluster — most BCG stellar mass was in place
by redshift 1.5–2. Later growth is theft: dynamical friction plus tidal
stripping sink satellites to the center ("galactic cannibalism"), rapid
mergers during collapse giving way to minor mergers and gas accretion.
The intracluster light is the receipt drawer — diffuse stripped stars
built over ~10 Gyr of mergers and harassment. Sources: A&A 2017
(aa28866-16, oldest cluster ellipticals); MNRAS 491:3751 (BCG+ICL over
10 Gyr); Wikipedia "Interacting galaxy".

## How they change over time

Infall is a transformation machine. Ram pressure (Gunn & Gott 1972:
P_r ≈ ½ρv²) strips a plunging spiral's gas where the ICM wind beats its
gravity — Virgo and Coma infallers show the HI depletion, full quenching
in 100 Myr (rapid) to a few Gyr, while compressed molecular gas can
briefly flare star formation first (Virgo NGC 4330/4402/4522). The
stripped tails trail behind as jellyfish, extreme star formation in the
wake, and jellyfish host more active nuclei than chance — likely the
same compression feeding their black holes. The giants only grow:
minor mergers plus cooling drizzle, throttled by their own feedback.
Sources: Wikipedia "Ram pressure"; Wikipedia "Jellyfish galaxy";
NASA APOD 2018-08-25.

## How it interacts with the others

Members tie every L3 part together. They trace the dark halo (globular
swarms as mass mappers), salt the ICM (their supernovae own the 1/3-solar
metals in `icm.md`), feed the BCG (cannibalism in `clusters.md`), and
record the group past (morphology still remembers pre-processing in
`groups.md`). M87's scale anchors the level: one galaxy holding
trillions of solar masses, 15,000 globulars against the Milky Way's
150–200, a jet outshining its host in radio — the member as landmark.
Sources: pages named above; `clusters.md`, `groups.md`, `icm.md`.

## Orphan light and dark tracers

The members map what cannot be seen. Globular swarms — M87's 15,000,
Coma's 22,000+ bridging its BCG pair, mostly intracluster — trace the
dark halo's shape where starlight gives out. The intracluster light
itself (up to ~10% of Virgo's stars as red giants, planetary nebulae,
stripped globulars) is the fossil record of every merger, now readable
by JWST as luminous dark-matter tracing. And the stripped carry a final
surprise: jellyfish galaxies host more active nuclei than chance, the
same ram compression that kills star formation also feeding central
black holes. Sources: Wikipedia "Messier 87"; ESA/Hubble potw1849a;
Wikipedia "Jellyfish galaxy".

## Key numbers

- M87: 53.5 Mly away, 132,000-ly body, ~490,000-ly envelope, ~15,000 globulars.
- M87*: 6.5 billion solar masses; jet ≥4,900 light-years; gas disk to 25,000 AU at ~1,000 km/s.
- Coma globulars: 22,000+ bridging the BCG pair.
- Intracluster light: up to ~10% of Virgo's stars.
- Stripping: Virgo members to ~1,600 km/s peculiar (~5.8M km/h); ESO 137-001 at 1,900 km/s.
- Quenching: 100 Myr rapid to a few Gyr; BCG bulk mass in place by z~1.5–2.

Sources: pages named above.
