# Houses — homes, residential buildings, yards

One of six L13 parts: houses and residential buildings at 10–100 m.
The bridge from L12 lives in `previous-l12.md`; the dive lives in `entry.md`; the timeline in `lifetime.md`.
This document covers the rows: detached houses, manufactured-home towns, and subdivisions —
from the styles and roofs that read at street level to the frames, lots, and buried systems
that make them work.

A house here follows the Census Bureau's construction universe: single-family homes completed
and sold each year, tracked by the Survey of Construction for size, framing, lot, heating,
and price [S01]. Of the 1,005,000 single-family homes completed in 2025, the median carried
2,142 sq ft of floor area [S01]; of the 678,000 new single-family homes sold in 2025,
the median price was $417,400 and the average $523,800 [S01].

## How it looks

A house is living quarters for one or more families: walls that carry the floors and roof,
a roof overhead, windows and a door to the street. Common American forms read clearly from
the ground — the Ranch on one level with a low roof and open rectangular plan and little
ornament; Colonial symmetry with classical detail; Cape Cod, Craftsman, Tudor Revival,
Victorian gables; Mid-Century Modern flat planes. Roofs run asphalt shingle, wood, clay tile,
slate, or metal over gabled, flat, domed, or vaulted shapes; the first roofs were thatch,
branches, timber, brick, and stone [S10].

From above, closely packed neighborhoods read light grey against leafy suburbs dark green,
with drainage canals, golf courses, and lakes threaded between the subdivisions; by night
the street grid glows and the desert around it stays dark. The target view is Barefoot Bay,
Florida, captured 2016-10-22 by the Operational Land Imager on Landsat 8: with few trees
and closely packed residential neighborhoods, the town appears light grey next to the
leafier suburbs around it, and a single drainage canal connects the golf course on the
western side with a lake on the eastern side [S08].

Barefoot Bay is one of the largest manufactured-home communities in Florida: established
in 1969 in southern Brevard County, with a population of roughly 10,000 people drawn by
affordable homes and recreational offerings, plus easy access to fishing, boating, and
swimming on the Indian River and the Atlantic [S08]. Every winter more than a million
snowbirds — many of them retired — arrive from Canada and the northern states and lift
Florida's population by roughly 5 percent; the developed barrier island just east of
Barefoot Bay is itself an RV campground popular with snowbirds [S08].

Sources: Britannica house, ranch-house, and roof pages [S10]; NASA Earth Observatory Barefoot Bay page [S08].

## How it changes over time

Houses age in place: the median owner-occupied US home is 40 years old (2022 American
Community Survey), and about three-fifths of the owner-occupied stock was built before
1980, with roughly 35 percent built before 1970 [S02]. New construction added nearly
1.7 million units from 2020 to 2022 — only about 2 percent of the owner-occupied stock —
while homes built between 2010 and 2019 make up only around 9 percent [S02]. The shortfall
keeps older structures standing and feeds a growing remodeling market, as old houses gain
new amenities or repaired and replaced components [S02].

Styles march by era — Colonial 1600s–1700s, Victorian late 1800s, Craftsman early 1900s,
Cape Cod and Colonial Revival 1920s–40s, Ranch with the car and suburb 1950s–70s, larger
two-story suburban into the 2015 size peak, smaller again after [S06] [S10]. Change comes
by renovation and addition — extra bathrooms, heat pumps replacing furnaces, finished
basements — and by lot splits and infill that shrink lots to record lows [S04]. Whole
communities are built at once for one stage of life, like the 1969 manufactured-home town
of Barefoot Bay with its roughly 10,000 people, affordable homes, recreational offerings,
and canal [S08].

Sources: NAHB housing-stock age pages [S02]; NAHB home-size pages [S06]; NAHB lot-size pages [S04]; NASA Barefoot Bay page [S08].

## How it is created

The predominant method is light wood frame: wood framing accounted for 94 percent of all
completed US single-family homes in 2024 — about 959,000 houses, up 3 percent on the year
and a rebound from 93 percent in 2023 — with concrete-framed homes at 5 percent and steel
under half a percent [S03]. Non-wood framing concentrates in the South for resilience:
concrete frames made up 9 percent of Southern completions, and nearly 95 percent of all
steel-framed homes rose in the South [S03].

The sequence runs permit authorization, then start — authorization to start averages about
1.4 months — then framing, sheathing and roof, plumbing and electrical, finishes, and
sale [S07]. Building took less time in 2024 than in the two prior years as supply-chain
pressure eased: permit to completion averaged 9.1 months overall, with built-for-sale
fastest at 7.6 months, contractor-built near 12, built-for-rent near 12.5, and owner-built
slowest at 15.1 [S07]. In 2025 the pace quickened again, to 7.4 months built-for-sale and
14.3 owner-built [S07]. Heating splits by region — gas furnaces in the Northeast, heat
pumps across the South, where 63 percent of 2025 completions used a heat pump and only
36 percent a forced-air furnace [S01] — and nine in ten new homes built for sale connect
to public sewer, the rest to individual septic [S01].

Sources: NAHB wood-framing pages [S03]; NAHB build-time pages [S07]; Census new-housing highlights [S01].

## How it interacts with the others

Houses line the subdivided lots along the street grids: close to two-thirds (65 percent)
of new single-family detached spec homes sold in 2023 sit on lots under 9,000 sq ft —
less than a fifth of an acre — the highest share on record, up from 46 percent in 1999;
the share under 7,000 sq ft reached a record 40 percent, up from 28 percent in 1999 [S04].
The shift is recent: lots under a fifth of an acre hovered near 48 percent until 2011,
then climbed to 61 percent before the pandemic and kept rising on lot shortages and
affordability pressure [S04]. The median sold lot measured 8,543 sq ft in 2025, near
historic lows [S05]; the Pacific division runs smallest, half its lots under 0.14 acres.
Custom homes on their owner's land play a different game, with a median of a full acre [S04].

Many answer to homeowners associations — 77 percent of attached homes in the Northeast
and 96 percent in the West sit in one [S01] — and multifamily buildings cluster 50 or more
units to a block: 73 percent of Northeastern completions stand in buildings of 50 units
or more [S01]. Between the detached house and the tower stands the missing middle —
duplexes, townhouses, and low-rise apartments that take house-scale lots at block-scale
density (see `blocks.md` for the tower end of that ladder, including offices converting
into apartments). Townhouses keep gaining ground as the affordable single-family option,
holding a record 17 percent of the single-family market against 10 percent in 2009 [S06].

Every house plugs into the same buried and wired systems — public water and sewer or
septic, paved streets, drainage canals, power lines — laid down over centuries: bored-out
logs as pipe conduits, Boston's 1652 gravity-fed conduit, Philadelphia's 1804 switch to
cast iron, Chicago's 1850s citywide sewers, rural lines under the 1936 Electrification Act
to 90 percent electrified by 1950 and universal by 1960 [S09]. Electric heating rose from
about 1 percent of households in 1950 to about 40 percent in 2020 [S09]. Lawns, streets,
and septic fields shed nutrients and sediment, so the runoff of a suburb can cloud a river
and feed algae blooms downstream: around Barefoot Bay, inputs from septic tanks, farms,
lawns, and city streets cloud the Indian River and have fed large blooms, while the line
of islands offshore was dredged in the 1950s to make the Atlantic Intracoastal Waterway [S08].

Sources: NAHB lot-size pages [S04] [S05]; Census new-housing highlights [S01]; NAHB buyer-preference pages [S06]; HUD history of housing systems [S09]; NASA Barefoot Bay page [S08].

## Typical size and mass

A completed new single-family home carries a median 2,142 sq ft of floor area (about 199 m2)
in 2025; the median sold home the same year carries 2,194 sq ft at a median price
of $417,400 [S01]. Sizes have been falling since the 2015 peak near 2,466 sq ft as
affordability worsened — the 2024 median of 2,150 sq ft was the lowest in 15 years —
with only a low-rate interlude upward in 2021; early-2025 starts data still reads below
2,200 sq ft [S06]. A two-story 2,100 sq ft home covers roughly a 1,050 sq ft footprint,
one to two floors, about 5–10 m tall. New multifamily units run a median 1,000 sq ft for
rent and 1,170 for sale, most in buildings of three floors or more — 7 percent in one-
to two-floor buildings, 32 percent in three-floor, 61 percent in four or more [S01].
Spec lots run under a fifth of an acre; custom owner-held land has a median of a full acre [S04].

Sources: Census new-housing highlights [S01]; NAHB home-size pages [S06]; NAHB lot-size pages [S04].

## Example objects

- Fallingwater, Mill Run, Pennsylvania — Frank Lloyd Wright's 1935 weekend house for the Kaufmann family over the Bear Run waterfall: three stories, 2,885 sq ft indoors plus 2,445 of terraces in a 5,330-sq-ft main house, building and site as one composition [S13].
- Biltmore House, Asheville, North Carolina — Richard Morris Hunt's French Renaissance chateau for George Vanderbilt, opened 1895 after six years of construction: 250 rooms and about 175,000 sq ft with 35 bedrooms, 43 bathrooms, and 65 fireplaces, the largest privately owned house in the US [S11].
- Monticello, Charlottesville, Virginia — Jefferson's Neoclassical home, built and remade from 1768 to a largely complete second house by 1809: 43 rooms in all (33 in the house proper, four in the pavilions, six under the South Terrace) with about 11,000 sq ft of living area under the first dome on an American home [S12].

Sources: Fallingwater facts and foundation pages [S13]; Biltmore numbers pages [S11]; Monticello house FAQ [S12].
