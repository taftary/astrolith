# Transport buildings — stations, terminals, garages

This page is the L13 part doc for transport buildings and
large vehicles at 10–400 m: rail stations, airport
terminals, parking structures, plus ships and aircraft as
objects — the scale where one terminal with its aprons, or
one vessel with its berth, fills the view. The complexes
live in L12 (`ports-airports.md`); the networks in L12
(`networks.md`); power and light in L14 (`light-power.md`
via L14); the dated story in `lifetime.md`.

From 100 m up, transport buildings are roofs over flow:
train sheds spanning platforms, terminal piers fingering
into aprons, garage helixes stacked with cars, dry-dock
hulls blocked and shored. Vehicles read as cargo with
engines — a ship longer than its pier is wide, an aircraft
wider than its gate. Everything here is sized by turning
circles and stopping distances, not by people.

## Stations — the train's rooms

Grand halls with trainsheds: arched roofs spanning a
dozen platforms, concourses as indoor plazas, clocks as
meeting points — cathedrals of departure, many now
shopping arcades with trains attached. Commuter sheds
differ: open canopies, parking lakes, bus loops timed to
the timetable. Freight has no passengers: intermodal pads,
hump towers, crew rooms — the yard's office furniture.
Stations anchor downtowns (`commercial.md`, `civic.md`)
and seed suburbs at every stop on the line (L12
`towns.md`); abandoned ones become markets, museums, and
the L14 Union Terminal image's promise.

## Terminals and garages — the air's rooms

Airport terminals finger piers between taxi lanes: hold
rooms at each gate, jet bridges flexing to parked doors,
baggage rivers underneath, control towers overseeing —
the building as an adapter between street grid and sky
grid (see L12 `ports-airports.md`). Parking structures
stack the waiting cars: helix ramps, flat plates,
clearances by mirror-width, airports ringed by them,
downtowns honeycombed — the most honest architecture, form
following the turning car. Surface lots differ only in
going flat: acres striped, lit, and drained, heat islands
in summer, snow farms in winter.

## Ships and aircraft — vehicles as objects

**Ships**: container vessels hundreds of meters long with
deck stacks ten high, tankers low and piped, cruise ships
white with balcony grids, ferries blunt and quick —
wakes, anchor scours, and dredge islands marking their
water in L13 `industrial.md:28-29,110-111`. Neo-Panamax
locks set the sizes; harbors dredge to fit (L12
`ports-airports.md`).

**Aircraft**: fuselages tens of meters, wingspans wider
than gates, tails over hangar doors — parked rows angled
by blast, boneyards gridded in desert sun. Trains run
kilometers in American freight, meters per car in
passenger sets — the yard's long sentences.

## How it interacts with the others

Houses ride (`houses.md`): station suburbs, airport noise
lines, garage apartments over old lots. Blocks stack
(`blocks.md`): air rights over yards and tracks, towers on
old depots. Commerce feeds (`commercial.md`): concourse
retail, terminal food courts, garage ground floors. Civic
keeps (`civic.md`): Union Terminals as museums, clocks as
landmarks. Industry builds (`industrial.md`): shipyards,
hangars, rolling stock works. Stadiums share
(`stadiums.md:91`): event crowds on transit timetables,
parking swapped by season. Sacred quiets (`religious.md`):
chapels in terminals, bells competing with announcements.
Green softens (`green.md`): station plazas, terminal
lawns, garage roofs planted. Bridges deliver
(`structures.md`): spans ending at quays and station
throats.

## Key numbers

- Stations: sheds spanning a dozen platforms; concourses
as plazas; clocks as meeting points.
- Terminals: piers between taxi lanes; bridges flexing to
doors; towers overseeing.
- Garages: helixes by mirror-width; lots striped by the
acre; snow farmed in winter.
- Ships hundreds of meters; aircraft tens; freight trains
kilometers.
- Old depots become museums, markets, homes.
- L13 frame: 10–400 m — one terminal or vessel per view.
