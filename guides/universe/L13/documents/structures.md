# Structures — bridges, dams, towers

This page is the L13 part doc for non-building structures
at 10–100 m spans and heights: bridges, dam walls, towers,
masts, turbines, and lighthouses — the scale where one
structure with its approaches fills the view. Buildings
they serve live across this level (`houses.md`,
`blocks.md`, `civic.md`, `industrial.md`); the crossings
in network context in L12 (`networks.md:150-151`); dam
reservoirs in L12 (`extraction-energy.md`); stadium roofs
in `stadiums.md`.

From 100 m up, structures are lines and points doing what
buildings cannot: a deck threading towers across water, a
curved wall holding back a lake, a needle over the grid, a
row of white blades turning on a ridge, a striped tower
winking at ships. No floors, no tenants — just span,
height, and hold.

## Bridges — the spans

**Suspension** hangs the deck from cables between towers:
Golden Gate (1,280 m main span, 1937 — longest in the world
until 1964), Verrazzano-Narrows (1,298 m, 1964), Akashi
Kaikyo (1,991 m, 1998), Çanakkale 1915 (2,023 m, 2022 —
first past two kilometers). Orange, grey, pearl — paint as
maintenance made visible, recoated end to end forever.

**Cable-stayed** fans straight cables from tower to deck —
stiffer, younger, the default for 200–1,000 m spans.
**Arch** pushes to its abutments — stone Romans, steel
Sydneys; **truss** triangulates iron across rail valleys;
**beam and box girder** carry the ordinary highway miles;
**causeways** hop barrier islands on piles (`coasts.md` at
L12). Tunnels continue where bridges yield (I-90's portals
in L12 `networks.md`). Lifetimes: paint 25 years, decks
50, cables a century with dehumidification — bridges are
maintained, not finished.

Sources: NASA Akashi Kaikyo photojournal (1991 m span);
suspension-span records.

## Dams — the walls

The wall is the structure; the lake belongs at L12
(`extraction-energy.md`). Gravity walls (Three Gorges'
181 m concrete face), arch walls curving into canyon
shoulders (Hoover), embankments piled long and low —
spillways for the flood that must never overtop, intakes
and penstocks feeding turbines at the toe, fish ladders
where salmon still run. From above: the white curve, the
blue stair behind, the braided riverbed below starved of
mud (`coasts.md` at L12). Removal is the new construction:
Northwest dams breached for salmon, silt released in
controlled floods.

Sources: USGS Three Gorges pages; in-repo L12
`extraction-energy.md`.

## Towers, masts, turbines, lights

**Towers** mark: Eiffel's iron lattice, broadcast masts
guyed over fields, church spires and minarets in
`religious.md`, stadium pylons in `stadiums.md`.
**Wind turbines** harvest in rows on ridgelines and passes
(Tehachapi's thousands, Alta's gigawatt scale in L12
`extraction-energy.md`): 100-m towers, blades sweeping
football fields of air, access roads threading between,
flicker lines drawing neighbor relations. **Lighthouses**
stripe cliffs and capes, lenses flashing signatures
ships read like names; water towers dome over flat towns,
each painted with the town's name — the smallest towers,
the most personal. **Chimneys and cooling towers** vent
the works of `industrial.md`: hyperboloids white against
grey works, plumes marking wind for miles.

## How it interacts with the others

Houses pass under and over (`houses.md`): bridge shadows,
turbine flicker, dam sirens downstream. Blocks anchor
(`blocks.md`): tower foundations in the grid, curtain
walls reflecting the span. Commerce clusters
(`commercial.md`): toll plazas, viewpoint pullouts, dam
visitor centers. Civic shares (`civic.md`): lighthouses as
monuments, bridges as seals on flags. Industry feeds
(`industrial.md`): steel, cable, concrete by the barge-
load; chimneys venting. Stadiums borrow the engineering
(`stadiums.md`): suspension-wheel roofs from bridge math,
compression rings from dam curves. Towns arrive by them
(L12 `towns.md`, `networks.md`): the bridge that made the
suburb, the causeway that made the beach town. Green
dodges (`green.md`): parks under spans, trails on
towpaths.

Target visual: a dedicated span frame (suspension deck or
dam curve, public-domain aerial) is proposed for a future
pass. No new raster file is added here.

## Key numbers

- Suspension spans: Golden Gate 1,280 m (1937); Akashi
Kaikyo 1,991 m (1998); Çanakkale 2,023 m (2022).
- Three Gorges wall: 181 m; Grand Coulee 6,800 MW behind
its face.
- Turbines ~100 m towers; blades sweeping field-sized air.
- Paint 25 years, decks 50, cables a century.
- Removal is construction too: breached dams, freed silt.
- L13 frame: 10–100 m spans and heights — one structure
with approaches per view.

Sources: NASA photojournal; USGS; in-repo pages named above.

## Example objects

Golden Gate is the icon standard: 1,280 m orange — paint
as maintenance.

Akashi Kaikyo is the span standard: 1,991 m — cables past
a mile of air.

Three Gorges is the wall standard: 181 m of concrete —
the lake behind, the river starved below.

ridge — current from air.
