# Wet rooms — bathrooms and laundry

This page is the L14 part doc for wet rooms: bathrooms,
toilets, tubs, showers, and laundries at 1–10 m — the
scale where fixtures, drains, and vents fill the view.
Wet walls and egress live in `shell.md:39,109`;
circulation in `layout.md:125`; plumbing's arrival in
`lifetime.md:103` and L13 `houses.md:56`; the dated story
in `lifetime.md`.

A bathroom is the smallest engineered room: water in under
pressure, water out by gravity, air out by fan, all inside
a waterproof box a few meters square. Every fixture answers
the body — seated, standing, reclining — and every pipe
answers gravity, slope by slope, to the stack and the
sewer.

## The fixtures

**Toilet** — the siphon jet or washdown bowl, tank or
valve-fed, clearing in one flush what five-plus gallons
did in grandfather's day: flush volumes fell several-fold
since the 1990s efficiency rules, with dual buttons for
light and full work. Wall-hung bowls free the floor;
comfort height seats the stiff-kneed; bidets wash where
paper wiped.

**Tub and shower** — the soak versus the spray: alcove
tubs 5 ft to the wall, freestanding sculptures, showers
from curtained corners to doorless wet rooms graded to a
linear drain. Thermostatic valves hold temperature against
toilet-flush theft; grab bars, benches, and hand showers
age the room in place.

**Sink** — pedestal, wall-hung, vessel, or vanity-run:
splash radius setting mirror, light, and outlet heights;
faucets metering public washrooms, touchless where germs
rule.

**Laundry** — washer and dryer paired 27 inches wide each
(or stacked in closets): top-load agitators versus
front-load tumblers sipping water, gas or 240-volt
electric heat, vents to daylight that must never be
plastic. The laundry migrates — basement to main floor to
bedroom wing — chasing the clothes it serves.

## Drains, vents, water

What goes down must breathe: every trap holds a water seal
against sewer gas, every stack vents through the roof so
flushing one fixture never siphons another, slopes run a
quarter inch per foot so solids float to the main. Supply
runs opposite — pressure up and over, manifolds homing
each fixture, shutoffs at every stop. Wet walls stack
back-to-back between baths and kitchens to share the
stack; laundry joins where its standpipe reaches. Leaks,
not floods, are the enemy: pans under washers, membranes
under tile, access panels where valves hide.

## Air and water use

Fans size to the room — volume changed eight times an
hour, ducted out (never into attics), timered past the
shower's steam; humidity sensors where forgetfulness
reigns. Water budgets: showers and toilets the house's two
thirsts, laundry third; low flows, recirculation loops,
and rain-fed greywater the savings. Mold is the audit:
ventilate, squeegee glass, caulk the corners yearly.

## Key numbers

- Bathrooms: meters square, every inch plumbed; fixtures
by posture — seated, standing, reclining.
- Flush volumes fell several-fold since the 1990s; dual
buttons split the work.
- Traps seal, stacks vent, slopes quarter inch per foot.
- Fans change room air ~8x/hour, ducted to daylight.
- Laundry pairs 27 in each; vents never plastic; pans
underneath.
- L14 frame: 1–10 m — one wet room with stack and vent
per view.

## Example objects

A 5-ft bath is the standard: tub, toilet, sink in one wet
wall — the minimum engineered room.

A doorless shower is the accessible standard: graded
floor, linear drain — aging in place by design.

A stacked laundry is the closet standard: 27-inch pair
vertical — the migrating room condensed.

A vent stack is the hidden standard: traps sealed, roof
flashed — the physics behind the tile.
