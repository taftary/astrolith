# Orbital rooms — living in microgravity

This page is the L14 part doc for spacecraft rooms: the
Destiny laboratory and its kin — the scale where racks,
sleep stations, and foot loops fill the view. The Destiny
anchor frame lives in `entry.md:26-29,84`; laboratory
modules in `README.md:17`; the ride upstairs in
`previous-l13.md:86-88`; the dated arc in `lifetime.md`.

A spacecraft room is a room with the floor removed: no up,
no down, no dust settling, no convection stirring — every
assumption in `shell.md` through `layout.md` renegotiated.
Walls become workbenches on all six faces; sleep stands
upright in bags tied to walls; crumbs are hazards and
water flies in balls. Small, loud, and busy — the most
engineered rooms humans inhabit.

## Destiny — the anchor room

The Destiny laboratory — the L14 anchor image — is a
bus-sized cylinder worked wall-to-wall: International
Standard Payload Racks in rows (each a removable
phone-booth laboratory), end cones docking Node modules,
a nadir window watching Earth slide past at 400 km
altitude inside the thermosphere (see L10
`atmosphere.md:78-80` — the station flies through auroral
glow). Crew translate hand over hand on rails, feet in
loops when torque is needed, tools velcroed to every
surface. Noise never stops — fans, pumps, exercise machines
— so earplugs sleep beside toothbrushes. Six crew share
volume smaller than a studio flat, scheduled to the
quarter hour.

## Living without up

Sleep: vertical bags cinched to walls, arms folded (they
float otherwise), airflow mandatory — exhaled carbon
dioxide pools around faces without convection. Food:
rehydrated pouches, tortillas instead of bread (no
crumbs), espresso drunk from capillary cups. Hygiene:
rinseless washcloths, suction toilets with leg restraints
and airflow entrainment, hair washed with no-rinse
shampoo. Exercise: two hours daily against resistance and
treadmill bungees — without it, bone and muscle melt away
in months. Windows: Cupola's seven panes facing down,
Earth-watching as duty and therapy.

## Racks, loops, and velcro — the fittings

Racks slide out for surgery on orbit: EXPRESS racks with
standard power, cooling, and data; stowage racks of
labeled bags tracked by barcode; the airlock as mudroom
for spacewalks. Foot loops and handrails everywhere torque
is applied; tethers on everything that floats; trash
compacted and burned in departing cargo ships. Fire
behaves worst here: flames spherical in still air,
detectors sniffing before eyes see — suppression fast and
total. Radiation shelters in shielded corners for solar
storms (see L10 `magnetosphere.md` for the storms
themselves).

## How it interacts with the others

Shell pressurizes (`shell.md`): micrometeoroid shielding,
seals, hatches — the wall is a spacesuit. Furniture
straps (`furniture.md`): sleep bags, thigh restraints,
lap desks velcroed down. Storage tracks (`storage.md`):
every bag barcoded, every gram manifested — mass is money
at $10,000+ per kilogram launched. Light and power rule
(`light-power.md`): 120-volt station bus, LED day cycles
faked for circadian health, radiators glowing infrared
outside. Textiles quiet (`textiles-decor.md`): acoustic
blankets, sleeping bag liners, clothing worn a week.
Layout schedules (`layout.md`): quarter-hour timelines,
translation paths kept clear, exercise as furniture.
Wet rooms economize (`wet-rooms.md`): suction, airflow,
no-rinse everything. Stairs vanish (`stairs-thresholds.md`):
hatches and rails replace steps. Occupants adapt
(`occupants.md`): puffy faces, bird legs, space
anemia — bodies negotiating weightlessness. The station
orbits inside the aurora layer (L10 `atmosphere.md`,
`magnetosphere.md`).
