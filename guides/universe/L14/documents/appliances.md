# Appliances — the machines in the room

This page is the L14 part doc for appliances and devices
at 1–10 m: fridge, range, dishwasher, washer-dryer, and
screens — the scale where one machine with its clearances,
plugs, and vents fills the view. Triangle points live in
`storage.md:109`; built-in walls in `layout.md:125`; desks
and electronics in `furniture.md:20-23`; sockets and
registers in `light-power.md`; the dated arrival in
`lifetime.md`.

A room's machines are its organs: the cold box, the hot
box, the water boxes, the screens — each with a standard
width (30-inch modules rule kitchens), a plug or pipe, a
vent or drain, and a lifespan measured in teens of years.
Plan the room around the machines that cannot move, and
furnish around the ones that can.

## The kitchen machines

**Fridge** — the cold box: top-freezer workhorses,
French-door wide shelves, counter-depth flush faces;
compressors humming at the back, coils shedding heat into
the room air conditioning must remove. Doors need swing
inches; ice makers need water lines with shutoffs; levels
matter or doors drift open.

**Range** — the hot box: gas flames or induction fields,
30-inch slides between counters, hoods above sized to the
burners and ducted out (never into attics, like bath fans
in `wet-rooms.md`). Ovens self-clean by pyrolytic fire;
microwaves hang above or drawer below. The range is the
kitchen's hearth and its biggest electric load beside the
dryer.

**Dishwasher** — the hidden water box: 24-inch front under
the counter beside the sink, racks for a day's dishes,
heat-dry steam fogging cold kitchens, filters needing
monthly rinses. Runs at night on timers where power prices
it.

**Small machines** — toasters, kettles, mixers, coffee:
counter colonies on their own circuits, cords kept from
sink splash, the clutter `storage.md` was invented to
swallow.

## The laundry machines

Washer and dryer detailed in `wet-rooms.md`; here as
loads: washers sipping water front-load or gulping
top-load, dryers burning gas or 240-volt electric — among
the house's hungriest circuits with range and water
heater. Vibration walks washers across floors (lock the
feet, pan underneath); lint kills dryers by fire, cleaned
per load at the trap and yearly at the duct. Heat-pump
dryers sip where venting cannot reach.

## Screens and devices

TVs thin on walls (studs or blocking — screws in bare
drywall fail, see `shell.md`), soundbars below, consoles
and routers in ventilated cubbies; desktops with monitor
arms at `furniture.md` desks; chargers colonizing outlets
by the bed and the couch. Standby loads sip all night —
the vampire draw power strips switch off. E-waste closes
the loop: teens of years, then recycler, never trash for
screens with lead and mercury.

## Power, heat, and lifespan

Big heat means big wire: dryers, ranges, and water heaters
on dedicated 240-volt runs; fridges and washers on
ordinary 120 with surge protection; disposals and
dishwashers sharing switched legs. Heat goes somewhere:
fridge coils warm kitchens, dryers vent out, ranges need
makeup air in tight houses. Lifespans run teens of years —
fridges longest, dishwashers shortest, screens obsolete
before they die. Buy for the door widths of the house:
no fridge wider than its hallway arrives.

## How it interacts with the others

Shell holds (`shell.md`): studs for wall mounts, blocking
for grab bars and TVs, egress past humming boxes. Furniture
serves (`furniture.md`): desks for screens, stools for
counters, beds with charging outlets. Storage swallows
(`storage.md`): small machines in appliance garages, work
triangle weighting sink-stove-fridge. Light and power feed
(`light-power.md`): dedicated runs, GFCI by water, LEDs
over benches. Textiles soften (`textiles-decor.md`): rugs
underfoot at ranges and sinks. Layout triangles
(`layout.md`): sink-stove-fridge steps minimized, laundry
migrating to bedrooms. Wet rooms plumb (`wet-rooms.md`):
shared stacks, shared vents. Occupants reach
(`occupants.md`): controls at standing and seated heights.
Stairs carry (`stairs-thresholds.md`): every machine
arrived up them — measure first.

## Key numbers

- Kitchen modules ~30 in; dishwashers 24 in; laundry 27 in
each.
- Big loads: dryer, range, water heater on 240 V; rest on
120 with protection.
- Lifespans in teens of years; screens obsolete first.
- Fridge doors need swing; ranges need hoods ducted out;
dryers need metal ducts cleaned yearly.
- No machine wider than its hallway arrives.
- L14 frame: 1–10 m — one machine with clearances per view.

## Example objects

A French-door fridge is the cold standard: swing, line,
level — humming for teens of years.

A 30-inch range is the hearth standard: flames or fields,
hood above — the kitchen's hot box.

A 24-inch dishwasher is the hidden standard: racks by
night — water boxed under counters.

A front-load pair is the sip standard: less water, 240-V
heat — laundry migrating upstairs.

A wall TV is the screen standard: studs or blocking —
thin glass on honest structure.
