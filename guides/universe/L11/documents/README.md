# L11 — Regions of a planet

L11 = L11 (ladder row L11, Regions of a planet, 10^5–10^6 m). This
folder is the L11 visual and lifetime reference (Issue #228): what
its parts look like, how they were created, how they changed through
time, and how they interact — each page with target-visual images
and named sources. The bridge from the previous level and the entry
transition come first; the dated timeline runs through everything.
L11 opens the beyond-MVP tail: many regions per planet, then cities below.
Start at the bridge, dive through entry, pick any part, then confirm
dates in the lifetime — and continue down to
[L12](../../L12/documents/README.md) for cities per region.

## Scope and scale

L11 covers regions of one planet: 100–1,000 km patches in the
10^5–10^6 m range around whole ranges, basins, coastlines, and
country-scale areas — about 0.08 of the 12,756 km planet width at
1,000 km, about 0.008 at 100 km. It sits between the
[L10 — Planets and moons](../../L10/documents/README.md) above and the
[L12 — Cities and landscapes](../../L12/documents/README.md) below,
under the [Universe](../../documents/README.md#levels) ladder.
Frozen scale rules live in `docs/universes/ladder.md`; this folder
holds the descriptive reference only. The worked example is Earth
regions; other worlds appear only as comparative notes in part docs.

Read order: bridge ([previous-l10.md](./previous-l10.md)) first, then
the entry transition ([entry.md](./entry.md)), then any part below,
then the dated spine ([lifetime.md](./lifetime.md)) for dates behind
each claim.

flat wears and fills it, blue cuts and floods its edge, flow threads
and drains it, fire rebuilds it, shaking revises it — the dated version runs through
[lifetime.md](./lifetime.md).

## Figures at a glance

Canonical detail lives in the linked pages; this index carries only
the navigation-level numbers: L11 span 10^5–10^6 m (regions 100–1,000 km
across); planet behind 12,756 km across with oceans over 71 percent
and mean depth about 3.6 km; air 78 percent nitrogen, 21 percent
oxygen; Moon 3,474 km across at 384,400 km (30 Earths, 1.3
light-seconds); drift cm per year (Atlantic about 2.5 cm per year,
Australia north about 7 cm per year); crust oceanic 6–7 km,
continental 30–50 km (mean about 41 km, to 70 km under ranges);
Everest 8,848.86 m, Andes 8,900 km long, Great Plains 2,800 km long,
Sahara about 9 million km2, Amazon basin about 7 million km2;
L10-to-L11 leg is short (one to two decades, quiet magnification;
open 0.14 rad, close 0.10 rad, preview from 0.02 rad) —
see the [L10 bridge](./previous-l10.md#key-numbers), the [entry
numbers](./entry.md#key-numbers), and the dated
[timeline](./lifetime.md#key-numbers-timeline).

## How this level differs from L10

[L10](../../L10/documents/README.md) frames one planet with its moons — a
whole disk with interior engine, blue-marble face, thin air blanket,
cratered companion, and magnetic shield with its auroras. Here one
patch of that face fills the view: the counted planetary order falls
back into surroundings (oceans as coasts and bays, air as sky and
weather, Moon as tides) while continents, ranges, plains, seas, and
rivers take center as the base, the lift, the flat, the blue, and the
flow — so new continent, range, plain, coast, and river results enter
through the part docs, not this index.
