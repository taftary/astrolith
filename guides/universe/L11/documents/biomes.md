# Biomes — the living zones

This page is the L11 part doc for biomes: rainforest, savanna,
desert, temperate lands, boreal forest, and tundra at
100–1,000 km — the scale where one frame holds a whole
ecological zone with its transitions. Grasslands sit in
`plains.md:15-19`; deserts in `plains.md:22` and
`weather.md`; ice in `ice.md`; forests at city scale in the
planned L12 pages; farmland in L12 `farmland.md`; the dated
story in `lifetime.md`.

From orbit, life is color with latitude. Dark green belts
straddle the equator, paling to patchy savanna, then tan
desert bands near 30 degrees, then the mixed greens of
temperate lands, then the unbroken dark ring of boreal
forest, then tundra's brown-green felt, then white. The
boundaries wander — ecotones hundreds of kilometers wide —
and they are moving now: forests march north, tundra
greens, deserts breathe with the rains. Climate draws the
zones; life colors them; change redraws them in real time.

## The tropical belt — rainforest and savanna

**Rainforest** grows where rain never stops: the Amazon
basin (~7 million square km), the Congo, Southeast Asia's
archipelagos. From 1,000 km up, unbroken dark green with
silver river threads (`rivers.md`) and towering daily cloud
(`weather.md`) — the most species per square kilometer on
Earth, and a carbon store whose clearing is tracked
satellite by satellite. Rainforest soils are famously poor:
the wealth stands in the trees, not the ground.

**Savanna** rings the forests where rain comes in seasons:
grass with scattered trees, the Serengeti's migrations, the
Brazilian cerrado, the Deccan fringe mixing grass with
trees (`plains.md:18`). Fire yearly, green with rains,
gold with drought — the patchy transition read from orbit
as texture between forest dark and desert tan.

## The dry belts — deserts at regional scale

Near 30 degrees north and south the Hadley cell sinks (see
L10 `clouds-weather.md`), and the ground answers in tan:
the Sahara (~9 million square km), Arabian, Kalahari,
Australian, Gobi, Atacama. From orbit, dune stripes, rock
pavement, and salt pans; playas that fill with lakes and
dry to salt (`plains.md:21`, `lakes.md`); desert blooms
after rare rains (NASA's 2026 Murchison report). The Sahel
at the Sahara's southern edge is the transition made
visible — farm meeting desert, greening and browning with
decades. Deserts grow and shrink with climate, and the
close-up of dunes and playas waits at L12.

## The temperate middle — forest, grassland, Mediterranean

Between the dry belts and the boreal ring, four seasons
sort the land: deciduous forests flushing green in spring
and firing red in fall; grasslands — the Great Plains
(2,800 km), steppes, pampas — waving with bison, wheat, and
storms; Mediterranean rims with wet winters, dry summers,
and fire-adapted scrub. Almost all of it farmed now: the
survey grids and pivots of L12 `farmland.md` overlie the
natural zones, and telling them apart from orbit is the
first skill of the L11-to-L12 dive (`entry.md`).

## The boreal ring — the largest biome

The taiga encircles the Arctic between ~50 and 65°N across
North America, Europe, and Asia: spruce, pine, fir, and
birch in even-aged stands reborn from stand-replacing fire
and insects — 16.6–20 million square kilometers, largest
terrestrial biome, ~17% of land, holding about a third of
terrestrial carbon in trees, moss, and peat over permafrost
(see `ice.md`). Its northern edge frays into the longest
transition on Earth: the taiga–tundra ecotone, 13,400 km of
stunted trees giving way to shrubs. And the ring is moving:
1984–2020 satellite mapping shows +0.844 million square km
(+12%) shifted 0.29° north, young stands banking 1–6
petagrams of carbon — while North American productivity
falters and fires rewrite whole provinces. NASA's 2024
study extends the greening to 2100, with the catch that
darker forest over permafrost thaws what it shades,
releasing ancient carbon and methane.

Sources: NASA Earth Observatory BOREAS, forest-threshold,
boreal-northward (2026), and tundra (2024) pages; NPS
boreal pages; US Forest Service boreal chapter.

## Tundra — the felt at the top

Beyond the trees, permafrost and nine-month winter allow
only shrubs, mosses, grasses, and lichens — a brown-green
felt greening measurably since the 1980s, growing taller
through 2100 by every projection. Caribou, musk oxen, and
migratory birds summer here; polar bears hunt the frozen
sea edge. Tundra stores the past (frozen carbon) and
decides the future (its thaw rate) — the hinge biome, small
in area, outsized in consequence.

## How zones change over time

Biomes track climate with lagging feet. Ice ages pushed
tundra to mid-latitudes and shrank rainforests to refuges;
the Sahara was grass with lakes 6,000 years ago (Mega-Chad
in `plains.md:68`); boreal forest still sits where ice
stood 10,000 years back. Fire sets the tempo inside zones:
savanna yearly, boreal in century crown fires, Mediterranean
in decade burns — and suppression changes the fuel, so
excluded fire returns worse. Humans now move boundaries
fastest: farms replace forest and grassland, cities pave
both, and warming drags every zone poleward and uphill —
the greening tundra and marching taiga are the current
frames of a permanent film.

## How it interacts with the others

The base sets the palette (`continents.md`): latitude,
elevation, and ocean currents draw the zones before life
chooses. The lift stacks zones vertically (`mountains.md`):
rainforest to cloud forest to paramo to ice in one slope —
a whole planet's biomes in a day's climb. The flat feeds
the world (`plains.md`): grasslands become breadbaskets,
deserts set the dry limit. The blue waters (`oceans.md`):
currents warm coasts into forest or chill them into desert
(Humboldt's Atacama, the Benguela's Namib). The flow
(`rivers.md`): rivers thread zones together, deltas farm
the richest ground. Fire resets (`volcanoes.md`, L10
`volcanism.md`): ash fertilizes, eruptions cool, lava
restarts succession. Shaking reroutes (`earthquakes.md`):
landslides strip forest, lakes drain. Still water anchors
(`lakes.md`): Great Lakes temper lee climates into snow
belts (`weather.md`). Ice bounds the top (`ice.md`):
sheets, permafrost, and the ecotone line. Storms water and
wreck (`weather.md`): monsoons feeding billions, cyclones
flattening forest. The global air, water, tilt, and shield
run the climate from L10.

## Key numbers

- Boreal: 16.6–20M km2, 50–65°N, ~1/3 terrestrial carbon;
+0.844M km2 (+12%) 1984–2020, +0.29° north; ecotone
13,400 km.
- Amazon basin ~7M km2; Sahara ~9M km2; Great Plains
2,800 km long.
- Tundra greening since 1980s, taller through 2100;
permafrost thaw releases ancient carbon/methane.
- Sahara grass with lakes 6,000 years ago; boreal on
10,000-year-old ice ground.
- L11 frame: 100–1,000 km — one biome with its
transitions per view; stands and fields at L12.

Sources: NASA, NPS, and USFS pages named above; L11
`plains.md` and README figures.

## Example objects

The Amazon is the rainforest standard: dark, wet,
diverse — the most life per frame on Earth.

The Serengeti is the savanna standard: grass, fire,
migration — seasons written in herds.

The Sahara is the desert standard: 9 million square km of
sinking air — and grass 6,000 years back.

The Great Plains are the temperate standard: grass turned
breadbasket — nature overlaid by survey grids.

The boreal ring is the carbon standard: a third of
terrestrial storage, marching north, burning in crowns.

The tundra is the hinge standard: felt greening, ground
thawing — small area, planetary consequence.

Sources: NASA Earth Observatory; NPS; USFS.
