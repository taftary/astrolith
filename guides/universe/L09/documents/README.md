# L09 — Stars

L09 = L9 (ladder row L9, Stars, ~10^9 m). This
folder is the L9 visual and lifetime reference (Issue #226): what
its parts look like, how they were created, how they changed through
time, and how they interact — each page with target-visual images
and named sources. The bridge from the previous level and the entry
transition come first; the dated timeline runs through everything.

## Scope and scale

L09 covers one star close-up: the Sun with its interior, visible
surface, outer atmosphere, activity with wind and light, and the
same planets as nearby points, in the ~10^9 m range around the
Sun-diameter anchor (1.39 x 10^9 m, IAU nominal radius). It sits between the [L08 —
Planetary system](../../L08/documents/README.md) above and the
[L10 — Planets and moons](../../L10/documents/README.md) below,
under the [Universe](../../documents/README.md#levels) ladder.
Frozen scale rules live in `docs/universes/ladder.md`; this folder
holds the descriptive reference only. The plural title follows the
ladder row name; the worked example is our Sun, with rare stellar
companions covered as company in [companions.md](./companions.md).

Read order: bridge ([previous-l8.md](./previous-l8.md)) first, then
the entry transition ([entry.md](./entry.md)), then any part below,
then the dated spine ([lifetime.md](./lifetime.md)) for dates behind
each claim.

## Figures at a glance

Canonical detail lives in the linked pages; this index carries only
the navigation-level numbers: L9 span ~10^9 m; anchor Sun diameter
1.39 x 10^9 m; Sun a 4.6-billion-year-old G2V dwarf holding
99.8 percent of the system's mass, about 1.4 million km across;
photosphere about 5,500 degrees C (10,000 degrees F), 250 miles
(400 km) thick; spots 1,600 to 160,900 km across; equator spins
once in about 25 Earth days (36 near the poles); corona past
1,000,000 degrees extending more than a million km; wind past one
million miles per hour with fast streams about 700 km/s; cycle
about 11 years (Cycle 25 maximum phase October 2024, active into
2026); planets as nearby points Mercury 0.39 AU to Neptune 30.1 AU
(Earth at 1 AU, 93 million miles, 8 light-minutes); L8-to-L9 ratio
7.76e-5 with 32 markers per L8 cell and three zoom gaps, L9-to-L10
ratio 9.33e-3 with about 12 markers per L9 cell and two zoom gaps
— see [key numbers](./previous-l8.md#key-numbers), [entry
numbers](./entry.md#key-numbers), and the [dated
timeline](./lifetime.md).

## How this level differs from L08

[L08](../../L08/documents/README.md) counts order — one yellow
dwarf at the center with its four rocky worlds, its four giants as
disks with rings and large moons, its belt populations, and the
wind bubble with the Voyager edge, all out to 120 AU. Here one of
those stars fills the view: the counted order falls back into
surroundings while a single stellar disk takes center with its
layered engine, its spotted face, its million-degree crown, its
flares and wind with the habitable-zone light, and the same planets
as nearby points — so new spot, flare, wind, and stellar-lifetime
results enter through the part docs, not this index.
