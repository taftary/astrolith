# Other stars — close-ups of every kind

This page is the L09 part doc for what other star kinds
look like up close: red-dwarf flare stars, giants with
continent-sized cells, hot blue stars with winds, and the
dead as disks filling the view. The Sun's own close-up
lives in `surface.md` and `atmosphere.md`; binary and
habitable-zone context in `companions.md:107-136`; mass
paths in `lifetime.md:34-43`; the number tables in the
[stars catalog](../../catalogs/documents/stars.md).

The Sun is one G among billions. Fill an L09 frame with
another kind and the face changes completely: tiny furious
red, bloated boiling orange, searing blue-white with no
spots at all, or a Earth-sized ember fading to black. Same
physics — plasma, magnetism, wind — every face different.

## Red dwarfs — small, furious, everywhere [M]

Three quarters of all stars, none visible naked-eye: dim
red disks where flares dwarf Carrington — starspots
covering half the face, flares doubling the light in
minutes, winds and UV scouring close planets (see
`companions.md:133-134`). Convection reaches the center,
so fuel mixes fully and lives run trillions of years.
Proxima's flares (see L06 `systems.md`) show the type:
habitable zones close-in, tidally locked, space weather
extreme. Up close: deep red limb-darkened disk, black spot
groups, white flare ribbons, prominences oversized to the
star.

## Orange and yellow dwarfs — the steady [K, G]

K dwarfs: orange, calm, long-lived — the premium habitat
real estate, flaring less than M or G. G stars: the Sun's
class, granulation and 11-year-like cycles; F stars
hotter white with shallow convection and fast spin.
Alpha Centauri B (K) and Tau Ceti (G) in L06 `stars.md`
are the neighborhood examples. Up close: familiar
granulation, spots in belts, faculae — the Sun with the
color slider moved.

## Hot blue stars — wind without spots [O, B, A]

Above ~1.5 Suns, convection sinks below the surface and
spots vanish: instead, radiation-driven winds thousands of
km/s sculpting nebulae for parsecs, no coronae like the
Sun's but shocked wind zones glowing in X-rays. O stars
live millions of years and die as supernovae or black
holes; Sirius A (A, L06 `stars.md`) shows the mild end.
Up close: searing blue-white uniform disk, no granulation
visible, wind streaming off the limb.

## Giants and supergiants — boiling continents [evolved]

Exhausted cores, swollen envelopes: red giants tens of
Suns across with convection cells the size of orbits,
pulsating; supergiants hundreds across shedding dusty
winds. Betelgeuse-class surfaces churn continent-sized
hot and cool patches — interferometry maps them directly.
Up close: orange-red disk with a few giant cells, limb
extended and hazy, mass visibly leaving. AGB stars add
carbon dredge-up and superwinds; the Sun's own fate (see
`lifetime.md`, L08 `sun.md`).

## The dead as disks [remnants]

White dwarfs: Earth-sized blue-white embers, no surface
features but crushing gravity stratifying pure hydrogen —
cooling track over billions of years (Sirius B in L06
`dwarfs.md`). Neutron stars: city-sized, beamed, spinning
to hundreds of Hz — a point of clockwork light, not a
disk at all. The L06 future `remnants.md` holds their
neighborhood view; the catalog holds numbers.

## The Sun's true color — a footnote

The Sun is white, not yellow: noon sunlight through thin
air reads neutral, and its spectrum wears thousands of
dark absorption lines — each element's barcode, the tool
every stellar class above was sorted with. Sunsets orange
is air, not star (see `atmosphere.md` at L10).
