# Entry — diving from L5 into the stellar neighborhood

This page walks the L5-to-L6 dive in plain steps: the approach, the
effects and visuals on entry, and how long it takes. The bridge from L5
lives in [previous-l5.md](./previous-l5.md); the part docs from the
loop's first pass — [stars.md](./stars.md),
[multiples.md](./multiples.md), [dwarfs.md](./dwarfs.md),
[ism.md](./ism.md), [systems.md](./systems.md) — hold the detail, and
the dated timeline runs through [lifetime.md](./lifetime.md).

## The approach, in plain steps

1. **Pick the home portal.** Start in the L5 cell holding the
    Orion-sized cloud complex — the 100-parsec anchor of L5. Thirty-two
    markers share the cell (cloud populations plus largest-cloud
    portals); only portals open, populations stay points (ADR 0010).
    Target the portal with the familiar triple glint — Alpha Centauri —
    and the steady yellow spark beside it: home. It holds one L6 stellar
    neighborhood at a true size ratio of 1.35e-2.

2. **Watch the preview.** Above 0.02 rad the marker draws its interior
    inside itself at the exact positions the open cell will show, at true
    size (`marker + child x ratio`, ladder R7): first a brighter patch of
    the cloud glow, then counted points resolving inside it — blue-white
    Sirius-like sparks, yellow Sun-like ones, swarms of faint red M
    dwarfs — growing as the dot fades from full brightness toward a faint
    floor (0.15) at the open angle while its children brighten from 0 to
    1, both curves continuous, so the moment of opening is a pure origin
    shift with no pop. At most 6 markers (largest on screen) preview at
    once. Only portal markers preview: field populations are displayed
    and counted but never open and never preview.
3. **Fall through the milestones.** The L5–L6 span is crossed through two
    invisible magnification milestones, because its 1.87-decade span
    (ladder anchors e 18.49 down to e 16.62) exceeds the 1.5-decade
    single-crossing limit. Each is a silent exact no-op on the path (zero
    position, unit ratio): generation, snapshots, labels and previews never
    see them. The dive pushes them by proximity to the targeted portal and
    unwinds them the same way. You notice them only as continued smooth
    motion across nearly two decades of scale (ladder gap note). On the
    way, the spiral-arm countryside smears past the view edges while the
    complex swells: first a lantern among many studding the arm, then the
    only thing ahead, its dust lanes and lit rims filling half the sky.
4. **Open at 0.14 rad.** Past the open angle (about a third of the view)
    the marker's dot eases to zero as the camera passes inside; the open
    cell closes back into its marker below 0.10 rad, re-targeted. You are
    now in L6, inside the stellar-neighborhood cell. The parent's siblings
    hang 1/ratio cells away (about 74 L6-cell widths for the 1.35e-2
    ratio); nothing shallower is drawn.

## Effects and visuals on entry

**What appears.** The cloud becomes suns: the glow that filled the sky
thins and dissolves into black, its light now behind you, while the
counted populations sharpen into individual stars with colors —
blue-white Sirius-like sparks, yellow Sun-like ones, swarms of faint red
M dwarfs, the dim majority the RECONS census counts (about three in
four nearby stars). Close doubles split — Alpha Centauri A and B as two
disks, Sirius B as a faint ember beside its brilliant primary — and the
triple nature of home stands revealed. The thin medium shows as almost
nothing, which is the point — the Local Bubble's near-vacuum, the wisp
of the Local Interstellar Cloud, the Sun's heliosphere nose pushing into
it (see [ism.md](./ism.md)).

**What fades.** The Galactic-structures context falls away: the spiral
arms that were terrain, the glowing nurseries, the scattered young
clusters — all shrink back into the L5 marker you came through. The
building-site story (collapse, feedback, dispersal) stops being weather
and becomes somebody else's story; here the story is the neighbors
themselves.

**What the markers do.** The L5 marker you entered closes behind you into
an ordinary portal, re-targeted, sitting 1/ratio cells away among its
siblings. Ahead, the L6 cell offers its own markers: at most 6 system
portals (ladder R6), usually one — the next dive is a single system, the
Oort shells of L7, with no zoom gap at all (ratio 3.55e-1). What waits
inside is now written up part by part: the suns you count
([stars.md](./stars.md)), the pairs they come in
([multiples.md](./multiples.md)), the faint endpoints
([dwarfs.md](./dwarfs.md)), the thin air between them
([ism.md](./ism.md)), and the worlds they hold
([systems.md](./systems.md)).

In order, on the way in:

1. The cloud glow thins and goes: the complex that filled the sky
   dissolves into black, its light now behind you.
2. Points sharpen into suns: the counted populations resolve into
   individual stars with colors — blue-white Sirius-like sparks,
   yellow Sun-like ones, swarms of faint red M dwarfs, the dim
   majority the RECONS census counts (284 of 378 stars within 10
   parsecs, about three in four; see [stars.md](./stars.md)).
3. Companions appear: close doubles split — Alpha Centauri A and B
   as two disks on their 79.8-year orbit, Sirius B as a faint ember
   beside its brilliant primary (10,000 times fainter, weighed at
   0.98 Suns through its gravitational redshift) — and the triple
   nature of home stands revealed (see [multiples.md](./multiples.md),
   [dwarfs.md](./dwarfs.md)).
4. The thin medium shows: almost nothing, which is the point — the
   Local Bubble's near-vacuum (~1,000 light-years across, blown by
   ~15 supernovae), the ~30-light-year wisp of the Local
   Interstellar Cloud, the Sun's heliosphere nose pushing into it
   (see [ism.md](./ism.md)).
5. The markers thin out: from 32 per L5 cell to at most 6 in L6,
   usually one — the next dive is a single system, the Oort shells
   of L7, with no zoom gap at all (ratio 3.55e-1; see
   [systems.md](./systems.md) for what that one system holds).

## Survey context

The neighborhood you dive into is counted, not decoration. The RECONS
10-parsec census — the trigonometric-parallax sample out to 32.6
light-years — holds 462 objects in 317 systems (April 2018, pre-Gaia
DR2), and its headline stands: M dwarfs are 284 of 378 stars, ~75% of
all nearby suns, while only ~27% of systems (85 of 317) are multiple —
so the preview's red swarm with a few bright sparks is the measured
population, and Gaia-era parallaxes since have only pinned the
distances harder (Alpha Centauri at 4.37 light-years, Proxima at
4.246). The cavity you cross is mapped too: the CfA/Zucker
reconstruction traces the ~1,000-light-year Local Bubble to ~15
supernovae starting ~14 million years ago, with the Sun wandering in
~5 million years ago — every young star within 500 light-years sits on
its expanding shell. And the ember beside Sirius is weighed, not
assumed: HST/STIS isolated Sirius B's spectrum through A's glare and
read its gravitational redshift — 0.98 Suns packed into 7,500 miles at
~25,200 K — a direct test of general relativity that also fixes what
"white-dwarf endpoint" means before [dwarfs.md](./dwarfs.md) shows it
up close. None of this changes the entry mechanics above; it is why
the preview's neighbor geography can be trusted as a real census.
Sources: RECONS census page; CfA Local Bubble release (Nature 2022);
NASA Sirius-companion weighing; L06 [stars.md](./stars.md) (Gaia-era
parallaxes) and [ism.md](./ism.md) (Bubble history).

## How long it takes

The seeded autopilot runs L1 to L10 in about 40 seconds, through the
Local Group with Virgo as the rich sibling (ladder R8); travel time per
rung scales with its decade span (`Δe · ln 10 / k`), so the L5–L6 leg
(1.87 decades, cloud scale at e 18.49 down to neighborhood scale at e
16.62) is one of the shorter deep crossings — the longest is the
four-decade heliopause-to-Sun leg far below. The L5 anchor (100-pc cloud
complex, 3.09 x 10^18 m) gives way to the L6 anchor (Alpha Centauri
distance, 4.13 x 10^16 m), ratio 1.35e-2 per the rung table. That
compression is honest: rung gaps on screen stay proportional to the real
decade gaps. Inside L6, distances read in light-years, not kiloparsecs:
4.37 to the nearest triple (Proxima at 4.246), 8.6 to Sirius, ~10
parsecs (32.6 light-years) to the RECONS census edge holding 462 known
objects. Sources: ladder R5 (anchors), R6 (navigation, travel time),
R8 (autopilot form); RECONS census.

## What entry proves

Entry is complete when the cloud is gone, the suns stand counted, and
the next markers are few: you count neighbors, not structures. If you
can name the triple (Alpha Centauri A, B, Proxima), the brightest spark
(Sirius A) and its weighed ember (Sirius B), and the yellow dwarf that
is ours — you are in L6. The detailed looks, lifetimes and interactions
of each kind live in the part docs: [stars.md](./stars.md) for the
suns, [multiples.md](./multiples.md) for the pairs,
[dwarfs.md](./dwarfs.md) for the faint endpoints, [ism.md](./ism.md)
for the thin medium, [systems.md](./systems.md) for what the suns hold
— with the dated story in [lifetime.md](./lifetime.md).

## Key numbers

- Preview starts: marker angular radius above 0.02 rad (at most 6 markers,
  largest on screen); dot fades full to 0.15 floor, children 0 to 1; past
  open angle dot eases to zero.
- Open: 0.14 rad; close back below 0.10 rad.
- L5 to L6 ratio: 1.35e-2; 32 markers per L5 cell (cloud populations plus
  largest-cloud portals); siblings ~74 L6-cell widths away.
- Span crossed: 1.87 decades (e 18.49 to e 16.62); L6 span 10^16–10^17 m.
- Invisible milestones L5–L6: two, silent exact no-ops.
- Autopilot L1 to L10: about 40 s total, through the Local Group with Virgo
  as the rich sibling.
- L5 anchor: 100 pc complex (3.09 x 10^18 m); L6 anchor: 4.37 ly
  (4.13 x 10^16 m).
- Portals ahead: at most 6 per L6 cell (usually one); L6–L7 takes no zoom
  gap (ratio 3.55e-1).
- Alpha Centauri 4.37 ly (Proxima 4.246); Sirius 8.6 ly, 50-year orbit,
  B at 0.98 Suns; RECONS edge 10 pc (32.6 ly), 462 objects in 317
  systems, ~75% M dwarfs (284 of 378).
- Local Bubble ~1,000 light-years across (~15 supernovae from ~14 Myr ago,
  Sun inside ~5 Myr); Local Interstellar Cloud ~30 light-years,
  ~7,000 K.
