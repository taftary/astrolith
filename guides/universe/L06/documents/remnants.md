# Remnants — neutron stars and black holes

This page is the L06 part doc for stellar remnants:
neutron stars, pulsars, magnetars, and stellar black holes
— the endpoints of massive stars in the neighborhood.
Mentions live in `ism.md:153` (Geminga), `multiples.md:89-
90` (gravitational pairs), L05 `globulars.md:60-61`
(millisecond pulsars) and `lifetime.md:187`; numbers in the
[stars catalog](../../catalogs/documents/stars.md); the
Sun's quieter exit in `evolved.md`.

Nothing here shines by fusion. A city-sized neutron ball
beaming radio like a lighthouse; a magnetar flaring
gamma-rays across the galaxy; a black hole bending
starlight with ten solar masses of nothing. The
neighborhood's most extreme objects are its darkest —
found by clocks, bursts, and wobbles, not by light.

## Neutron stars and pulsars

Core collapse leaves neutrons at nuclear density: ~20 km
wide, teaspoonfuls weighing mountains, spinning to
hundreds of Hz at birth. Beams make pulsars — clockwork
pulses rivaling atomic time, slowing over Myr, glitching
as crusts crack. Geminga (see `ism.md:153`) is the near
quiet one; the Crab (see L05 future `supernovae.md`) the
young loud one; millisecond pulsars in L05 `globulars.md`
are spun up by ancient companions. Kicks at birth send
many racing — high-velocity runaways from long-dead
clusters.

## Magnetars — the magnetic extreme

A fraction are born with quadrillion-Gauss fields: star-
quakes cracking the crust power giant flares — SGR 1806-20's
2004 blast squeezed Earth's ionosphere from 50,000
light-years away. Magnetars may drive fast radio bursts;
they spin down in millennia, fading to quiet neutron
stars. Rare, violent, brief.

## Stellar black holes — the dark endpoints

Past ~20–25 Suns, collapse passes neutron support: holes
of 3–100 Suns, found by X-ray binaries, microlensing, and
gravitational waves. Gaia BH1 and BH3 are the nearest
known, found by stellar wobbles in ESA Gaia data — dark
companions betrayed by orbits. Mergers chirp (see
`multiples.md:89-90`); kilonovae forge gold (see catalog
`elements.md`, GW170817). Intermediate-mass holes remain
debated (see L05 `globulars.md`); supermassive ones belong
at L04 (`nuclei.md`).

Sources: ESA Gaia pages; LIGO Laboratory pages.

## How it interacts with the others

Singles die (`stars.md`): only the massive reach these
exits; M dwarfs never will. Multiples merge
(`multiples.md`): gravitational pairs, X-ray binaries,
recycled millisecond pulsars. Dwarfs contrast
(`dwarfs.md`): white-dwarf embers versus neutron crush.
The medium records (`ism.md`): Geminga's halo, iron-60
from nearby supernovae, supernova-sculpted Bubble. Systems
feel (`systems.md`): planets around pulsars (first
exoplanets ever, 1992), orbits perturbed by kicks. The
explosions hang at L05 (future `supernovae.md`); the
fates at L09 (`lifetime.md:205`).

Target visual: a dedicated remnant frame (Crab pulsar wind
nebula, NASA — link, not copy) is proposed; no new raster

## Key numbers

- Neutron stars ~20 km, nuclear density, spins to 100s Hz;
kicks to 100s km/s.
- Magnetar fields to 10^15 Gauss; 2004 flare squeezed
Earth's ionosphere from 50 kly.
- Stellar holes 3–100 Suns; Gaia BH1/BH3 nearest by wobble.
- Mergers chirp; kilonovae forge gold (GW170817).
- L06 frame: 10^16–10^17 m — one remnant with its field
per view.

Sources: ESA Gaia; LIGO; in-repo pages named above.

## Example objects

Geminga is the near standard: quiet gamma pulsar next
door — lighthouse without radio.

The Crab pulsar is the young standard: 33 ms, wind nebula
lit — 1054's heartbeat.

SGR 1806-20 is the magnetic standard: galaxy-wide flare —
crust cracking.

Gaia BH3 is the dark standard: 33 Suns by wobble — mass
without light.

Sources: pages named above.
