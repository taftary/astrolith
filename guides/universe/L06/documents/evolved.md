# Evolved stars — giants and their nebulae

This page is the L06 part doc for evolved stars: red
giants, AGB stars, supergiants, variables, and the
planetary nebulae they shed — the exit this level's stars
take. Single-clause mentions live in `lifetime.md:135-139`
(Sun's fate), `stars.md:82`, `dwarfs.md:106-108`
(nebulae), and L05 (`open.md:49,189`, `arms.md:223-224`);
white-dwarf aftermath in `dwarfs.md`; numbers in the
[stars catalog](../../catalogs/documents/stars.md).

A 0.1–3 light-year nebula fills an L06 frame exactly —
which is why planetary nebulae live here, not at L05. The
story: hydrogen exhausts, shells ignite, envelopes swell
and pulse, winds strip, cores emerge lit — a star
unmaking itself in public over a hundred thousand years.

## Giants, AGB stars, supergiants

Hydrogen exhaustion ends the main sequence; shell fusion
begins and the envelope balloons: subgiants first, then
red giants tens of Suns across (Arcturus, Aldebaran),
then asymptotic-branch stars dredging carbon into winds —
Miras breathing on year timescales, carbon stars reddened
by soot. Supergiants are the massive parallel: Betelgeuse-
class, hundreds across, continent-sized convection cells,
dusty mass loss. Hyades giants and the red clump in L05
`open.md` are these phases caught in clusters.

## Variables of the exit

Pulsation comes with swelling: Miras (year-long, amplitude
tenfold), RR Lyrae (day-scale, halo tracers in L04
`halos.md:243`), Cepheids (period-luminosity standard
candles measuring the universe). Flare activity from L06
`systems.md:45` belongs to youth, not age — old stars
pulse, young ones flare.

## Planetary nebulae — the shed skins

The exposed core ionizes the ejected envelope: Helix
(nearest), Ring, Dumbbell, Owl — 0.1–3 ly of glowing
shells in oxygen green and hydrogen red, expanding tens of
km/s, visible ~10,000–100,000 years before dispersing
into the ISM (see `ism.md`). Shapes record the exit:
round for single stars, bipolar for binaries, jets for
magnetic ones. The Sun's fate in 5 billion years (see L08
`sun.md`, L09 `lifetime.md`); L07 `lifetime.md:69` nods
from outside.

## How it interacts with the others

Singles age (`stars.md`): mass sets the clock, OBAFGKM
each with its exit. Multiples complicate (`multiples.md`):
mass transfer, common envelopes, novae and Type Ia instead
of quiet nebulae. Dwarfs inherit (`dwarfs.md`): every
white dwarf is a nebula's leftover core. The medium
receives (`ism.md`): nebulae and winds enriching carbon,
nitrogen, dust. Systems watch (`systems.md`): planets
engulfed or surviving around white dwarfs, debris
polluting atmospheres. Clusters date (`previous-l5.md`
context): turnoff points reading ages. The center and arms
set the backdrop (L05 `disk.md`, `arms.md`); the catalog
holds numbers.

Target visual: a dedicated exit frame (Helix or Ring
nebula, NASA/ESA — link, not copy) is proposed; no new
raster file is added here.

## Key numbers

- Giants tens of Suns; supergiants hundreds; nebulae
0.1–3 ly expanding tens of km/s over 10–100 kyr.
- Miras year-scale; RR Lyrae day-scale; Cepheids standard
candles.
- Sun's exit in ~5 Gyr: nebula then white dwarf.
- L06 frame: 10^16–10^17 m — one exit with its shell per view.

Sources: in-repo pages named above; catalog stars page.

## Example objects

The Helix is the near standard: closest shed skin —
white dwarf centered.

The Ring is the icon standard: smoke-ring geometry —
pole-on bipolar.

A Mira is the breathing standard: tenfold yearly —
envelope on the way out.

Hyades giants are the cluster standard: same age, same
exit — evolution synchronized.
