# Lifetime — dated stages of the outer solar system

Loop doc for Issue #224.

A short dated story of L7, from dust to Voyagers to the far future. Each stage gives one time, what changed, and where to read the full part doc.

![Earth as a single pixel in a sunbeam, Voyager 1 1990 (screensize)](../images/pale-blue-dot-revised.jpg)

## 4.6 Ga — solar nebula collapses, giants start to form

About 4.6 billion years ago a nearby cloud collapse forms the Sun and a disc of gas and dust. Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune grow from that disc over the next few million years: rock and ice cores first, then gas capture for Jupiter and Saturn. This sets up everything else in L7: a crowded Kuiper Belt, a scattered disc, and a cloud of icy leftovers that will become the Oort cloud. Details in `giants.md`.

Sources: NASA solar-system formation overviews; NASA Our Solar System formation pages.

## 4.5–4.4 Ga — giants migrate, belt sculpted, Oort emplaced

The giant planets do not stay where they formed. Jupiter moves slightly inward while Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune move outward, scattering icy bodies everywhere. Three things happen at once: the Kuiper Belt is sculpted into resonant, classical, and scattered populations (see `kuiper.md`); millions of icy bodies are thrown outward and the galaxy's pull rounds them into the spherical Oort cloud (see `oort.md`); Neptune migrates outward through the disc, trapping Pluto and others in resonances.

Sources: Nice-model literature via NASA and ESA formation explainers; NASA Kuiper Belt and Oort Cloud pages.

## 4.0–3.8 Ga — bombardment and comet delivery

Around 4 billion years ago the inner planets record heavy cratering. Outer-system scattering delivered comets and asteroids inward during this window. For L7 this is the Oort cloud and Kuiper Belt paying their debt inward: long-period comets and impactors released by the same migration that built them. Details in `comets.md`.

Sources: NASA lunar cratering explainers (~3.8–4.0 Ga).

## 70 kyr ago to 1.3 Myr future — showers, passages, slow erosion

The Oort cloud keeps losing and gaining comets. About 70,000 years ago Scholz's star passed ~0.8 light-years from the Sun, through the outer Oort cloud. About 1.3 million years from now Gliese 710 will pass ~0.06 pc (~12,000 AU) from the Sun and stir fresh comets loose. Day to day, the Sun steadily strips the outer Oort edge while interstellar comets pass through.

Sources: Scholz's star flyby summaries (~70,000 years ago); ESA Gaia pages on Gliese 710 (~1.3 Myr future, ~0.06 pc).

## 1977–2018 — Voyager era: launches, shock, heliopause

Humanity touches L7 in place. 1977: Voyager 2 launches 20 August, Voyager 1 launches 5 September. 1979–1989: grand tour — Jupiter (1979), Saturn (1980–1981), Uranus (1986), Neptune (1989). December 2004: Voyager 1 crosses the termination shock at 94 AU. August 2007: Voyager 2 crosses the termination shock at 84 AU. August 2012: Voyager 1 crosses the heliopause at 122 AU. November 2018: Voyager 2 crosses the heliopause at 119 AU. 1990 bookend: Voyager 1's Pale Blue Dot (14 February 1990, 6 billion km out) looks back. Details in `comets.md`.

Sources: NASA Voyager mission and Interstellar Mission pages; NASA Pale Blue Dot page.

## 5 Gyr future — red giant Sun, then white dwarf; cloud stripped

About 5 billion years from now the Sun becomes a red giant. The outer system heats up: ices melt farther out, Kuiper Belt and Oort objects are stirred, orbits shift as the Sun loses mass. Afterward the Sun becomes a white dwarf. The weakened gravity plus galactic tide and stellar passages strip much of the Oort cloud into interstellar space — the slow fate of L7. The word fate belongs here: this is the far-future fate of the shells.

Sources: NASA Sun evolution pages (~5 billion years to red giant; white dwarf after).
