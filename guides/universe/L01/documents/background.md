# Cosmic background

The background is everything the
web sits in: the microwave afterglow, the expanding space itself, the dark
matter scaffolding and the dark energy driving acceleration. Template follows
the pilot (`cosmic-web.md`): looks, creation, change over time,
interactions, numbers, example probes, target visuals, sources.

## How the background looks

The microwave background is an all-sky glow with no source object: a perfect
2.72548 +/- 0.00057 K blackbody in every direction (FIRAS on COBE; true
error bars invisible even on an enlarged spectrum plot), isotropic to
~1 part in 25,000 after subtracting our motion dipole — RMS variations
just over 100 microK — with intrinsic ripples of ~1 in 100,000, the
seeds of all structure, teased out through COBE to WMAP to Planck. Our
peculiar velocity toward the dipole is 369.82 +/- 0.11 km/s, toward
Crater near its boundary with Leo (often rounded to 370 km/s). Its
acoustic peaks encode curvature (first peak) and the baryon vs
dark-matter densities (second and third); fainter E-polarization (~10x
weaker than temperature anisotropy, correlated with it), and still
fainter B-modes, carry lensing and possibly primordial-wave signals.
The CMB holds ~400x more photons than all stars ever emitted (~411 per
cm^3, energy density 0.260 eV/cm^3 or 4.17 x 10^-14 J/m^3 — about a
billion photons per baryon). Without expansion cooling it, the night
sky would shine as bright as the Sun.
Sources: CMB summary; COBE summary; BAO summary.

![Planck 2013 all-sky map: the microwave sky, seeds of galaxies at 380,000 years](../images/planck-cmb-2013.jpg)

Expansion looks like universal recession: galaxies in all directions recede
proportionally to distance (v = H0 x D) — metric growth of space itself, not
motion into anything; bound systems do not expand, and recession past the
Hubble sphere is superluminal without violating relativity. Dark matter shows
only by gravity across every scale: flat outer rotation curves (Babcock's
1939 Andromeda rise, Rubin and Ford's 1970s optical work, Bosma's 1978 HI
extension to 30 kpc and beyond), cluster velocity dispersions (Zwicky's 1933
Coma virial estimate — 400x the luminous mass with his obsolete Hubble
constant, direction right, scale overstated), lensing mass maps with the
Bullet Cluster's offset gas-versus-mass peaks, the third acoustic peak's
height, and clustering excess. The 1974 halo papers (Ostriker, Peebles and
Yahil; Einasto, Saar and Kaasik) made galactic dark halos consensus; the
concordance particle is cold — non-baryonic, slow at radiation-matter
equality, dissipationless, collisionless — some 85% of all mass, with WIMP
searches (XENON, LUX, PandaX, LZ) now hitting the neutrino-fog floor and
axion hunts (ADMX at DFSZ sensitivity) taking up the chase. Modified-gravity
alternatives (MOND and successors) still cannot cover every line of evidence
at once. Dark energy shows only by acceleration: distant Type Ia supernovae
10-15% farther than a matter-only universe allows, plus the flatness-plus-30%-matter remainder. Sources:
expansion and Hubble-law summaries; dark matter and dark energy summaries;
1998 supernova teams (Nobel 2011).

## How the background was created

CMB photons are relic light from ~378,000 years (z ~ 1090-1100), when protons
and electrons finally bound into neutral hydrogen and Thomson opacity
collapsed — recombination and photon decoupling (last scattering), distinct
but contemporaneous processes: recombination made the atoms, decoupling freed
the photons once free electrons diluted enough that their mean free path
exceeded the Hubble length. Before recombination a photon barrier hides all
earlier epochs from direct photon view. Helium recombined earlier behind the
still-opaque curtain, in two steps: He III to He II near Saha equilibrium at
z ~ 6000, then He II to He I (slower than equilibrium) at z ~ 2000 — details
that matter less for the CMB because the universe stayed optically thick
until hydrogen recombined. Equilibrium (Saha) estimates alone give 50%
ionization at ~4000 K (z ~ 1500), but the true history is non-equilibrium:
direct recombinations to the ground state just re-ionize a neighbor, so
electrons cascade through excited states and reach the ground only via
Lyman-alpha escape (redshifted out of resonance before reabsorption) or the
slow 2s two-photon decay (rate 8.22 s^-1), competing with photoionization by
ambient CMB photons. This effective three-level atom picture (Peebles 1968;
Zel'dovich, Kurt and Sunyaev 1968-1969) puts 90% neutrality at z ~ 1070; with
excited-state and radiative-transfer refinements, modern codes (CosmoRec,
HyRec) model it to 0.1%. The freed shell we see is the surface of last
scattering. Sources: recombination summary.

Expansion comes from the hot dense start plus inflation: the Friedmann
scale-factor history runs from ~0.1 s to today under general relativity, and
inflation's quantum fluctuations, frozen before equilibration, became the
density ripples gravity later amplified. Dark matter clumped first into
blobs and filaments — the scaffolding galaxies lit up. Dark energy's nature
is unknown: Einstein's cosmological constant (constant vacuum density,
negative pressure p = -rho c^2, w = -1) vs evolving scalar fields
(quintessence, moduli, interacting models); the term itself was coined by
Michael Turner in 1998. Its density is absurdly dilute — ~7 x 10^-30 g/cm^3
— yet dominates because it fills all space uniformly and never dilutes with
expansion. The triple evidence is supernova distances (Riess et al. and
Perlmutter et al. 1998, Nobel 2011), the flat-universe remainder
(BOOMERanG/Maxima 2000 first peak says ~100% critical, 2dFGRS 2001 says
~30% matter), and large-scale clustering plus late-time ISW. DESI DR1-DR2
(March 2025: BAO plus CMB plus lensing plus supernovae, 2.8-4.2 sigma
depending on dataset combo, below the 5-sigma discovery threshold) hints
at slowly decreasing density — Gaussian-process reconstructions agree a
two-parameter w(z) captures the trend, strongest at z < 0.3 — consistent
with Lambda alone so far. Sources: Lambda-CDM summary; dark
matter summary; dark energy and DESI summaries.

## How the background changes over time

Everything cools and dilutes by its equation of state — for a perfect fluid
with p = w rho, rho scales as a^-3(1+w): radiation (w = 1/3) as a^-4
(volume dilution plus redshifting of each photon), matter (w = 0) as a^-3,
vacuum energy (w = -1) constant — hence the radiation to matter to Lambda
sequence. The CMB cools as (1+z): ~3000 K at emission, 2.725 K today, while
nonrelativistic gas cools faster (T as a^-2). Inflation (~10^-32 s, at least
e^60 or ~10^26 growth) set the seeds; radiation domination ran through
neutrino decoupling (~1 s, scale factor growing as the square root of time)
to matter-radiation equality (~47-50 kyr), then matter domination until
deceleration flipped to acceleration ~9.8 Gyr in — 4 billion years ago
(z ~ 0.4-0.66 depending on convention); peculiar velocities and momenta decay
as 1/a, sorting the universe toward pure Hubble flow. With constant Lambda
the particle horizon converges and an event horizon caps the ever-observable
volume. Far-future: with acceleration dominant, no new large bound
structures freeze in and today's filaments dissolve as bound clusters
separate. Sources: expansion summary; Lambda-CDM summary; dark energy
summary.

## How the background interacts with structure

The CMB backlights everything after it: hot cluster electrons
inverse-Compton kick CMB photons to higher energies (thermal
Sunyaev-Zel'dovich, predicted by Sunyaev and Zel'dovich 1970; first seen
toward three clusters in 1984 by Birkinshaw, Gull and Hardebeck, first
imaged by the Ryle Telescope in the 1990s), a spectral distortion whose
surface brightness is redshift-free — high-z clusters detect as easily as
nearby ones, with the angular-size-redshift relation nearly flat from
z ~ 0.3 to 2 — while bulk motions add the kinematic SZ
(also Ostriker-Vishniac; Delta_T = -T_CMB V_p/c tau, first statistical
detection by ACT 2012, first individual object MACS J0717.5+3745 in 2012),
plus a fainter polarization SZ. SZ plus X-ray data give cluster thermal
structure, baryonic mass along the line of sight, and an independent Hubble
constant via the angular diameter distance; the South Pole Telescope SZ
survey found 415 clusters over 2500 square degrees by 2015. Planck's nine
bands (30-857 GHz, LFI plus HFI at 0.1 K above absolute zero — the coldest
known object in space from 2009 to 2012) mapped SZ clusters, lensing, ISW
and foregrounds. Expansion stretches the frozen 150-Mpc sound-horizon ruler
(~490 million light-years today; only 0.14 Mpc or 450,000 light-years in
radius at recombination, z = 1089): radial BAO reads H(z) from the redshift
interval, transverse BAO reads the angular diameter distance from the
subtended angle — dark matter dug the wells (it stayed at the perturbation
center while photon-baryon sound waves ran outward at just over half light
speed; after decoupling the photons diffused away and only the resonant
baryon shell plus the central dark overdensity remained), dark energy now
suppresses the digging, measured through H(z), distances, cluster counts and
w0-wa fits. The first acoustic peak (BOOMERanG/Maxima 2000) demanded a flat
universe with only ~30% matter — the original dark-energy remainder. In 2023
astronomers even claimed a single BAO bubble, Ho'oleilana (radius
155 h_75^-1 Mpc), traced by the Bootes supercluster plus the Sloan, CfA2 and
Hercules walls. Beyond the primary peaks, secondaries rewrite small scales:
photon diffusion (Silk damping) erases fine structure, gravitational lensing
by all intervening matter smears and B-converts polarization (mapped by
Planck and ACT/SPT into an all-matter map — the sibling pilot's fifth
visual), the late-time integrated Sachs-Wolfe rise correlates CMB lows with
supervoids, and reionization re-scatters ~6% of photons (tau = 0.054),
stamping the large-angle E-mode bump that dates first stars to z ~ 7.7.
Peak ladder in brief: first peak (l ~ 200, ~1 degree) fixes spatial
flatness; second-to-third ratio fixes baryon vs dark-matter density;
damping tail fixes the expansion rate and neutrino count (N_eff = 2.99).
Sources: SZ summary; BAO summary; dark energy summary; Planck
spacecraft summary.

## Key numbers

- T0 = 2.72548 +/- 0.00057 K (FIRAS); recombination ~3000-4000 K;
  Saha 50% estimate ~4000 K (z ~ 1500), true 90% neutral at z ~ 1070.
- Age: 13.787 +/- 0.020 Gyr (Planck 2018); 13.77 (WMAP9, within 0.5%);
  13.798 (Planck 2013+BAO) — dataset combos, not errors.
- H0 tension: 67.4 +/- 0.5 km/s/Mpc (Planck 2018, model-inferred) vs
  70-73 (local distance ladder); DES 2018 supernovae give
  Omega_Lambda = 0.669 +/- 0.038 independently of Planck's
  0.6847 +/- 0.0073; gravitational-wave standard sirens give ~70 as a
  third route. The tension exceeds 4-5 sigma between the extremes and
  is the sharpest crack in the concordance model.
- Budget (Planck 2018): ~4.9% ordinary (Omega_b h^2 = 0.0224 +/- 0.0001),
  ~26.5% cold dark matter (Omega_c h^2 = 0.120 +/- 0.001),
  ~68.5% dark energy (Omega_Lambda = 0.6847 +/- 0.0073); 2013 and WMAP9
  differ by ~1-2 points with data combo. Radiation today is negligible
  (Omega_rad ~ 10^-4); curvature is zero to within 0.4% (WMAP) and below
  1% precision (Planck).
- Equality ~47-50 kyr; last-scattering thickness ~118 kyr; deceleration to
  acceleration transition at a = (Omega_m/2 Omega_Lambda)^(1/3) ~ 0.6,
  z ~ 0.66 for Planck best fit (quoted as z ~ 0.4-0.66 depending on
  convention); reionization optical depth tau = 0.054 +/- 0.007,
  midpoint z_re = 7.68 +/- 0.79.
- Base-6 extras: scalar index ns = 0.965 +/- 0.004 (tilted from 1, an
  inflation prediction confirmed), ln(10^10 A_s) = 3.043 +/- 0.014,
  sigma8 = 0.811 +/- 0.006; tensor-to-scalar ratio r_0.002 < 0.06
  (no primordial B-modes yet). Open threads: S8 lensing tension, Cold
  Spot, low quadrupole, axis of evil — all marginal.

Sources: Lambda-CDM parameters summary; WMAP and expansion summaries.

## Example probes

- COBE (Explorer 66, 1989-1993): FIRAS polarizing Michelson interferometer
  (0.1-10 mm, differential sky-vs-internal-3K reference) proves the perfect
  blackbody at 2.7 K, killing the 1987 Berkeley-Nagoya excess claim; DMR
  (31.4, 53, 90 GHz differential horns, 60-degree throw, 7-degree beams)
  finds the 10^-5 primordial seeds, announced April 1992 (Nobel 2006 to
  Mather and Smoot — "the starting point for cosmology as a precision
  science"); DIRBE (1-300 micron) maps dust, zodiacal light and the
  infrared background. Precision cosmology starts.
- WMAP (2001-2010, Sun-Earth L2): differential experiment with passively
  cooled radiometers, five bands 22-90 GHz, rapid spin plus slow precession
  spirograph scan; 45x sensitivity and 33x resolution over COBE with the
  first fine 0.2-degree full-sky map; 13.77 Gyr within 0.5%, curvature flat
  to 0.4%, baryons ~5% — plus full-sky E-polarization and the reionization
  epoch. Three most-cited 2000s physics/astronomy papers are WMAP papers
  (Breakthrough Prize 2018, Gruber 2012, Shaw 2010).
- Planck (ESA, 2009-2013, L2 Lissajous with Herschel launch): 3x finer than
  WMAP with LFI (30-70 GHz HEMTs) plus HFI (100-857 GHz JPL bolometers, six
  bands, lower four polarized); 2013 first all-sky map (13.798 Gyr, 4.8%
  ordinary, 26.8% dark, 69% energy, H0 67.8), 2015 polarization plus joint
  BICEP2 dust verdict (no primordial waves), 2018 legacy parameters; SZ
  catalog, lensing, ISW, Galactic magnetism. Decommissioned to heliocentric
  graveyard orbit October 2013 (Gruber 2018, Shaw 2018, Cocconi 2019).
- SDSS BAO (2005): 150-Mpc acoustic peak in 46,748 LRGs confirms the WMAP
  sound horizon; 6dF, WiggleZ, BOSS extend it.
- Supernova cosmology (1998): High-Z plus SCP teams find acceleration
  (Nobel 2011); JLA re-analyses debated, consensus stands.
- DESI (2021-): 5,000-fiber 3D map on the Mayall telescope; DR1 (first-year
  data, public March 2025): 18.7M high-quality redshifts — 13.1M galaxies,
  1.6M quasars, 4M stars, the largest 3D map yet; DR2 (first three years,
  March 2025 papers, cosmology chains October 2025): 14M+ galaxies and
  quasars across six galaxy/quasar tracer samples plus the Lyman-alpha
  forest, BAO precision ~0.24% overall (factor ~2 over DR1) and 0.65%
  above redshift 2 from the forest alone (universe ~3 Gyr old) — BAO plus
  RSD plus evolving-dark-energy test, preference up to ~4.2 sigma with
  supernova combos, still below discovery threshold. Companion analyses
  bound the neutrino mass sum below 0.0642 eV (in ~3-sigma tension with the
  0.059 eV laboratory floor; allowing evolving dark energy relaxes the cap
  to 0.163 eV), and the collaboration ran blinded analyses with synthetic
  mock validation (100 Saclay plus 300 CoLoRe-QL mocks) recovering BAO
  parameters within a third of the statistical error. Survey completed its
  planned five years in April 2026 with 50M+ redshifts.

Sources: COBE, WMAP, Planck spacecraft, BAO, DESI, accelerating-expansion
summaries.
