# L10 — Planets and moons

L10 = L10 (ladder row L10, Planets and moons, 10^6–10^8 m). This
folder is the L10 visual and lifetime reference (Issue #227): what
its parts look like, how they were created, how they changed through
time, and how they interact — each page with target-visual images
and named sources. The bridge from the previous level and the entry
transition come first; the dated timeline runs through everything.
L10 is terminal for the MVP journey (autopilot L1 to L10): one planet
with its moons, the journey's end for the seeded dive — the
beyond-MVP tail continues in
[L11 — Regions of a planet](../../L11/documents/README.md).

## Scope and scale

L10 covers one planet with its moons: Earth with its interior,
surface, atmosphere, moons, and magnetosphere, in the
10^6–10^8 m range around the Earth-diameter anchor (1.28 x 10^7 m).
It sits between the [L09 — Stars](../../L09/documents/README.md)
above and the [L11 — Regions of a planet](../../L11/documents/README.md)
below, under the [Universe](../../documents/README.md#levels) ladder.
Frozen scale rules live in `docs/universes/ladder.md`; this folder
holds the descriptive reference only. The worked example is Earth
with its Moon; other planets and moons appear as comparative examples
in the part docs.

Read order: bridge ([previous-l9.md](./previous-l9.md)) first, then
the entry transition ([entry.md](./entry.md)), then any part below,
then the dated spine ([lifetime.md](./lifetime.md)) for dates behind
each claim.

## Figures at a glance

Canonical detail lives in the linked pages; this index carries only
the navigation-level numbers: L10 span 10^6–10^8 m; anchor Earth
diameter 1.28 x 10^7 m (12,756 km, 7,926 miles); Earth the biggest
rocky planet and fifth largest overall, oceans over 71 percent,
average depth about 3.6 km; air 78 percent nitrogen, 21 percent
oxygen; Moon 3,474 km across at 384,400 km (30 Earths, 1.3
light-seconds), orbit 27.3 days (29.5 days phase to phase), drifting
4 cm farther per year; spin 23.9 hours, year 365.25 days, tilt 23.4
degrees; Sun 150 million km behind (8.35 light-minutes); L9-to-L10
ratio 9.33e-3 with about 12 markers per L9 cell and two zoom gaps —
see the [key numbers](./previous-l9.md#key-numbers), the [entry
numbers](./entry.md#key-numbers), and the [dated
timeline](./lifetime.md#at-a-glance-dated-timeline).

## How this level differs from L09

[L09](../../L09/documents/README.md) frames one star close-up — a
burning disk with interior zones, spotted face, million-degree crown,
and wind with flares, plus planets as nearby points. Here one of
those points fills the view: the counted stellar order falls back
into surroundings (Sunlight as daylight, wind as weather) while a
single round world takes center with its layered engine, blue-marble
face, thin air blanket, cratered companion, and magnetic shield with
its auroras — so new planet, moon, air, and shield results enter
through the part docs, not this index.
