# Jets — radio galaxies and their lobes

This page is the L04 part doc for radio galaxies: FR I/II
classes, jets, hotspots, and megaparsec lobes — the
structures nuclei and ICM pages only ever tabled. Mentions
live in `nuclei.md:70` (one row), L03 `icm.md:83`, and
L01 `nodes.md:107`; M87's jet is described nowhere in
full; Centaurus A only names a group (L03 `groups.md:117`).

Black holes eat messily: a fraction of infalling matter is
flung out at near light speed along magnetic poles,
drilling megaparsecs of intergalactic medium and lighting
lobes bigger than clusters. Radio maps draw what eyes
cannot — twin beams, bright knots, terminal hotspots —
and the Fanaroff-Riley split sorts them by where they
shine brightest.

## FR I versus FR II

**FR I (edge-darkened)** — brightest near the nucleus,
fading outward: twin jets gradually widening, bending,
entraining gas; hosts giant ellipticals in clusters; M87
the prototype, Centaurus A the near one (see
`ellipticals.md`). Lower power, subsonic outer reaches,
pressure-balanced with surroundings.

**FR II (edge-brightened)** — faint collimated jets
ending in brilliant hotspots at lobe edges: Cygnus A the
archetype (z=0.056, jet power ~10^46 erg/s, hidden quasar
inside), hotspots demanding continuous electron injection
— light-travel versus cooling-time proved the beam model
in 1974. Higher power, relativistic to the end, often
one-sided by beaming. Blazars aim FR jets at us
(see `nuclei.md` unified table).

Giant radio galaxies stretch lobes to megaparsec scales —
7-Mpc record holders now — crossing into L03's ICM, where
cavities and bubbles record past outbursts (see L03 future
`radio.md`).

Sources: NED Cygnus A review (Carilli & Barthel);
Fanaroff & Riley 1974 via NED glossary; Kellermann & Owen
radio-galaxy review.

## The near gallery

**M87** — the FR I standard: jet imaged from event-horizon
(EHT rings in `nuclei.md`) to kiloparsec knots, HST-1
flaring, lobes stirring Virgo's ICM (see L03 `icm.md`,
`members.md`).

**Centaurus A** — the near standard: 12 million
light-years out, dust lane across an elliptical, inner
lobes, middle lobe, giant outer lobes degrees across —
jets restarting over episodes, warps recording mergers.

**Cygnus A** — the power standard: strongest extragalactic
radio source in the sky, double hotspots each end, X-ray
cavities around lobes — the beam model proven here.

## Feedback — jets heating clusters

Lobes displace ICM, carve cavities, and stir metals —
AGN feedback balancing cooling flows (see L03 `icm.md`):
without jets, cluster cores would cool catastrophically;
with them, bubbles rise and heat distributes. M87's lobes
in Virgo are the process caught working; Perseus's ripples
its echo. Galaxy-scale jets in this page, cluster-scale
bubbles in L03 `radio.md`.

## Key numbers

- FR I: center-bright, bending, cluster ellipticals; M87.
- FR II: hotspot-edged, collimated, distant quasars;
Cygnus A (10^46 erg/s).
- Lobes to Mpc scales; cavities heating clusters.
- L04 frame: 10^20–10^21 m — one radio galaxy per view
(lobes to L03).

Sources: NED and review pages named above.

## Example objects

M87 is the FR I standard: horizon to kiloparsecs —
the complete jet.

Centaurus A is the near standard: dust lane, restarting
lobes — episodes recorded.

Cygnus A is the power standard: double hotspots —
the beam proven.
