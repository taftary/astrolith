# L04 — Galaxies

L04 = L4 (ladder row L4, Galaxies, 10^20–10^21 m). This folder is the
L4 visual and lifetime reference (Issue #221): what its parts look
like, how they were created, how they changed through time, and how
they interact — each page with target-visual images and named sources.

## Scope and scale

L04 covers one cell of galaxy geography: spirals, ellipticals, dwarfs,
nuclei, and halos in the ~10–100-kiloparsec range, anchored on the
Milky Way (100,000 light-years, 9.46 x 10^20 m). It sits between the
[L03 — Galaxy clusters and groups](../../L03/documents/README.md)
above and [L05 — Galactic structures](../../L05/documents/README.md)
below, under the [Universe](../../documents/README.md#levels) ladder.
Frozen scale rules live in `docs/universes/ladder.md`; this folder holds
the descriptive reference only.

Read order: bridge ([previous-l3.md](./previous-l3.md)) first, then the
entry transition ([entry.md](./entry.md)), then any part below, then the
dated spine ([lifetime.md](./lifetime.md)) for dates behind each claim.

## Figures at a glance

Canonical detail lives in the linked pages; this index carries only the
navigation-level numbers: L4 span 10^20–10^21 m; anchor Milky Way
100,000 light-years (9.46 x 10^20 m, D25 ~87,000 light-years),
100–400 billion stars in a 0.5–1.4-trillion-solar-mass halo; spirals
5–100 kiloparsecs, 10^9–10^12 solar masses; Andromeda ~2.5 million
light-years out with a ~152,000-light-year disk falling toward us at
~110–123 km/s; M87* 6.5 billion solar masses, Sgr A* 4.297 million;
L3-to-L4 ratio 6.76e-3 with 32 markers per L3 cell (2.17 decades,
e 23.15 to e 20.98) — see
[key numbers](./previous-l3.md#key-numbers),
[entry numbers](./entry.md#key-numbers), and the
[dated timeline](./lifetime.md).

## How this level differs from L03

[L03](../../L03/documents/README.md) shows the city — bound swarms of
hundreds to thousands of galaxies in hot plasma inside a shared dark
halo. Here one member is the subject: the structured island inside,
with disks, bulges, nuclei, and halos resolved. The cluster becomes
surroundings — the swarm you came down stops being a crowd and starts
being everywhere, because you are inside one of its members. The
sorting rule arrives with the passengers: dense cores run elliptical,
outskirts run spiral (morphology–density), and dwarfs bent by tides
carry their group pre-processing with them — so new member-census
results enter through the part docs, not this index.
