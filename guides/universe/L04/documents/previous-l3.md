# L3 behind us — Galaxy clusters and groups as previous level

This page is the bridge from L3 (Galaxy
clusters and groups) into L4 (Galaxies): what the previous level looks
like, what carries over when you zoom in, and what changes at L4 scale.
Entry itself — the visual transition and its effects — lives in
[entry](./entry.md). Part detail lives in [spirals](./spirals.md),
[ellipticals](./ellipticals.md), [dwarfs](./dwarfs.md),
[nuclei](./nuclei.md) and [halos](./halos.md); the dated timeline in
[lifetime](./lifetime.md).

## What L3 is and how it looks

L3 is the city scale of the universe at ~10^23 m: hundreds to thousands
of galaxies bound together, swimming in superheated plasma at 10–100
million kelvin that holds most of the cluster's ordinary matter and
outweighs all the stars several times over, inside
a dark-matter halo holding ~80–90% of the total mass (convention
shared with L03: ~85–90% dark matter, ~5–15% gas, ~1–2% stars;
values vary per cluster). Its anchor landmark
is the Virgo Cluster: ~15 million light-years across, 1,300 to 2,000
member galaxies centered 54 million light-years away (53.8 ± 0.3 Mly,
16.5 Mpc) on the Virgo A subclump around the giant
elliptical M87 — the second-brightest member after M49, but the
dynamical center with its 6.5-billion-solar-mass black hole. The Local Group — our own poor group of 134+ known
members within 1 Mpc of the center, spanning ~16.7 million light-years
tip to tip — sits
nearby, with the Coma Cluster (Abell 1656, richness 2, Bautz–Morgan II:
1,000+ galaxies in a ~20-million-light-year sphere ~320 million
light-years out at 99 Mpc, dispersion ~1,000 km/s, binding mass
~7 x 10^14 solar masses, ICM at 8–9 keV, co-ruled by NGC 4874 and
NGC 4889)
as the rich archetype further off. Our region drifts: the Local Group's
recession from Virgo is slowed ~10% by Virgocentric flow while the
larger Local Sheet rides toward the Norma Cluster (Abell 3627, ~222 Mly,
~10^15 solar masses, hidden in the Zone of Avoidance at the Great
Attractor center) and on toward the Shapley Supercluster — the same
basin-and-current geography named at L2.

## What carries over into L4

Everything L3 shows at galaxy scale is still there, only closer. The
member galaxies L3 counts by the thousand — Virgo's 1,300 to 2,000,
Coma's thousands, our Local Group's 134+ known members within 1 Mpc of
the center, spanning ~16.7 million light-years tip to tip (5.11 Mpc
major axis) in a Milky Way–Andromeda dumbbell with lobes ~800 kpc
apart — become the L4 contents:
individual galaxies with shapes, disks, bulges, and nuclei of their
own. The L4 anchor is home: the Milky Way, ~100,000 light-years across
in the ladder anchor (9.46 x 10^20 m; D25 isophote ~87,000 light-years),
a barred spiral (SBbc) of 100–400 billion stars whose disk
we see edge-on as the glowing band across our night sky. The far
landmark is Andromeda (M31): the nearest major spiral, about 2.5
million light-years away (765 kpc), a barred spiral (SAB(s)b) of ~1
trillion stars whose bright disk spans ~152,000 light-years (D25
isophote, 46.6 kpc) with an extended faint halo and HI envelope
reaching several hundred thousand light-years — slightly larger than home, and
the galaxy we are falling toward at ~110–123 km/s for a possible merger
billions of years out (now only ~50% within 10 Gyr in Gaia+HST Monte
Carlo work, so the far-future fate stays open; see
[lifetime](./lifetime.md)). Hubble's
January 2025 mosaic stacked ~600 fields over 10 years to resolve 200
million of its stars. Between them sit the smaller companions: Triangulum (M33),
the third spiral of the Local Group, and the dwarf satellites
crowding around us — the Large and Small Magellanic Clouds and dozens
of fainter dwarfs. What L3 calls "cluster and group portals plus
field" resolves here into addressable objects. Sources: ladder R5
anchors; Wikipedia "Milky Way", "Andromeda Galaxy", "Local Group";
NASA PHAT Andromeda page; L03 `../../L03/documents/members.md` (brightest cluster
galaxies, M87 jet).

## What changes at this zoom

**From cities to buildings.** At L3 a galaxy is a point of light with a
type label — spiral, elliptical, brightest-cluster-giant. At L4 each
point opens into a structured island: spiral arms marbled with dark dust
and glowing star-forming clouds around a yellowish central bulge;
smooth featureless ellipticals; ragged irregulars and faint dwarfs;
and at many centers a supermassive black hole — M87* weighs 6.5
billion Suns ((6.5 ± 0.2 ± 0.7) x 10^9, EHT data April 2017, first image
10 April 2019, polarized ring 2021); our own Sagittarius A* at about 4.3
million (4.297 ± 0.012 million, GRAVITY; EHT image May 2022 from 2017
data). Detail on nuclei, jets, and duty cycles lives in
[nuclei](./nuclei.md).

**From one cell to thirty-two portals.** The L3 cell opens into L4
through 32 markers per cell (cluster and group portals plus field),
each showing its L4 cell at a true size ratio of 6.76e-3
(`../../../../docs/universes/ladder.md`, R6 table: 10^(20.98 − 23.15)).
Populations (field galaxies shown as points) never open; only portals do
(ADR 0010). The crossing spans 2.17 decades (e 23.15 down to e 20.98)
through two invisible milestones — the mechanics live in
[entry](./entry.md). Our path runs through
the home galaxy, the Milky Way, with Andromeda as the far spiral
landmark. Sources: ladder R6 amendment; ladder gap note; ADR 0010 (portal vs
population).

**From weather to architecture.** L3 shows what the cluster current does
to its passengers — headwind stripping, jellyfish tentacles, central
cannibals. L4 shows why each passenger looks the way it does: disks
built by gas settling and steady star birth (see
[spirals](./spirals.md)), ellipticals puffed up by mergers that scramble
orbits (see [ellipticals](./ellipticals.md)), dwarfs easily bent by tides
and pre-processed in groups before any cluster sees them (see
[dwarfs](./dwarfs.md)), nuclei lit by matter falling into the central
black hole (see [nuclei](./nuclei.md)), halos and satellite systems
holding the whole island together (see [halos](./halos.md)). The
stripping wind is still there, but now we see the victim's spiral arms
fraying — and the stripped stars themselves drifting on as intracluster
light between the islands. The sorting rule arrives with the passengers:
dense cores run elliptical, outskirts run spiral (morphology-density;
detail in L03 [members](../../L03/documents/members.md), group
pre-processing in L03 [groups](../../L03/documents/groups.md)).
Sources: NASA galaxy-type pages; L03
[ICM](../../L03/documents/icm.md) (stripping wind).

## Key numbers

- L3 span: ~10^23 m; anchor Virgo Cluster 15 Mly (1.42 x 10^23 m), 53.8 Mly away, 1,300–2,000 members.
- L4 span: 10^20–10^21 m; anchor Milky Way 100 kly (9.46 x 10^20 m; D25 ~87,000 ly).
- Milky Way: ~100,000 ly across, 100–400 billion stars, barred spiral (SBbc).
- Andromeda: ~2.5 Mly away (765 kpc), bright disk ~152,000 ly, extended halo and HI to several 100,000 ly; ~1 trillion stars.
- Andromeda approach: ~110–123 km/s; merger only ~50% within 10 Gyr (Gaia+HST 2025), outcome open.
- Local Group: 134+ known members within 1 Mpc of center, ~16.7 Mly tip to tip (5.11 Mpc); Triangulum third spiral; lobes ~800 kpc apart.
- Coma: 1,000+ members, ~320 Mly out (99 Mpc), ~7 x 10^14 solar masses, ICM 8–9 keV.
- M87*: 6.5 billion solar masses (EHT 2019); Sgr A*: 4.297 million (GRAVITY; EHT 2022).
- L3 to L4 ratio: 6.76e-3, 32 markers per L3 cell; span 2.17 decades (e 23.15 to e 20.98).

Sources: ladder R5/R6/R7; pages named above; EHT releases (2019 M87*, 2022 Sgr A*); Sawala et al. 2025 (merger odds).
