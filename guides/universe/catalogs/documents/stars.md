# Star kinds — every sun detailed

This catalog details star kinds: look, makeup, sizes,
history, ladder location, and examples. Tags: **observed**
(seen), **candidate** (likely, unconfirmed), **theoretical**
(predicted). Close-ups at L09 (future `other-stars.md`);
populations at L06 (`stars.md`, `dwarfs.md`,
`multiples.md`, `systems.md`); birthplaces at L05
(`clouds.md`, `open.md`).

## Protostars and T Tauri — stars under construction [observed]

**Look:** infrared cocoons, bipolar jets, flared disks edge-on as dark lanes. **Makeup:** collapsing
molecular hydrogen that has not yet fused — gravity
powered, not fusion powered. **Sizes:** final masses set
here, 0.08 to 150+ Suns. **History:** cloud knot to Class
0/I/II cocoon to T Tauri star with disk to main sequence
over millions of years; most disks photoevaporate or make
planets (see L08 future `disks.md`). **Location:** L05
clouds, L06 systems forming. **Examples:** Orion Nebula
proplyds, Taurus T Tauris.

Sources: L05 `clouds.md` and `lifetime.md`; L06
`systems.md`.

## Main sequence O–M — the hydrogen burners [observed]

The long adulthood: fusing hydrogen to helium in the core,
stable for millions to trillions of years by mass.

| Class | Color | Mass | Life | Share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| O | blue | 16+ | Myr | rare |
| B | blue-white | 2–16 | 10s Myr | rare |
| A | white | 1.4–2 | ~1 Gyr | ~1% |
| F | yellow-white | 1–1.4 | Gyr | ~3% |
| G | yellow | 0.8–1 | ~10 Gyr | ~6% |
| K | orange | 0.45–0.8 | 10s Gyr | ~13% |
| M | red | 0.08–0.45 | 100s Gyr–trillions | ~73% |

**Look:** O/B fierce blue with winds sculpting nebulae;
A white; F/G yellow-white; K orange; M dim red, often
flaring (see L09 future `other-stars.md`). **Makeup:**
hydrogen envelopes over fusing cores; M dwarfs fully
convective, mixing to the center. **History:** O stars die
young as supernovae; M dwarfs will outlive the universe's
current age many times. **Location:** everywhere, L04–L09.
**Examples:** Sirius A (A), Sun (G2V — the reference in
L08 `sun.md`), Proxima (M flare star with planets, L06
`systems.md`), Tau Ceti (G), Epsilon Eridani (K with
belts).

Sources: L06 `stars.md:14-52`; L09 `companions.md:131-136`.

## Brown dwarfs L/T/Y — failed stars [observed]

**Look:** dim magenta-infrared embers, weather bands,
lightning; Y dwarfs near room temperature. **Makeup:**
under 0.08 Suns — deuterium fusion briefly, never
hydrogen; atmospheres of methane, water, ammonia by
cooling class. **Sizes:** ~13–80 Jupiter masses straddling
planets and stars. **History:** born like stars in cloud
knots, cooling forever; free-floaters blur into rogue
planets (see `planets.md`). **Location:** L06 `dwarfs.md`.
**Examples:** Luhman 16 (binary brown dwarfs, L06),
WISE 0855 (near-room-temperature Y).

Sources: L06 `dwarfs.md:14-35`.

## Giants, AGB stars, supergiants — the swollen [observed]

**Look:** red-orange disks hundreds of times the Sun,
huge convection cells, slow pulsations, dusty winds.
**Makeup:** exhausted-core shells fusing hydrogen and
helium around carbon-oxygen cores; AGB stars dredge carbon
up. **Sizes:** 10–1,000+ Suns across; supergiants to
~1,500. **History:** every mid-mass star's fate — Sun
included (see L08 `sun.md`, L09 `lifetime.md`): subgiant
to red giant to AGB, shedding a planetary nebula and
leaving a white dwarf. **Location:** L06 future
`evolved.md`; L09 `other-stars.md`. **Examples:** Arcturus
and Aldebaran (giants), Betelgeuse and Antares
(supergiants).

## Massive stars — Wolf-Rayet and kin [observed]

**Look:** searing blue-white with broad emission lines,
winds stripping solar masses per millennium, nebulae
illuminated for parsecs. **Makeup:** exposed helium/carbon
cores after winds shed the hydrogen envelope. **History:**
millions of years only, ending as supernovae or direct
black holes; the main oxygen and heavy-element factories
(see `elements.md`). **Location:** L05 clouds and
clusters (Eta Carinae, Westerlund 1, R136 in L05
`clouds.md`, `open.md`). **Examples:** Eta Carinae
(LBV), R136a1 among the most massive known.

Sources: L05 `clouds.md:179`; L05 `open.md:174`.

## Variable stars — the pulsating [observed]

**Look:** rhythmic brightening and dimming over days to a
year. **Makeup:** instability strips where helium
ionization drives pulsation (Cepheids, RR Lyrae) or
evolved giants breathing (Miras). **History:** Cepheids
and RR Lyrae are standard candles — Leavitt's law for
Cepheids measures the universe (see L01–L02 survey
context); Miras shed the envelopes becoming planetary
nebulae. **Location:** L04 halos, L05 disk tracers, L06
future `evolved.md`. **Examples:** Delta Cephei
(prototype), RR Lyrae populations, Mira itself.

Sources: L04 `halos.md:243`; L05 `disk.md:28,52,57`.

## White dwarfs — the embers [observed]

**Look:** Earth-sized blue-white points, fading over
billions of years. **Makeup:** carbon-oxygen (or helium)
ash supported by electron degeneracy; polluted ones wear
shredded asteroids (see L06 `systems.md` for rings).
**Sizes:** Sun's mass in Earth's volume; limit 1.44 Suns
(Chandrasekhar — past it, collapse). **History:** endpoint
of 97% of stars including the Sun; cooling track runs Gyr.
**Location:** L06 `dwarfs.md`; L09 `lifetime.md:199-210`.
**Examples:** Sirius B (with Sirius A, L06), van Maanen 2.

Sources: L06 `dwarfs.md:88-110`.

## Neutron stars, pulsars, magnetars — the crushed [observed]

**Look:** city-sized, spinning lighthouse beams of radio
(and X/gamma for magnetars), glitching clocks rivaling
atomic time. **Makeup:** neutrons packed to nuclear
density, crusts of iron, fields to a quadrillion Gauss in
magnetars. **History:** born in core collapse (see L05
future `supernovae.md`); spun down over Myr; mergers make
gold and gravitational waves (2017's GW170817 — see
`elements.md`). **Location:** L06 future `remnants.md`;
millisecond pulsars in L05 `globulars.md`. **Examples:**
Crab pulsar, Geminga (L06 `ism.md:153`), magnetar
SGR 1806-20.

## Black holes — stellar and beyond [observed]

**Look:** nothing — accretion glow, jets, and lensed star
fields where mass bends light. **Makeup:** collapsed past
neutron support; stellar (3–100 Suns) from failed
supernovae, intermediate debated, supermassive millions to
billions (see L04 `nuclei.md`). **History:** endpoints of
the most massive stars; mergers chirp in gravitational
waves; Gaia BH1/BH3 are the nearest known stellar ones.
**Location:** L06 future `remnants.md`; L04 `nuclei.md`.
**Examples:** Cygnus X-1, Gaia BH3.

## Black dwarfs — the cold theoretical [theoretical]

**Look:** predicted: cold dark Earth-sized carbon spheres,
invisible. **Makeup:** white dwarfs cooled to background
temperature. **History:** takes quadrillions of years —
longer than the universe has existed, so none can exist
yet. Listed because the catalog must end somewhere, and
this is the end. **Location:** far-future L01/L06.
