# Filaments — the threads of the web

Filaments are the branching threads
of L2: dense ridges of galaxies and hot gas joining cluster nodes across
voids. In the NEXUS+ census they hold roughly half of all cosmic mass
(~46–50%, finder-dependent) in only ~6–7% of the volume, with walls
~24–28%, voids ~15–21% and nodes the remainder — which is why every
share in this page cites its method. Sibling docs: `walls.md`, `voids.md`,
`superclusters.md`; bridge in `previous-l1.md`, entry in `entry.md`,
timeline in `lifetime.md`. For close-up bridge views see the L01 companion
[Filaments](../../L01/documents/filaments.md) (3-Mly Lyman-alpha bridge,
Millennium threads); this page covers filaments at full L02 scale —
tens-to-hundreds of Mpc threads, giant complexes, and the 2020–2026
detections.

## What they are and how they look

In redshift maps filaments read as thin chains of galaxies with vast empty
gaps between; in simulations they are the bright fibres of the foam, knots
at the intersections, voids everywhere else. A filament in emission — the
model below, light spread over 50+ million light-years — shows the same
topology the surveys trace: threads, junctions, emptiness.

Two subtypes share the name. Rope-like filaments have roughly similar
cross-section axes along their length; flattened ones have a much larger
major than minor axis and grade into walls (see `walls.md`). The
distinction matters because finders classify borderline objects
differently — part of why mass shares are method-dependent.

At L02 scale a single thread is tens of Mpc long and only ~1–2 Mpc thick;
giant complexes reach hundreds of Mpc (see Example objects). Statistical
filamentarity in SDSS holds to ~80 Mpc/h in 2-D; longer visual impressions
are usually chance alignments of shorter segments (Bharadwaj et al.).
Individual DisPerSE segments in IllustrisTNG run ~1.5 to 15+ Mpc, chained
into complete node-to-node filaments.

## How they are found

No single finder owns the truth: NEXUS+ (scale-space Hessian, log-density
filtering that also catches tenuous threads), the Multiscale Morphology
Filter, DisPerSE (Morse-theory skeleton joining saddles to maxima), and
Bisous (cylinder-stacking on galaxy positions) disagree most on tenuous
objects, moving mass shares by factors of two — which is why every share
above cites its method. NEXUS+ at z = 0 gives filaments ~46% of mass in
~7% of volume (Cautun et al. 2014); the earlier MMF inventory gives ~39%
in ~9% (Aragon-Calvo et al. 2010). Shares evolve: sheets drain into
filaments, so filament share rises from ~30% at z = 2 to ~40–50% today.

Filament gas itself is extraordinarily thin: 1–10 particles per cubic meter
in the warm-hot phase, seen only in long integrations or stacks:

- Thermal Sunyaev-Zel'dovich stacking: 5.3-sigma signal (Delta-y
  ~1.3 x 10^-8 after halo subtraction) from ~260,000 SDSS luminous-red-
  galaxy pairs (Tanimura et al. 2019); 4.4-sigma from DisPerSE filaments
  at 0.2 < z < 0.6 with gas near 10^6 K (Tanimura et al. 2020); stacked
  X-ray emission at 4.2-sigma in 0.56–1.21 keV (Tanimura et al. 2020).
- CMB-lensing stacking independently weighs filament mass (first detection
  He et al. 2018, filament bias ~1.5).
- Kinematic SZ with ACT + DESI (2025) now traces gas motion in filaments
  and halos, complementing the thermal weighing.
- X-ray absorption (O VII / O VIII) toward bright AGN and stacked
  emission complete the picture; the single-filament benchmark is the 2025
  Shapley WHIM spectroscopy below.

Sources: Cautun et al. (NEXUS+); Aragon-Calvo et al. (MMF); Sousbie
(DisPerSE); Tanimura et al.; de Graaff et al.; He et al.; Wikipedia
"Warm-hot intergalactic medium".

## Example objects

- Sloan Great Wall filaments: the 433 Mpc wall complex (~1.4 billion
  light-years, ~1 billion light-years away at z ~ 0.078) is itself a bundle
  of filaments and embedded superclusters including SCl 126 — 1.8–2.7 times
  the CfA2 wall; later work suggests it may be a chance alignment of three
  structures. Sources: Wikipedia "Galaxy filament"; Wikipedia "Sloan
  Great Wall"; Gott et al. 2003.
- Quipu (reported 2025): ~400–428 Mpc / ~1.3–1.4 billion light-years long,
  ~2 x 10^17 solar masses (~200,000 Milky Ways) in 68 X-ray clusters —
  the largest known structure by mass and length, found in ROSAT X-ray
  clusters (redshift range ~0.027–0.065); named for the knotted Andean
  textile. The 428 Mpc figure is the discovery-paper value; summaries round
  to 400 Mpc. Sources: Wikipedia "Quipu (cosmic structure)";
  Boehringer et al., A&A 695:A59.
- BOSS Great Wall (redshift ~0.47, ~6.8 billion light-years light-travel):
  ~1 billion light-years across, 830+ visible galaxies, ~10,000 Milky-Way
  masses; two wall components (186 and 173 Mpc/h) plus 91 and 64 Mpc/h
  superclusters (Lietzen et al. 2016). Whether it moves together or is
  being pulled apart by expansion is still debated. Source: Wikipedia
  "BOSS Great Wall"; Einasto et al. 2017.
- Coma Filament: holds the Coma Supercluster and forms part of the CfA2
  Great Wall — the nearest giant thread, feeding the Coma Cluster node.
  Source: Wikipedia "Coma Filament".
- Pisces-Cetus (~1-billion-light-year complex, ~20 clusters) and
  Perseus-Pisces (~300-million-light-year wall anchored by Perseus A426):
  nearby giant threads that set the L02 scale. Hercules-Corona Borealis
  (~3 Gpc, z ~ 2, from gamma-ray-burst sky distribution) remains
  unconfirmed; if real it would dwarf Quipu.
- Typical size: commonly 50–80 Mpc (160–260 million light-years) long;
  masses from group scale up to 10^17 solar masses in the giants. Long
  straight NEXUS+ branches exceed 100 h^-1 Mpc. Source: Wikipedia
  "Galaxy filament"; Cautun et al. 2014.

## How they were created

Inflation stretched quantum flickers into over- and under-density ripples
(one part in 100,000, traced by the microwave background). After
recombination the pressure-free dark matter collapsed first into halos,
then ordinary matter fell in. Collapse runs fastest along one axis at a
time (Zel'dovich 1970): sheets first, then filaments, then knots — which is
why the web is thread-dominated. In deformation-tensor language a filament
is collapse on two axes with expansion on the third; tidal shear from the
surrounding megaparsec distribution stretches bridges between density peaks
(Bond, Kofman and Pogosyan 1996). The caustic-skeleton refinement traces
filaments as A3 cusp lines of the primordial deformation field, with
clusters at higher-order (A4/D4) singularities. Adhesion and truncated
Zel'dovich extensions handle stream-crossing and bottom-up buildup that
the first model misses. Growth of small-scale modes had waited out
the radiation era (Meszaros effect) until matter-radiation equality at
~47,000 years freed them. Sources: Wikipedia "Structure formation";
Wikipedia "Zeldovich approximation"; Wikipedia "Void (astronomy)";
Hidding, Shandarin and van de Weygaert (caustic skeleton).

## How they change over time

Small halos form first, then galaxies, groups, clusters and superclusters —
the Millennium-class simulations show matter condensing into filaments and
halos while voids drain to a tenth of mean density. Matter flows
voids-to-walls-to-filaments-to-nodes, so filament share rises from ~30%
at z = 2 to ~40–50% today while sheets shrink.

Filaments fatten as they feed: IllustrisTNG DisPerSE segments grow from
~0.3 Mpc wide at z = 2 to ~1.0–1.5 Mpc at z = 0 (median; full span
0.4–2.75 Mpc today), with width tracking linear halo-mass density nearly
linearly — a potential width estimator from observable galaxy light
(Yang et al. 2025). High-mass segments grow more common late. Caveat:
reported widths span 0.5–8.4 Mpc across studies; always cite finder,
tracer, and boundary definition.

Filament gas heats as it falls in: 30–50% of ordinary matter today is
100,000–10,000,000 K intergalactic plasma in filaments (the warm-hot
medium holding the "missing" baryons), against ~11% at redshift 2.
Direct imaging caught the feeding in the act: 2019 Lyman-alpha fluorescence
around forming clusters, 2021 MUSE diffuse glow over 2.5–4 comoving Mpc
outside nodes. The 2025 benchmark is a single pristine Shapley filament
between cluster pairs A3530/32 and A3528-N/S: 7.2 Mpc long in 3-D
(23 million light-years on the sky), (21 +/- 3)% excess X-ray emission at
6.1 sigma, gas at 0.8–1.1 keV (~10 million K) with electron density
~10^-5 cm^-3 and baryon overdensity 30–40 — the first X-ray spectroscopy
of pure WHIM in one thread, matching simulations where earlier stacked
averages had not (Migkas et al., A&A 2025; ESA "models were right"
release). Suzaku mapped the faint glow, XMM-Newton removed black-hole
contaminants.

In the far future, accelerating expansion (dominant for ~6 billion years)
pulls bound clusters apart and filaments dissolve — Laniakea given as the
example: a watershed flow basin, not a bound object, already dispersing
(Siegel; Tully et al. 2014). Sources: Wikipedia "Warm-hot
intergalactic medium"; Wikipedia "Large-scale structure of the universe";
Wikipedia "Galaxy filament"; Yang et al. 2025 (widths); Migkas et al. 2025.

## How it interacts with the others

Filaments are the middle managers of the web: matter flees void interiors
for walls, drains along filaments, and lands in nodes — voids up to 100 Mpc
across at under a tenth of mean density, walls near mean density, clusters
where walls meet and filaments cross. Void centers are gravitational hills
(effectively repulsive), cluster centers are wells. Massive clusters such
as Coma sit exactly at filament intersections and are fed by them.

**Feeding nodes.** Warm gas funnels along filaments into clusters; inflow
is anisotropic and aligns cluster major axes with their filaments. Abell
2744 (Pandora, z = 0.308) is an active node with hot-gas structures
coherent over ~8 Mpc in four filaments tied to the core. Cluster
connectivity grows with mass (~2 filaments at 10^14 solar masses, ~5 above
10^15); Euclid Q1 (63 deg^2, March 2025) now measures this relation
directly (Gouin et al.).

**Spinning galaxies up — and spinning themselves.** Low-mass blue galaxies
align their spins with filaments; high-mass red ones go perpendicular,
flipping near ~3 x 10^10 solar masses in stars (SAMI/GAMA kinematics;
MaNGA finds no overall signal — the effect is weak, ~10%, and
finder-dependent). Filaments themselves rotate: stacking thousands of SDSS
filaments reveals redshift/blueshift across the spine, clearest edge-on
and stronger with massive endpoint halos — angular momentum on the largest
known scale (Wang et al., Nature Astronomy 2021). A 2025 HI-selected
15-Mpc filament at z = 0.032 is more rotation-dominated than the SDSS
median, with effective radius ~0.8–1.0 Mpc matching SDSS and MillenniumTNG
stacks.

**Feeding star formation, then quenching it.** Filaments host more
star-forming gas than knots; cold streams feed galaxies from the
environment. Near Coma, galaxies redden toward filament spines out to
~1 Mpc; an 8-degree filament carries excess post-starburst galaxies, and
weak lensing traces intracluster filaments inside Coma aligned with the
~10-Mpc feeding threads. Late on, galaxies quench near nodes as gas supply
fades.

**Dominating gravity.** Filament-induced forces average ~60% of local
gravity and are the largest share in ~97% of volume in a 300 Mpc/h-box
inventory (voids ~20%, nodes ~17%, walls ~14%) — shares are method- and
box-specific, the ranking is the point; nodes dominate only their
immediate neighborhood (Cosmic Web Dynamics 2024).

**Mapped by new surveys.** DESI's Early Data Release has its first public
probabilistic web catalogue (ASTRA, 2026): every BGS/LRG/ELG/QSO object
across ~175 deg^2 classified as void, sheet, filament or knot, with
environment-dependent star formation matching GAMA/SDSS benchmarks.
Euclid Q1 adds filament-connectivity and galaxy-shape alignments at
0.5 < z < 0.9.

Sources: Wikipedia "Void (astronomy)"; Wikipedia "Galaxy filament";
Cosmic Web Dynamics 2024; Wang et al. 2021; Welker et al. (SAMI);
Zapata-Zuluaga et al. (ASTRA); Euclid Q1 (Gouin et al.; Laigle et al.).

## Key numbers

- Scale: 1 Mpc = 3.26 million light-years = 3.09 x 10^22 m.
- Typical filament: 50–80 Mpc = (1.5–2.5) x 10^24 m; statistically
  significant to ~80 Mpc/h (SDSS 2-D), long straight branches >100 h^-1 Mpc.
- Widths: 1–2 Mpc classic; ~1.0–1.5 Mpc median at z = 0, ~0.3 Mpc at z = 2
  (IllustrisTNG segments); linear density ~10^12 solar masses per Mpc.
- Mass share: ~46% in ~7% of volume (NEXUS+), ~39% in ~9% (MMF) —
  always cite the finder.
- Sloan: 433 Mpc, ~1.4 Gly, 1.3 x 10^25 m; Quipu: ~400–428 Mpc,
  ~2 x 10^17 suns; BOSS: ~1 Gly, ~10^4 Milky Ways, redshift ~0.47.
- Shapley WHIM filament: 7.2 Mpc, 0.8–1.1 keV, n_e ~10^-5 cm^-3,
  overdensity 30–40, 6.1-sigma excess.
- Warm-hot medium: 10^5–10^7 K, 1–10 particles per m^3, 40–50% of baryons
  today; connectivity ~2–5 filaments per cluster by mass.

Sources: pages named above; 1 Mpc conversions standard; Cautun et al. 2014;
Yang et al. 2025; Migkas et al. 2025.
