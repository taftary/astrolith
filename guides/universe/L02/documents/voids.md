# Giant voids — the empty cells of the web

Voids are the vast near-empty
expanses bounded by filaments, walls and clusters — the cells of the L2
foam, filling most of the volume with a small share of the mass. Sibling
docs: `filaments.md`, `walls.md`, `superclusters.md`; bridge in
`previous-l1.md`, entry in `entry.md`, timeline in `lifetime.md`.

## What they are and how they look

A void is a roundish region tens of megaparsecs across at a tenth or less of
the mean density in bright galaxies — not perfectly empty (matter stays above
roughly 15% of the mean, and a sparse population of void galaxies lives
inside), but empty enough to look like holes in every redshift map. There is
no single universal void definition: like dark-matter halos, each catalog
reflects its finder (watershed, spherical, or dynamical — see the
[finder landscape](../../L01/documents/voids.md#void-catalogs-finder-landscape)),
so every size and density below cites its source. Reviews quote 20–50 Mpc/h
near-empty roundish regions filling most of the volume (~75–80% once
overlapping watershed basins are counted). In lensing stacks they show a central
demagnification dip ringed by the piled-up matter of their walls. The map
below centers the Bootes void, the archetype: ~330 million light-years
across with only a handful of known galaxies inside.

## Example objects

- Local Void: the nearest, at least 150 million light-years across and
  apparently empty of bright galaxies; it stretches from the Local Group
  boundary out past ~60 Mpc, lying largely in the Zone of Avoidance.
  Cosmicflows-3 bounds it by the Perseus-Pisces and Norma-Pavo-Indus
  filaments ~8,500 km/s apart, finds passages onward to the Hercules and
  Sculptor voids, and traces a minor Virgo-Perseus filament threading the
  void with substantial galaxies in its depths. Its push on us is measurable:
  the Local Sheet recedes ~260 km/s from the void center, and the Local Group
  carries 200–250 km/s of deviant motion — void repulsion plus Virgo
  attraction accounting for about half of our CMB-frame motion. Distinct from
  the KBC hypothesis below. Sources: NASA Science ("Lonely
  Galaxy Lost in Space"); Tully et al. 2008; Tully et al. 2019
  (Cosmicflows-3); Karachentsev et al. 2025.
- Bootes (Great) Void: ~330 million light-years (~100 Mpc) across, one of
  the largest known — the archetypal giant void that made voids undeniable.
  Kirshner and colleagues found it in 1981 as a 6000 km/s
  redshift gap holding 1 of 133 survey galaxies (just ~60 galaxies where
  ~2,000 were expected); deeper work later counted
  53 galaxies inside its bounds, including a sparse central string
  consistent with coalescence of smaller voids. Sources: BBC Sky at Night Magazine;
  Kirshner et al. 1981; Aldering et al. 1997 (via the Bothun review).
- KBC Void / Local Hole: a proposed ~600 Mpc (~2 billion light-year)
  diameter underdensity around us, measured at a relative underdensity of
  ~0.46 between 40 and 300 Mpc — a hypothesis, not an established fact, and
  distinct from the observed Local Void above. The MOND-side case (Haslbauer
  et al., MNRAS 499:2845) argues mass conservation in such a void could raise
  the local H0 while finding the void itself in ~6-sigma tension with
  Lambda-CDM expectations from the MXXL simulation; standard-cosmology
  supernova tests instead see no bubble bias and read the data as consistent
  with homogeneity. Treat as disputed. Sources: Haslbauer et al. 2020 (MNRAS
  499:2845); UniverseMap KBC page.
- Eridanus supervoid: roughly billion-light-year scale (1.8 billion
  light-years wide, ~30% below mean density, center ~2 billion light-years
  away), elongated along the
  line of sight below redshift 0.3, aligned with the microwave Cold Spot —
  the leading candidate for its Integrated Sachs-Wolfe imprint. DES Year-3
  confirms it as a z < 0.2 redMaGiC underdensity and, at S/N >= 5, the most
  prominent large-scale dip in the DES lensing mass maps — the largest such
  dark-matter map, over a quarter of the Southern Hemisphere — yet its ISW
  imprint still accounts for only ~10–20% of the temperature depression in
  Lambda-CDM, and its lensing amplitude runs ~30% below simulation
  expectations. The wider puzzle stands: R >= 100 Mpc/h supervoids elsewhere
  show ISW amplitudes ~5x Lambda-CDM expectations while null surveys oppose
  any causal link — unresolved. Sources:
  Szapudi et al. (arXiv:1511.09008); Kovacs et al. 2022; Fermilab news 2022.
- Typical sizes: catalogued voids run 5–120 Mpc/h; reviews quote 20–50 Mpc/h
  near-empty roundish regions. Stacked analyses peak near ellipticity ~0.15
  (smaller voids more elliptical — expansion sphericalizes as they grow), and
  the Hamaus et al. two-parameter density profile (central density plus scale
  radius, PRL 112, 2014) fits watershed voids across size, redshift and
  tracer, with linear theory turning the density fit straight into the
  velocity profile. Example void galaxy: NGC 6503, an 18-million-
  light-year-distant dwarf spiral (~30,000 light-years across) at the Local
  Void edge. Void galaxies run small, gas-rich, blue and star-forming, with
  calmer merger histories — see the canonical
  [L01 discussion](../../L01/documents/voids.md#how-voids-interact-with-the-rest-of-the-web).
  Sources: Sutter et al. void catalogue (via Melchior et al.,
  arXiv:1309.2045); NASA Science (NGC 6503); Hamaus et al. 2014;
  Contarini et al. 2026.

## How they were created

Voids grow from primordial density troughs in the fluctuation field. An
underdense region has weaker gravity — an effectively repulsive peculiar
pull — so it expands faster than the Hubble flow and evacuates; the evicted
matter piles at the edges into sheets, filaments and cluster boundaries.
Inner shells outrun outer ones until shell-crossing, when a dense ridge
stands. Aspherical voids round themselves off as they expand (Icke's 1984
bubble theorem) but never reach perfect spheres, because tides and
neighbors intervene. Small voids merge hierarchically: inter-void matter is
squeezed into thin walls that fade as matter drains to the enclosing
boundary. Sources: van de Weygaert and Platen review; spherical void model
(arXiv:1605.05286); Sheth and van de Weygaert 2004.

## How they change over time

Voids grow, merge and stratify: small basins expand and merge into parents
once walls and filaments coalesce (around scale factor 0.2–0.4, when the web
crystallizes enough for basins to be identified and dark energy begins to
shut off new structure formation), then large
voids simply keep growing. True mergers that dissolve identity are rare:
most voids follow a single line of descent and keep their identity as
annexed subvoids of a larger parent, with the old inter-void walls fading to
a faint imprint. Merger growth outweighs steady expansion roughly
tenfold when it happens, but almost all voids follow only a single line of
descent with infrequent minor mergers. Small
voids inside overdense surroundings can be squeezed ("void-in-cloud"),
while large ones keep expanding like miniature open universes
("void-in-void"). Simulations find squeezed small voids survive as
identifiable voids rather than vanishing, so late-time destruction is
negligible except for the smallest — voids live far quieter lives than
halos, which keeps their primordial and dark-energy imprint clean. SDSS work splits the population
further: R-type voids keep expanding with clean outflow profiles, S-type
voids mix expansion with infall from dense surroundings. Volume share is
definition-dependent (~80% once
overlapping watershed basins are counted) against a small mass share
(single digits to tens of percent). Coherent outflows run in simulations
and observations alike. Sources: Sutter et al., "Life and death of cosmic
voids" (arXiv:1403.7525); Sheth and van de Weygaert 2004 (via Ceccarelli et
al., arXiv:1501.02120); Valles-Perez et al. 2021.

## How they interact with the others

Voids push matter out into walls and filaments: evacuation runs along the
boundaries with tangential flow inside fading dividers, feeding the
filament highways and, through them, the cluster nodes. Local proof is the
260 km/s recession of our Sheet from the Local Void center — motion read as
Virgo pull plus Local Void push. Treat any single gravity-share percentage
as model-dependent (no fixed verified number; the pattern of void push plus
node pull is what is robust). Voids also serve as dark-energy probes:
stacked void shapes enable the Alcock-Paczynski test (joint RSD plus AP
modeling recovers growth rate and geometric distortion together, cutting AP
systematics from ~15% to ~5%), and void lensing plus
abundances constrain growth and modified gravity. Lensing reads void mass
directly through outward-bending anti-lensing — de-magnification plus radial
shear, free of galaxy bias — with stacked BOSS detections at signal-to-noise
5–13. Screening mechanisms stay ineffective inside voids, amplifying
modified-gravity signals; Euclid forecasts have voids alone reaching ~0.3%
on D_M/D_H and ~6% on the dark-energy equation of state. Sources: Tully et al.
2008; Hawaii Extragalactic Distance Database; Lavaux and Wandelt 2012; Bos
et al. 2012; Raghunathan et al. 2020; Contarini et al. 2026; Radinovic et
al. 2023.

## Void catalogs at L02 scale

Catalog numbers are finder outputs, not sky facts — each finder (watershed,
spherical, dynamical) draws different boundaries around the same
underdensities. The reference chain behind the objects above:

- SDSS DR7 (Pan et al., VoidFinder): 1,054 voids, largest just over
  30 Mpc/h effective radius, median ~17 Mpc/h — the baseline L02-scale
  census the Bootes and Local Void numbers sit inside.
- VAST (Douglass et al.): multi-algorithm SDSS catalogs (VoidFinder plus
  VIDE watershed) that make finder dependence explicit by publishing
  parallel samples.
- CAVITY: watershed-selected voids with dedicated integral-field follow-up
  of their galaxies — the environmental-counterpart sample for void-galaxy
  claims.
- DESIVAST (Rincon et al., ApJ 982, 2025): DESI DR1 Bright Galaxy Survey
  volume-limited to z = 0.24 — 1,461 interior VoidFinder voids, 420 with
  V^2/REVOLVER pruning, 295 with V^2/VIDE pruning. Properties agree broadly
  with overlapping SDSS catalogs, but volume overlap differs significantly
  (galaxy selection plus survey masks) — a caution for cross-survey
  comparisons.
- Coming: DESI full-survey (50M+ redshifts, completed April 2026) and
  Euclid (first data March 2025, 26M galaxies) will multiply void counts.

Rule of thumb: always cite finder, tracer, minimum radius (~10 Mpc floor),
and pruning — counts without them do not compare. Small-void samples need a
purity cut below ~2.5x tracer mean separation, where catalogs fill with
spurious Poisson voids. For the canonical finder discussion, see
[Void catalogs](../../L01/documents/voids.md#void-catalogs-finder-landscape).

## Recent results (2020–2026)

- DES Year-3 lensing (Kovacs et al. 2022, MNRAS 510): Eridanus confirmed as
  the most prominent large-scale dip (S/N >= 5) in the DES mass maps, but its
  lensing amplitude runs ~30% below Lambda-CDM simulation expectations —
  an open tension alongside the ISW excess (A_ISW ~ 5.2 +/- 1.6 for
  R >= 100 Mpc/h supervoids elsewhere against the expected 1).
- BOSS void lensing (Raghunathan et al. 2020): stacked detections at
  signal-to-noise 5–13, establishing the central demagnification dip as
  routine rather than marginal.
- DESIVAST (Rincon et al., ApJ 982, 2025): first DESI-era void census to
  z = 0.24; cross-survey volume overlap with SDSS differs significantly —
  finder and selection systematics now dominate over raw counts.
- Euclid forecast (Radinovic et al., A&A 677, 2023, Flagship mocks with
  pre-void-finding reconstruction): voids alone reach ~0.3% on D_M/D_H,
  5–8% on f sigma_8 per bin over 0.9 <= z < 1.8, Delta Omega_m = +/-0.0028,
  ~6% on the dark-energy equation of state — beating either Euclid
  clustering or lensing alone on Omega_m.
- Survey outlook: DESI five-year survey completed April 2026 (50M+
  redshifts); Euclid first data March 2025 (26M galaxies) — void counts are
  about to multiply, opening the void auto-correlation (exclusion dip plus
  peak, so far modeled but rarely measured) to cosmology.
- Synthesis: Contarini et al. 2026 precision-voids review (A&ARv 34:1)
  consolidates finder classes, purity cuts, universal profiles, and probe
  forecasts used throughout this page.

## Key numbers

- Typical void diameter: 20–100+ Mpc; catalogued 5–120 Mpc/h.
- Local Void: 150+ million light-years across, 60+ Mpc deep; 260 km/s local outflow.
- Bootes: ~330 million light-years, dozens of known galaxies.
- KBC: ~600 Mpc across, underdensity ~0.46 (proposed, disputed).
- Eridanus: ~1.8 billion light-years wide, ~30% below mean density, below redshift 0.3.
- Catalogs: SDSS DR7 1,054 voids; DESIVAST 1,461 / 420 / 295 by finder.
- Volume ~80% voids; mass single digits to tens of percent.
- Lensing dip ~-0.001, stacked detections at signal-to-noise 5–13.
- Euclid forecast (voids alone): ~0.3% on D_M/D_H, ~6% on dark-energy equation of state.
