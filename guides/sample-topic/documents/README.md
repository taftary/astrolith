# Sample Topic: Horizontal Garden Sundial

## Scope

This topic explains how a small horizontal garden sundial works and how to read
it. It is a self-contained sample unrelated to the main project, created as a
smoke run of the `guides` workflow.

Out of scope: other sundial types (equatorial, vertical, analemmatic), precise
dial layout mathematics, and clock-time corrections (equation of time,
longitude, daylight saving).

## Overview

A horizontal garden sundial tells solar time with sunlight and shadow. A flat
plate (the dial) lies level on the ground or a plinth, and an inclined blade
(the gnomon) casts a shadow across hour lines marked on the plate. The shadow
of the gnomon's time-telling edge (the style) points to the current solar
hour. Because the style must stay parallel to the Earth's rotation axis, the
gnomon's angle and the spacing of the hour lines both depend on the dial's
geographic latitude.
